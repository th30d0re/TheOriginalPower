And let's go deeper on the pre-diabetes analogy, because it actually proves the framework's point if you look at the full pipeline.

Chronic racialized stress does not stay in your head. It becomes **embodied system state**: allostatic load, inflammation, telomere shortening, and accelerated epigenetic aging (Gerominus 2006, Ruiz-Narvaez 2024). The stress of living inside engineered enclosure — redlined neighborhoods, over-policing, employment discrimination, generational wealth stripping — literally degrades your biology at the cellular level. So before the person ever walks into a fast-food restaurant, their body is already operating under a **degraded health baseline** produced by the system's operating conditions.

Then comes the **Affordability Trap**. The food that sustains you is priced out of reach. The food that degrades you is subsidized, mass-produced, and shelf-stable. A 2026 Harvard Law School audit proved that price strictly dictates a population's exposure to toxicity — and the cheap calories available in food deserts are engineered for shelf life and profit margin, not human health. This is not a free market outcome. It is a meticulously engineered pipeline: cheap degraded food goes in, metabolic disease comes out, and the medical-industrial complex monetizes the treatment.

So the "choice" to eat fast food is occurring inside a stacked deck: your stress biology is already degraded, your grocery stores were destroyed by eminent domain or redlining, the healthy food is priced beyond your budget, and the cheap food is chemically optimized for addiction and shelf stability. Then, when diabetes manifests, it is coded as a **personal moral failure** rather than the predictable output of a food-to-medical profit pipeline.

Pre-diabetes does not have a five-century extraction architecture. It does not deliberately degrade your stress biology, destroy your grocery infrastructure, price the antidote out of reach, and then blame you for the symptom. The system does. That is not a "mitigating factor." That is the primary causal chain.
