# Per-Axis Frequency Reconciliation
The published values and TT-OOS-02 are numerically reproducible, but they estimate peaks from different inputs and cannot validate one another. The source values are SCOTUS majority-opinion keyword-rate Lomb–Scargle peaks: race `3.59 yr` and gender `6.18 yr`. The appendix rounds these to `3.6 yr` and `6.0 yr`; `6.0` is less faithful than the chapter's `6.2`. TT-OOS-02 obtains gender `10.0 yr` in both epochs and a race peak at the first available 30-year FFT bin (`0.0667 cyc/yr`, `15 yr`) from model-generated Congressional Record shares. These are correct outputs under their respective assumptions. Neither analysis establishes a physical “natural frequency”; both locate a dominant spectral peak in an operationally chosen series and band.
## Lead hypothesis: confirmed
`eq_fourier_per_axis.py` explicitly calls the decomposition a historical-event mixture model and disclaims ground-truth recovery (`Paper/scripts/eq_fourier_per_axis.py:8-33`). It creates logistic baselines (`:86-106`), adds Gaussian impulses whose centers, amplitudes, and widths are chosen from historical events (`:108-132`), turns the three modeled curves into mixture weights (`:134-148`), and multiplies those weights by the observed aggregate identity frequency (`:150-153`). It then writes the per-axis CSV (`:160-178`). Frequency content is therefore fixed by the observed aggregate together with those chosen trends and event parameters; the per-axis series is synthesized, not independently measured. The script imports the SCOTUS periods as “validation anchors” (`:35-43`) and places them only in plot-title text as predicted values (`:295-318`); it contains no per-axis peak-extraction step.
## Provenance and reproduction
The numbers originate in `Paper/scripts/scotus_corpus_analysis.ipynb`: the input is a 57-case, 145-year, irregular SCOTUS corpus (`:124-146`); majority rows are averaged per case, mean-centered, evaluated with normalized SciPy Lomb–Scargle on a 500-point `3–50 yr` grid, and selected by global argmax (`:327-369`). Its saved output is race `3.59`, gender `6.18`, religion `8.52`, sexuality `50.0` (`Paper/data/scotus_spectral_results.json:15-37`); the notebook output reports the same values (`Paper/scripts/scotus_corpus_analysis.ipynb:307-323,700-704`). A read-only rerun produced grid-exact race `3.588035 yr` (`0.278704 cyc/yr`, power `0.121989`) and gender `6.176252 yr` (`0.161910 cyc/yr`, power `0.167272`). A dense grid gave `3.5910` and `6.1668`; linear detrending gave `3.6088` and `6.1652`. The chapter identifies the SCOTUS method and publishes `3.6/6.2` (`Paper/chapters_src/15_tweedism_and_the_puppet_class_the_algori.tex:1267-1292`). `eq40_45_interference_engine.ipynb` reads the JSON rather than recomputing it (`Paper/scripts/eq40_45_interference_engine.ipynb:486-499,588-605`), and `eq_scotus_electromagnetic_conversion.py` likewise reads those periods (`Paper/scripts/eq_scotus_electromagnetic_conversion.py:16-39`). The aggregate electoral script analyzes only class and aggregate identity (`Paper/scripts/eq_fourier_electoral_cycle.py:60-90`); its Welch estimates are robustness checks (`:93-105`). The robustness script uses per-axis model output only for OLS trends (`Paper/scripts/eq_fourier_electoral_cycle_robustness.py:35-80`). `spectral_fourier.ipynb` uses detrended, Hann-windowed FFTs on aggregate CR/GT/ANES signals (`Paper/scripts/spectral_fourier.ipynb:130-157,174-220,260-281`). None produces `3.6/6.2` from Congressional Record per-axis observations.
## Divergence and correct values
TT-OOS-02 uses 30-year halves of synthesized **shares**, linear detrending, mean removal, a Hann taper, an unpadded FFT periodogram, and argmax over `0.05–0.50 cyc/yr` (`Paper/scripts/tt_oos_02_gender_branch.py:14-28,44-65`). Running it unchanged reproduced gender `0.1000 cyc/yr = 10.0 yr`, `delta=+2.1000` in both halves, and race `0.0667 cyc/yr = 15.0 yr` at the first available in-band bin; its report records the gender spectra and race edge condition (`Paper/prereg/tt-oos-02-RESULTS.md:16-39`). The divergence is driven first by series/provenance (curated SCOTUS keyword rates versus modeled CR mixture shares), then by span/sampling (57 irregular cases across 145 years versus two annual 30-year epochs), estimator (Lomb–Scargle versus Hann FFT), and search band/grid. The defensible repository values are therefore: for the published SCOTUS operationalization, race `3.59 yr` and gender `6.18 yr` (reportable as `3.6` and `6.2`), under its small, selected-corpus and `3–50 yr` argmax assumptions; for the synthetic CR diagnostic, gender `10.0 yr` and race unresolved at/longer than the available `15 yr` edge. The CR values must be labeled model-output diagnostics, not empirical per-axis measurements.
## Figure assessment and findings
`fig:extraction_chart` is not correct as a measured per-axis chart. It inherits exploratory SCOTUS peaks, labels its markers “measured,” rounds gender from `6.18/6.2` to `6.0`, and assigns sexuality a marker despite no resolved sexuality period (`Paper/apx_extraction_chart.tex:326-356,429-461`). Using the exact source-grid periods with `delta=T/4-4/T` gives race `delta=-0.2178` and gender `delta=+0.8964`; using the manuscript's one-decimal `3.6/6.2` gives `-0.2111/+0.9048`. The current `-0.211/+0.833` is internally consistent only with `3.6/6.0`. If the chart instead adopted TT-OOS-02, gender would be `+2.100`, race would have no point estimate (the `15 yr` edge gives `+3.483` only as an edge-bin value), and sexuality would remain unresolved. Judgment call: the SCOTUS values “stand” only as exploratory dominant periods; the evidence does not support calling either set intrinsic natural periods. One brief item was wrong: `TT-OOS-02-RESULTS.md:61` prints race `≤15 yr`; the edge logic and the task brief support `≥15 yr`/longer-period unresolved. No repository candidate produced `6.0` exactly; it is rounding of the SCOTUS `6.18/6.2` result.
