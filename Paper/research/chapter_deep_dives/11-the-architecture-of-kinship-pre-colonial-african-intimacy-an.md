### 3. The Indigenous Baseline: Distributed Intimacy Architectures

#### 3.1 The Igbo Configuration (Flexibility & Genealogy)
The Igbo utilized **bridewealth** as a social technology to create mutual accountability. It functioned as a financial liability for husbands; mistreatment of a wife triggered the return of the wealth by the bride's lineage.
*   **Gender Decoupling:** Gender roles were operationally decoupled from biological sex to ensure lineage preservation.
*   **The Male Daughter (*okpara nwanyi*):** A daughter socially and legally reclassified as a son to inherit property and perform ancestral rituals, addressed in the masculine social register.
*   **Woman-to-Woman Marriage (*igba ohu/inyomdi*):** A wealthy woman could pay bridewealth for a younger woman, becoming her legal "husband." All children born to the younger woman belonged to the female husband’s lineage, solving genealogical problems through social engineering rather than biological sex.

#### 3.2 The Yoruba Configuration (Economic Autonomy & Dual-Sex Governance)
Yoruba architecture was defined by the structural economic independence of women, who dominated regional trading networks and retained wealth independently of husbands.
*   **The *Iyalode*:** A formal chieftaincy office (the "mother of the outside world") with sovereign authority to regulate markets and challenge the male *Oba* (king). 
*   **The *àlè* Institution:** A socially acknowledged intimate relationship distinct from the procreative marital contract. This distinguished lineage alliance from human intimacy, a distinction the colonial "concubinage" label sought to flatten.

#### 3.3 The Akan Configuration (Matrilineality & The *Abusua*)
Akan society was organized around the ***abusua*** (maternal clan), where blood and civic belonging were transmitted exclusively through the mother.
*   **The *Wofa* Node:** The primary male authority was the maternal uncle (*wofa*), not the husband. Because children belonged to the mother’s clan, the European "father-centric" model of authority could not compile.
*   **Coverture Impossibility:** As lineage and property were transmitted matrilineally, European coverture—where a husband subsumes a wife's legal identity—was structurally impossible.
*   **The *Ohemaa* (Queen Mother):** A sovereign co-ruler and royal genealogist with the exclusive authority to nominate male successors.

### 5. Phase I: The Demographic Cataclysm (1500–1900 CE)
The transatlantic slave trade functioned as a systemic shock that warped the kinship equilibrium before formal colonization.

*   **Male-Biased Extraction:** European demand for plantation labor prioritized adult males, leading to sex ratios approaching 2:1 (women to men). This created a demographic void that forced the **universalization of polygyny** to prevent societal collapse.
*   **The East African Control Case:** The Indian Ocean trade was female-biased (domestic service), resulting in lower polygyny rates in East Africa. This "natural control" confirms that West African polygyny was a structural adaptation to the extraction of men.
*   **The Arms-for-Slaves Feedback Loop:** An "externally imposed prisoner’s dilemma." European traders sold muskets only for captives. To survive the firearms asymmetry, African kingdoms were forced to capture neighbors, becoming the "fuel" for a system they did not author.
*   **The Seasoning Ritual:** The Middle Passage functioned as the "gastronomic face of extraction," where "meat-in-process" was handled using butchery vocabulary. This formed the aesthetic appetite of the colonial Buffer Class, aligning with the **psychological wage ($\psi_s$) architecture**.
