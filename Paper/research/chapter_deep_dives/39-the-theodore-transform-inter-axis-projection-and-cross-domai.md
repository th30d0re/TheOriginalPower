### 4. The Invariance Condition and Theorem I.1

**Principle vs. Local Rule**  
A claim ($c$) functions as a **principle** only if it maintains validity across its asserted scope ($S(c)$), as verified by Equation I.2:
$$c \text{ holds on } D_\alpha \implies ተ_{\alpha\to\beta}(c) \text{ holds on } D_\beta \forall \beta \in S(c)$$

**Theorem I.1 (Selective-Application Detection)**  
If a claim holds in domain $\alpha$, but its transform $ተ_{\alpha\to\beta}(c)$ fails in a domain $\beta$ within the asserted scope, and the claimant accepts this failure, the commitment is a **local rule**, not a principle.
*   **The "Gap" Argument:** A principle is the pair $(c, S(c))$. If the claimant honors only $(c, \{\alpha\})$, the "honored scope" is a proper subset of the "asserted scope." This discrepancy or "gap" formally identifies selective application (bias).

**The Three Unavailable Conclusions**  
The transform is an audit of consistency, not a tool for establishing:
1.  **Truth:** A claim can be invariant but factually or morally false.
2.  **Direction of Repair:** It does not specify whether to expand the claim to the new domain or retract it from the original.
3.  **Quantitative Measurement:** It provides a logical scope audit, not a calibrated numerical value.
