## 3. The Concession Theorem: Formalism and Falsification

### The Formal Claim
The Concession Theorem states that for every non-kinetic reform $R_i$ within the 1450–2026 dataset, $\Delta max(R_i) = 0$. This asserts that no reform achieved through purely legislative or litigation-based means has permanently reduced $E$’s extraction share as measured at the system apex.

### Three-Point Falsification Criteria
To falsify the Concession Theorem, a historical case must **simultaneously** satisfy the following three conditions:

1.  **Structural Reduction:** The reform must cause a measurable, absolute decline in $E$’s extraction share (top-decile wealth share) sustained over $\geq 30$ years. A mere deceleration of inequality growth is insufficient.
2.  **No Compensatory Extraction:** The reduction must not be offset by "leakage," such as capital flight to offshore jurisdictions, expansion of the extraction base to new populations, or the substitution of financial extraction for labor-based models. Aggregate extraction at the apex must decline across all channels.
3.  **Threat Cycle Durability:** The reduction must persist through periods of low kinetic threat. If the reform's benefits dissipate once social instability recedes, the theorem is confirmed rather than falsified.

### The Kinetic-Precondition Prediction
The theorem predicts that concessions are defensive adaptations offered when class resistance ($min$) approaches the kinetic threshold ($\tau$). The "Great Compression" (1932–1945) serves as the primary exemplar: reforms rose with kinetic convergence and began a long-run decay after **1973** as the threat environment dissipated. In these cases, $\Delta max$ eventually returned to zero, representing a return to the system's invariant set.

## 4. The Haitian Theorem: Formalism and Falsification

### The Formal Claim
The "Strong-Form" Haitian Theorem is defined by two operational equations:
*   **Eq. 19.10:** For all non-kinetic interventions $R_i$, $\Delta max(R_i) = 0$.
*   **Eq. 19.11:** There exists at least one kinetic action $K_j$ such that $max(t_{post}) = 0$ locally, signifying kernel termination.

### Five-Point Falsification Criteria
Falsification of the strong-form theorem requires a documented case **simultaneously** satisfying:

1.  **Intra-national Racial Partition:** The society must possess an operative $\psi$ mechanism.
2.  **Absence of Kinetic Precondition:** Liberation must occur without background kinetic threat (no armed wings, insurrection, or external military pressure). If kinetic potential exists but is not discharged, the case is categorized as the **"Weak Form"** (threat without discharge), which does not falsify the Strong Form.
3.  **Kernel-Level Liberation:** The outcome must terminate the material extraction kernel. "Interface updates" (political desegregation) are insufficient if the economic commanding heights remain in $E$'s possession.
4.  **Durable Outcome:** Kernel dismantlement must persist through at least one full threat cycle without requiring renewed kinetic pressure.
5.  **No Elite Class Substitution:** The case must avoid **"Kernel Transplant"** (the Zimbabwe pattern), where a replacement Elite assumes control of the existing extraction topology.

## 5. Empirical Record and Historical Evidence

Major 20th-century reforms—including the New Deal, Civil Rights Act, OSHA, and the Voting Rights Act—fail the falsification test. While they produced temporary structural reductions, the long-run data shows $\Delta max$ returning to zero (baseline extraction) within 20–30 years. ### The Haitian Record

These cases either lacked an operative racial partition at the time of reform, relied on kinetic potential (Weak Form), or resulted in a Kernel Transplant. Consequently, the Strong-Form Haitian Theorem remains unfalsified within the 1450–2026 dataset.
