The electrodynamic isomorphism establishes a formal mapping between the dynamics of social oppression and the principles of circuit theory, thermodynamics, and control systems. This framework posits that power structures are not merely the result of historically contingent social accidents but are governed by the physical constraints inherent to complex engineering systems.

**Problem Statement:** Determining whether the electrodynamic isomorphism serves as a descriptive analogy or represents a structural constraint. This investigation seeks to identify if there are a finite number of viable topologies for the organization of power and control, and whether social systems can effectively escape the mathematical attractors of hierarchical extraction.

### 2. Conjecture G.1: Universality of the Extraction Kernel
In statistical mechanics, a universality class comprises disparate physical systems—such as the Ising model, lattice gas, or binary fluids—that share identical critical exponents and scaling behaviors regardless of microscopic variance.

**Formal Definition:** Conjecture G.1 posits that the **Predatory Min-Max Function** belongs to a universality class of **hierarchical far-from-equilibrium extractors**. These systems are designed to concentrate resources (energy, matter, or information) against a gradient while maintaining internal stability against the entropy generated by the extraction process.

**The Three Essential Conditions for Convergence:**
For any system to converge toward the extraction kernel, three mathematical conditions must be satisfied:
1.  **Bounded Resources:** The system operates under constraints that preclude a uniform distribution to all nodes.
2.  **Positive Feedback:** A discrete subset of nodes possesses the capacity to modify distribution rules to amplify their own resource share.
3.  **Dissipation Threat:** Excluded nodes maintain "coherence capacity"—the mathematical potential to coordinate and mobilize, thereby threatening system stability.

**Assertion and Falsification:**
*   **Assertion:** Any system meeting the three conditions will inevitably converge to the five-node topology and utilize a phase-loading control law.
*   **Falsification:** **Conjecture G.1 would be falsified if a system satisfying these three conditions were found to converge on a stable, non-hierarchical topology (e.g., pure anarchy or stochastic leadership).**

### 6. Technical Vocabulary and Conceptual Framework
*   **Far-from-equilibrium extractors:** Systems that maintain high organization and resource concentration by actively working against natural gradients of distribution.
*   **Internal dissipation:** The thermodynamic loss of efficiency or increase in entropy within a system, manifesting as social friction or uncoordinated resistance.
*   **Coherence capacity:** The mathematical potential for a population to coordinate actions effectively to influence or disrupt the system state.
*   **Actuator:** The $Fenforce$ node; the physical interface that executes the control law to modify the state vector $q$.
*   **Kinetic intervention:** The application of physical force or resistance to alter the system's state trajectory.
*   **Stochastic resource allocation:** A non-hierarchical distribution method based on random probability.

**Extraction as an Algorithm:** This framework treats oppression as the algorithmic result of a system attempting to solve an optimization problem. Extraction is a mathematical convergence of "circuit topology" rather than a purely cultural phenomenon.
