# Contemporary Consumer Product Toxicity Data

## Toothpaste (2024-2025)

### Lead Safe Mama / Independent Lab Testing (2025)
- 51 common toothpaste brands tested: **90% contained detectable lead, arsenic, mercury, or cadmium**
- Lead Safe Mama has tested 67+ toothpaste/tooth powder products
- Only **8 products tested "non-detect"** for all four metals (Lead, Cadmium, Mercury, Arsenic)
- Problem ingredients: bentonite clay, hydroxyapatite, calcium carbonate, hydrated silica, titanium dioxide

### Specific Brand Findings
- **Hello** (fluoride-free children's): trace lead in watermelon flavor formulas
- **Uncle Harry's** (clay-based): Proposition 65 warning; bentonite clay contains lead
- **Colgate/Crest**: consistently pass heavy metal screening; ADA-approved lines lead-free
- Pre-1950s: toothpaste tubes made from **lead-tin alloy**

## Tampons (Shearston et al., 2024 — UC Berkeley/Columbia)

### Key Study Data
- Published: *Environment International*, 2024
- 30 tampons from **14 brands**, 18 product lines tested
- **All 16 metals detected** in at least one sample
- **12 of 16 metals found in 100% of samples** above method detection limit
- Lead: geometric mean = **120 ng/g** (highest among toxic metals)
- Cadmium: GM = **6.74 ng/g**
- Arsenic: GM = **2.56 ng/g** (present in 95% of samples)

### Comparative Findings
- **Non-organic tampons**: higher lead
- **Organic tampons**: higher arsenic
- **No category had consistently lower concentrations of all metals**
- EU/UK-purchased tampons: significantly lower cadmium, cobalt, lead than U.S.
- **52-86% of U.S. menstruators use tampons**
- Lifetime usage: **~11,000 tampons** over fertile lifetime
- Vaginal mucosal membrane: highly absorptive tissue
- **No safe exposure level for lead**
- **First study ever** to measure metals in tampons (patented 1933, 90+ years later)
- FDA currently investigating

## Baby Bottles

### Glass Baby Bottle Lead (Mamavation/DetectLead.com, 2024)
- **91% of glass baby bottles tested had detectable lead in print/design** (22 of 24)
- At least **34% had lead high enough to issue recall**
- Lead transfers to human skin upon handling (UV perovskite detection confirmed)
- Brands with lead issues: Pura Kiki, Green Sprouts, Nuk, Hevea, Tabor Place, Lansinoh, Pigeon, Nouri, Maymom, Simba, Cupkin, Wee Sprout, Crocodile Creek, Paulie Jar, Jervis & George, Zak!

### Recalls
- **Green Sprouts** (Nov 2022): 10,500+ stainless steel sippy cups/bottles recalled; solder dot contained lead; base could break off exposing lead
- **Disney-themed clothing** (Nov 2022): 85,000 items recalled; lead in textile ink; sold at TJ Maxx, Ross, Burlington, Amazon
- **Pigeon/Lansinoh** glass bottles: paused sales April 2024 after Lead Safe Mama detected lead; updated designs claimed lead-free

### General Patterns
- Stainless steel bottles: leaded "sealing dot" consistently found in insulated bottles (Stanley, Takeya, ThermoFlask, Yeti, Zak!)
- Decorative paint on glass/metal bottles: common lead vector
- Clear glass WITHOUT painted markings: generally safe
- Modern plastic/silicone: generally lead-free; silicone may have trace cadmium

## Dinnerware / Cookware

### Corelle (2024 Warning)
- New Hampshire DHHS warning: **pre-2005 Corelle pieces contain high lead levels**
- 77,000+ shares on social media
- Decades of use deteriorates paint, increasing lead ingestion risk
- Corelle acknowledged: before 2000, small amount of lead used in decoration of many household items

### Ceramic Glazes
- Lead used in glazes for durability and bright colors
- Cadmium used for red, yellow, orange colors
- Leaching accelerated by: acidic foods, microwaving
- Imported ceramic dinnerware often leaks lead/cadmium above FDA limits
- EU limits: Category 1 (small items): 0.8 mg/dm² lead; Category 2 (fillable): 4.0 mg/L

### Artisan/Recycled Cookware
- Sub-Saharan Africa: locally made cookware leaches significant lead, aluminum, cadmium, chromium, nickel
- Artisan cookware from recycled materials (cans, engine parts) poses health risk
- By 2000, 43 nations used scrap metal as principal aluminum source; 35% from U.S.
- South African artisan foundry workers: higher blood lead than community non-exposed

### Enamelware
- Falcon Housewares plate (2024): Lead 16-60 ppm, Antimony 192-1,686 ppm, Arsenic 31 ppm on food surface
- Antimony (known carcinogen, added to U.S. carcinogen list Dec 2021) has replaced lead in many modern applications
- Le Creuset and other premium brands: typically test positive for lead, cadmium, or antimony in at least one component

## Supplements and Food

### Lead Safe Mama Testing Initiative
- 600+ lab reports published
- 7 product recalls (FDA and CPSC) since July 2022 attributable to their work
- Breakfast cereals, coffee, tea, candy, snacks, salt, sweeteners all tested
- Found lead in various "natural" and "organic" products via contaminated soil, manufacturing processes

### General Mechanisms
- Heavy metals enter products via: contaminated soil/water, pesticides, fertilizers, manufacturing processes, intentional additives (whitening, antimicrobial, lubrication, odor control)
- No global quality standards for menstrual products exist (ISO TC 338 in development)
- FDA does not require heavy metal testing for many consumer product categories
- ALARA principle (as low as reasonably achievable) for lead — but often not enforced
