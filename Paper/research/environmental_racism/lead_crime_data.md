# Lead-Crime Hypothesis: Empirical Data for Biological Extraction Chapter

## Core Epidemiological Findings

### Nevin (2000, 2007) — International Lead-Crime Correlation
- Childhood gasoline lead exposure (1941-1986) explains **90% of variation in U.S. violent crime rates** (1960-1998)
- 23-year developmental lag consistent with peak violent offending age
- Paint lead trends (1879-1938) explain **70% of variation in U.S. murder rates** (1900-1960)
- 21-year lag consistent with typical murder arrest age
- Same relationship holds across Britain, Canada, France, Australia, Finland, Italy, West Germany, New Zealand

### Reyes (2007) — Lead Abatement and the 1990s Crime Drop
- Elasticity of violent crime with respect to childhood lead exposure: **approximately 0.8**
- Reduction in lead exposure in 1970s responsible for **56% drop in violent crime in the 1990s**
- Phase-out of leaded gasoline under Clean Air Act

### Mielke & Zahran (2012) — City-Level Aggravated Assault
- 1% increase in air Pb released 22 years prior → **0.46% increase in present aggravated assault rate** (95% CI 0.28-0.64)
- Model explains **90% of variation** across 6 major U.S. cities
- New Orleans: **85% of temporal variation** in aggravated assault explained by air Pb
- For every metric ton of Pb released 22 years prior: **1.59 latent increase in aggravated assaults per 100,000**

### Wright et al. (2008) — Prenatal/Childhood Blood Lead and Criminal Arrests
- Cincinnati cohort, 250 individuals aged 19-24
- Each 5 μg/dL increase in blood lead:
  - Prenatal: **RR=1.40** (95% CI 1.07-1.85) for total arrests
  - 6-year: **RR=1.27** (95% CI 1.03-1.57) for total arrests
  - Prenatal: **RR=1.70** (95% CI 1.06-2.76) for violent crime arrests

### Needleman et al. — Bone Lead and Juvenile Delinquency
- 194 youths aged 12-18, arrested/adjudicated delinquent vs 146 controls
- Cases had significantly higher mean bone lead (11.0 vs 1.5 ppm)
- Adjudicated delinquents **4× more likely** to have bone lead >25 ppm (OR=4.0, 95% CI 1.4-11.1)
- Lead levels **4× higher** among convicted juvenile offenders vs non-delinquent high school students

### Higney, Hanley & Moro (2022) — Meta-Analysis
- 542 estimates from 24 studies
- Mean partial correlation: 0.16
- Mean elasticity: 0.09
- Lead abatement responsible for **7-28% of fall in U.S. homicide**
- 6-20% of convergence in urban/rural crime rates
- Publication bias means effects are OVERSTATED in literature; even corrected estimates are significant

## Environmental Racism: Disparate Exposure Data

### Geographic and Racial Disparities
- Black children in 1970s: exposed to atmospheric lead **15× higher** than rural levels
- 1966-1974: **62% of Black children under 6** lived in central cities vs **24% of white children**
- Air lead adjacent to heavy traffic: **up to 15× higher** than city average
- Cities >1M population: ambient air lead **2× higher** than smaller cities
- Black families occupied **56% of substandard housing** in central cities (1960)
- Houses built before 1940: ~80% likely to contain lead paint; 1940-1959: 46% likely

### New Orleans Case Study
- Pre-Katrina: **93.5% of children** had blood Pb ≥2 μg/dL
- 20-30% of inner-city kids had blood Pb >10 μg/dL
- Black residents: 5.4 μg/dL (males), 3.4 μg/dL (females) vs white: 4.4/3.0
- Black residents **2× more likely** to live in high-lead areas than white residents
- 10 census tracts had median soil Pb ≥1000 mg/kg (U.S. standard: 400 mg/kg)
- Iberville Housing Project soil Pb: up to 916 mg/kg

### St. Louis
- Aggregate blood lead at census tract level predicts violent crime
- Risk ratios per unit increase: firearm crimes 1.03, assault 1.03, robbery 1.03, homicide 1.03
- Statistically significant after accounting for sociological variables

### Flint
- African American populations in East/Central Flint: **76.8% and 67%** had highest water lead levels
- Far north/south (middle-class): lowest BLLs
- Areas with highest lead poisoning showed increase in poverty and crime, population decrease

## Neurobiological Mechanism
- Lead mimics calcium (Ca²⁺), crosses blood-brain barrier easily
- Children under 6: neurological defenses not fully formed
- Permanently damages prefrontal cortex, impairing executive function
- Results: lowered IQ (~0.5 points per 1 μg/dL), ADHD, impulsivity, aggression
- "Behavioral constellation of deprivation" linking environmental toxicity to social inequality

## Lead in Schools — The Unregulated Vector
- **EPA does NOT regulate lead in school drinking water** under Safe Drinking Water Act (schools not public water systems)
- Testing voluntary in majority of states; remediation unfunded
- GAO 2018: only **43% of school districts tested** for lead; of those, **37% found elevated levels**
- Millions of children attend schools where lead testing never conducted
- Schools built before 1986: lead solder, brass fixtures, lead service lines
- Harvard study (Doré et al., 2019): first-draw lead concentrations spike after weekends/vacations
- Property-tax feedback loop: redlined neighborhoods → depressed property values → lowest per-pupil spending → oldest plumbing → lead in school water
