# SOURCE_CHECK_3 — Massachusetts 2024 feature-list rebuttal video

Source check for five claims about rifle features (adjustable stock, pistol grip,
forward grip, threaded barrel, barrel shroud) and who they help.

Status: COMPLETE — all five items checked, 2026-09-25. Per the user's constraints this
is a single-researcher pass; no independent verifier pass (AGENTS.md Rule 4) was run.

## Item 1 — Length of pull and shooter size

(claim: adjustable-length stock lets shorter/smaller shooters fit a rifle; poor fit hurts control and comfort)

**VERIFIED.** The general fit claim — proper length of pull varies by shooter frame and
poor fit costs comfort and control — is directly supported by NRA-published training
content.

Sources:

1. [The Importance of Length of Pull — American Hunter (NRA), 2025](https://www.americanhunter.org/content/the-importance-of-length-of-pull/)
   - Quote (21 words): "A proper length of pull ensures best posture, minimizes felt recoil and I feel it allows a shooter to perform their best."
   - Shows: LOP should vary with shooter frame (12–15 inches); author's 5'2" wife
     shoots a rifle with a shorter 12.5-inch LOP for a smaller frame; wrong LOP hurts
     comfort, shoulder position, and recoil management ("It pounded me to bits").
   - Does not show: it discusses fixed and dedicated stocks, not collapsible AR
     stocks specifically, and it is a hunting context, not a defensive-rifle context.

2. [Length of Pull: A Complete Guide for Fitting Your Rifle to Your Body — Athlon Outdoors](https://athlonoutdoors.com/article/length-of-pull-guide/)
   - Not opened (search-result level only): presents LOP measurement as improving
     comfort and precision; usable as corroboration, weaker than the NRA source.

Provenance tier: trade/instructional press (American Hunter is an official NRA
publication). Artifact opened: full article text via web fetch, 2026-09-25.

## Item 2 — Pistol grips and forward grips for control / limited hand-arm strength

**PARTLY.**

- Forward grip half: **VERIFIED.** Adaptive-shooting coverage directly recommends
  vertical and angled foregrips for shooters with shoulder and mobility limitations.
- Pistol grip half: **PARTLY.** Trade sources tie pistol-grip angle to wrist
  alignment, recoil control, and comfort; no opened source ties pistol grips
  specifically to shooters with limited wrist, hand, or arm strength.

Sources:

1. [Best Guns & Gear for Disabled Shooters — Pew Pew Tactical, 2023](https://www.pewpewtactical.com/choosing-handgun-disabled-shooters/)
   - Quote (23 words): "Vertical foregrips and angled foregrips can help you find the right placement to support your gun without causing extra stress to your shoulder."
   - Shows: a writer with post-traumatic shoulder arthritis reports foregrips "made a
     huge difference"; catalogues adaptive gear (bipods, assisted triggers, NRA
     Adaptive Shooting Program, Adaptive Defensive Shooting Summit).
   - Does not show: it is one enthusiast publication's guide, authored by a disabled
     shooter; it covers foregrips, not pistol grips; no clinical or OT evidence.

2. [AR-15 Grip Angle Explained: 15°, 17°, and Vertical Grips — Black Rifle Depot](https://blackrifledepot.com/ar-15-grip-angle-explained15-17/)
   - Quote (search-result snippet, 15 words): "A 15° grip helps keep the wrist
     straighter, making it easier to maintain consistent pressure and trigger control."
   - Shows: pistol-grip angle is selected for wrist alignment and control.
   - Does not show: vendor content, not disability-specific; snippet level only
     (page not opened).

3. [NRA's Adaptive Shooting Program Aids Disabled Hunters — NRA Hunters' Leadership Forum, 2019](https://www.nrahlf.org/articles/2019/9/30/nra-s-adaptive-shooting-program-aids-disabled-hunters/)
   - Quote (16 words): "It can be as simple as lighter caliber guns to accommodate decreased grip strength associated with arthritis."
   - Shows: the NRA Adaptive Shooting Program (launched 2015, successor to the
     decades-old Disabled Shooting Program) aids disabled hunters and trains adaptive
     instructors; the program manager names decreased grip strength from arthritis as
     an explicit accommodation case; competition waivers permit equipment modifications.
   - Does not show: pistol grips and forward grips are never mentioned; the
     grip-strength example is solved with lighter caliber guns, not grip hardware.

Provenance tier: trade/adaptive-shooting press; NRA-affiliated hunting outlet for the
program description. Artifacts opened: full Pew Pew Tactical and NRA HLF article text
via web fetch, 2026-09-25.

## Item 3 — Muzzle brakes on threaded barrels reduce felt recoil

**VERIFIED** for the mechanical claim. The further inference that this matters
specifically to smaller or recoil-sensitive shooters is supported only as general
shooter-comfort language; no opened source names that demographic.

Sources:

1. [How Muzzle Brakes Work and The Physics of Recoil Reduction — Savage Arms](https://www.savagearms.com/blog?p=how-muzzle-brakes-work-and-the-physics-of-recoil-reduction)
   - Quote (21 words): "This results in a reduction of felt recoil, making the firearm more comfortable to shoot and allowing for quicker follow-up shots."
   - Shows: brakes thread onto the muzzle ("a series of ports that is threaded onto
     the muzzle"), redirect propellant gas to counteract rearward force, and reduce
     felt recoil, muzzle rise, and physical stress on the shooter.
   - Does not show: a percentage figure, an independent measurement, or any statement
     about smaller-statured or recoil-sensitive shooters as a class.

2. [Browning Recoil Hawg muzzle brake product copy](https://internationalshootingsupplies.com/product/browning-recoil-hawg-muzzle-brake-burnt-bronze-30-cal-w-5-8-24-and-1-2-28-thread-adapters/)
   - Quote (search-result snippet, 12 words): "devouring felt recoil by as much as
     76% (depending on the caliber)."
   - Shows: a manufacturer quantified recoil-reduction claim for a thread-on brake.
   - Does not show: vendor product copy; no methodology; snippet level only (page
     not opened).

Provenance tier: manufacturer/trade. Artifact opened: full Savage Arms article text
via web fetch, 2026-09-25.

## Item 4 — Women and Black gun owners a growing share of AR-15-style owners/buyers

**PARTLY.** Current demographics of AR-15-style ownership are documented by a large
academic survey; growth among women and Black buyers is documented for gun buyers
generally (NSSF retailer surveys); no opened source tracks growth in AR-15-style
rifle ownership by sex or race over time.

Sources:

1. [2021 National Firearms Survey: Updated Analysis Including Types of Firearms Owned — William English, PhD (Georgetown), expanded report May 13, 2022](https://michellawyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2024-09-06-Exhibit-1-William-English-2021-National-Firearms-Survey-ISO-State-Defendants-Mtn-to-Preclude-Consideration-of-William-English-NSSF-Surveys.pdf) (court-filed exhibit; also at SSRN abstract 4213687)
   - Quote (16 words): "30.2% of gun owners – about 24.6 million individuals – have owned an AR-15 or similarly styled rifle."
   - Exact figures (survey fielded Feb 17 – Mar 23, 2021; n=54,244 adults, 16,708
     gun owners): 42.2% of gun owners are female; 10.6% of gun owners identify as
     Black; 25.4% of Black adults own firearms vs 34.3% of White adults.
   - A demographic table quoted from this survey in a filed declaration
     ([Hanish declaration, Dec 1, 2022](https://michellawyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2022-12-01-Dec-of-Mark-Hanish-ISO-Plaintiffs-Supp-Brief.pdf),
     snippet level — the table page itself was not rendered in my fetch) reports
     ever-ownership of AR-15-styled rifles at 34.0% among Black gun owners
     (95% CI 31.0–...) vs 29.6% among White gun owners (95% CI 28.9–30.4%).
   - Shows: women are 42.2% of gun owners, and Black gun owners report AR-15-style
     ever-ownership at a higher rate than White gun owners in 2021.
   - Does not show: a time series. The survey is cross-sectional, so "growing share"
     is not directly measured; it also uses "have ever owned," not current ownership.

2. [NSSF Retailer Surveys Indicate 5.4 Million First-Time Gun Buyers in 2021 — NSSF, Jan 25, 2022](https://www.nssf.org/articles/nssf-retailer-surveys-indicate-5-4-million-first-time-gun-buyers-in-2021/)
   - Quote (12 words): "Over 33 percent of first-time gun buyers in 2021 were women."
   - Exact figures (page opened in full): 5.4 million first-time buyers in 2021,
     nearly 30% of all 2021 firearm purchases; over 33% of 2021 first-time buyers
     were women; 44% of retailers saw increased African-American purchases in 2021;
     for 2020: 8.4 million first-time buyers (40% of all buyers) and a 58% increase
     in African-American purchases vs 2019 (49% Hispanic-American, 43%
     Asian-American). [American Rifleman, Feb 15, 2022](https://www.americanrifleman.org/content/survey-indicates-millions-of-new-gun-owners-changing-demographics/)
     (opened in full) confirms these figures: "One third of the people purchasing
     their first firearm in 2021 were women."
   - The widely cited figure that 40% of 2020 first-time buyers were women traces
     to NSSF's May 2020 retailer survey (snippet-level corroboration:
     [The Truth About Guns, Jun 1, 2020](https://www.thetruthaboutguns.com/retailers-40-of-2020-gun-buyers-are-first-timers-and-40-of-those-are-female/);
     [A Girl & A Gun, Aug 20, 2020](https://www.agirlandagun.org/why-are-women-buying-guns/));
     the primary NSSF 2020 release was not opened.
   - Shows: women and Black Americans were a large and growing share of new gun
     buyers in 2020–2021.
   - Does not show: these are all-firearm buyer surveys, not AR-15-specific, and
     the retailer-demographic figures are retailer impressions, not buyer counts.

Provenance tier: scholarly secondary (English survey, court-filed) and industry
trade association (NSSF). Artifacts opened: full NSSF press release and full
American Rifleman article via web fetch, 2026-09-25; English report PDF pages
1–18 of 46 opened earlier the same day, remaining pages not retrieved.

## Item 5 — Published critique that feature bans fall on accessibility features / women / disabled shooters

**VERIFIED.** Such critiques exist in filed litigation and in a law-review article.
They come from Second Amendment advocates; no neutral or academic treatment making
the accessibility argument was found.

Sources:

1. [FFL-IL et al. v. Pritzker, No. 3:23-cv-00215-SPM (S.D. Ill.), Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint, filed Sept 13, 2024](https://michellawyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2024-09-13-Unopposed-Mtn-to-File-SAC.pdf) — challenges Illinois's feature-based ban.
   - Quote (24 words, complaint ¶4): the ban reaches "various attachments with ergonomic
     features, such as those designed to aid the physically disabled and/or smaller
     stature individuals in protecting themselves, their families and/or their homes."
   - Complaint ¶21: plaintiff Chris Moore "wants his wife to be able to use the AR-15
     for the self-defense of her family and herself, because she is of shorter stature
     with a weaker grip and the AR-15 is easier, and, thus, safer for her."
   - Complaint ¶72: "Women have increasingly taken Oakley's advice, opting for the
     AR-15, because its modular design that's easily fitted with accessories make the
     firearm ideal for users of all sizes."
   - Shows: a filed, public critique arguing a state feature ban falls on ergonomic
     features serving disabled and smaller-statured shooters and women.
   - Does not show: an adjudicated finding; these are plaintiffs' allegations, and the
     case concerns Illinois, not Massachusetts.

2. David B. Kopel, "Rational Basis Analysis of 'Assault Weapon' Prohibition," 20
   Journal of Contemporary Law — cited in the [Brief for the Petitioners, Nos. 25-238 & 25-566 (U.S. Supreme Court, filed Aug 28, 2026)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-238/422409/20260828162156469_25-238%2025-566%20Brief%20of%20the%20Petitioners.pdf) (snippet level; brief not opened in full).
   - Quote (search-result snippet, 6 words): "The pistol grip solves that problem."
   - Shows: a published scholarly critique of feature-test bans, invoked at the
     Supreme Court level, treating the pistol grip as a functional aid.
   - Does not show: the article itself was not opened; its full argument on fit,
     women, or disabled shooters is unconfirmed.

Provenance tier: primary litigation documents (public court record) and scholarly
secondary (Kopel). Artifact opened: pages 1–21 of 106 of the SAC motion PDF via web
fetch, 2026-09-25.
