# Sources: Chapter 135 / Question 9 reply

> **The framework behind this video is *The Original Power*.**
> **[Read or download the book: github.com/th30d0re/TheOriginalPower/releases](https://github.com/th30d0re/TheOriginalPower/releases)**

This page lists the sources for every factual claim in the video, in the order the claims appear. Each entry says what the video states and where to check it. Where a source is a news report and not the primary document, or a figure is national and not Massachusetts-specific, the entry says so. Web pages were last checked in September 2026.

Some lines in the video are the speaker's own arguments and opinions: that the law works as racism, sexism, and ableism together, that a guilty conscience may lie behind the governor's choice of word, and that reparations are the fix. The sources below establish the facts those arguments rest on. They do not establish the conclusions.

## 1. What Chapter 135 is

**In the video:** Chapter 135 is "An Act Modernizing Firearm Laws," House Bill 4885, signed by Gov. Healey on July 25, 2024, and put into effect immediately by an emergency preamble.

- [Session law text, malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/2024/Chapter135)
- [Congressional Sportsmen's Foundation, "Massachusetts' New Gun Laws"](https://congressionalsportsmen.org/news/massachusetts-new-gun-laws/) (provisions, signing and effective dates)

## 2. The ballot question

**In the video:** Question 9 is on the November 3, 2026 statewide ballot. A yes vote keeps the 2024 law. A no vote repeals it.

- [Commonwealth Beacon, 2026 ballot guide: firearms](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/ballot-question/firearms/)
- [Serpa Law, "Massachusetts Question 9: What It Changes... and What It Does Not"](https://www.serpalaw.com/boston-criminal-law-updates/massachusetts-question-9-firearms-referendum-2026/)
- [Ballotpedia, Massachusetts Firearm Regulations Referendum (2026)](https://ballotpedia.org/Massachusetts_Firearm_Regulations_Referendum_(2026)) (confirms the election date and the vote mechanics; the measure number "9" rests on the first two sources)

## 3. Who put it on the ballot, and the deadline

**In the video:** The campaign collected 78,707 certified signatures, above the 37,287 required. The law's registration and serialization requirements come due in October 2026, before the November 3 vote.

- [Serpa Law, Question 9 summary](https://www.serpalaw.com/boston-criminal-law-updates/massachusetts-question-9-firearms-referendum-2026/): "The registration and serialization requirements of chapter 135 come due in October 2026, before the November 3 election."
- [Ballotpedia News, "Veto referendum on firearm regulations certified for 2026 ballot"](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2024/12/03/veto-referendum-on-firearm-regulations-certified-for-2026-ballot-in-massachusetts/) (93,229 signatures filed, 78,707 valid; thresholds of 37,287 for the ballot and 49,716 to suspend the law)

## 4. The reel

**In the video:** The @harvardseye reel with Gov. Healey was filmed in front of a wall of "Yes on 9" signage. One shot shows a podium with a "Yes on 9" placard.

- [The reel on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdoZBRXIbK5/) (the video file was reviewed frame by frame)

## 5. Neighborhood disadvantage and gun-violence exposure

**In the video:** A study across a hundred cities found close to a 50-percentage-point gap in gun-violence exposure between the most and least disadvantaged neighborhoods, far larger than the gap from household poverty alone.

- [Inequities in Community Exposure to Deadly Gun Violence by Race/Ethnicity, Poverty, and Neighborhood Disadvantage among Youth in Large US Cities (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9172977/)

## 6. Lead exposure and crime; Boston's redlining

**In the video:** Economists have tied childhood lead exposure to crime. Boston was mapped and graded by the federal government in 1938, and Roxbury and Dorchester received the lower grades.

- [Reyes (2007), "Environmental Policy as Social Policy? The Impact of Childhood Lead Exposure on Crime," NBER Working Paper 13097](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13097): ties the phase-out of leaded gasoline to a large share of the later decline in violent crime.
- [Mapping Inequality, University of Richmond: Boston](https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/map/MA/Boston/context): Boston's 1938 Home Owners' Loan Corporation map. The South End and Roxbury corridor is graded D ("Hazardous"), and Dorchester is graded C ("Definitely Declining"). The map digitization is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- [Frontiers in Public Health (2025), "Connections between redlining, food access, hypertension, diabetes, and obesity in Boston"](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1505462/full) (quotes the 1938 area descriptions)

**Limit:** the Reyes study is national. No study found directly links Boston's redlining to crime through lead. That connection is the video's own argument.

## 7. Baltimore's homicide decline

**In the video:** Baltimore's homicide rate dropped 23 percent in a single year, a decline credited to a violence-reduction strategy and not to a new gun law.

- [Baltimore City, Mayor Scott announces continued decline in homicides](https://mayor.baltimorecity.gov/news/press-releases/2025-10-01-mayor-brandon-m-scott-announces-continued-decline-homicides): 201 homicides in 2024 against 261 in 2023.
- [The Trace, "What Can Cities Learn From Baltimore's Extraordinary Decline in Violence?"](https://www.thetrace.org/2026/07/baltimore-violence-reduction-strategy/) (the Group Violence Reduction Strategy and its services)

## 8. New York City's decline in shootings

**In the video:** New York's shootings fell for a third straight year, and its police department credits targeted enforcement.

- [NYPD, "New York City Crime Continues to Decline" (Jan. 2025)](https://www.nyc.gov/site/nypd/news/pr006/new-york-city-crime-continues-decline-1-700-fewer-major-crimes-january-2025): credits hot-spot deployment and "a laser-like focus on criminals who use illegal guns."

## 9. Article 48, the 90-day stay, and the emergency preamble

**In the video:** Under Article 48 of the Massachusetts Constitution, a qualifying referendum petition normally stays a new law until voters decide. An emergency preamble makes a law take effect immediately, and a petition cannot stay it.

- [Ballotpedia, Article XLVIII of the Massachusetts Constitution](https://ballotpedia.org/Article_XLVIII,_Amendments_to_the_Massachusetts_Constitution)
- [mass.gov, Effective dates of laws in Massachusetts](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/effective-dates-of-laws-in-massachusetts)

## 10. The timeline: signing, the preamble, and the signatures

**In the video:** Healey signed the bill on July 25, 2024, with an October 23 effective date. She signed the emergency preamble on October 2, 69 days later, as it became public that the repeal campaign had the signatures to trigger a stay.

- July 25 to October 2, 2024 is 69 days by calendar arithmetic. Some coverage, including a WCVB broadcast quote, says 71.
- The campaign submitted 93,229 signatures to local election officials on October 9. The Secretary of the Commonwealth's office verified 78,707 on November 22, 2024.
- Healey's stated reasons, as reported that day: the measures needed to "go into effect without delay" (AP, via Ballotpedia) and "we needed to give time to our agencies and municipalities to take the steps and do what they need to do in order to get ready for implementation on Day One" (NBC Boston).

## 11. Historical grandfather clauses

**In the video:** Louisiana's 1898 constitution exempted anyone from new voting tests if they, their father, or their grandfather had been eligible to vote before January 1, 1867. Black registration fell from 130,344 to 5,320 in three years, a drop of about 96 percent. White registration fell by about a quarter. The Supreme Court struck down grandfather clauses in *Guinn v. United States* (1915), and said the clause was racially neutral on its face.

- [BlackPast, "(1898) Louisiana Grandfather Clause"](https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/1898-louisiana-grandfather-clause/)
- [Guinn v. United States (1915)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinn_v._United_States)

**Limit:** the registration figures come from secondary summaries. The white figure varies by end year: 164,088 in 1897 and 125,437 in 1900 (down about 24 percent), with a dip to 74,133 in 1898.

## 12. Chapter 135's own grandfather clause

**In the video:** A firearm lawfully possessed in Massachusetts on August 1, 2024 is exempt from the ban if the owner holds a license to carry or a license to sell and the firearm is registered and serialized. The registration deadline is October 28, 2026. A firearm acquired after that date cannot legally be acquired.

- [G.L. c. 140, § 131M](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section131M) (the exemption for lawfully possessed firearms)
- [MassLegalResources, "The October 2026 Gun Registration Deadline in Massachusetts"](https://masslegalresources.com/2026/08/23/massachusetts-gun-registration-deadline/)

## 13. The penalty

**In the video:** A first offense carries a fine of $1,000 to $10,000, one to ten years in prison, or both.

- [G.L. c. 140, § 131M](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section131M): the statute reads "fine of not less than $1,000 nor more than $10,000 or by imprisonment for not less than 1 year nor more than 10 years." A second offense carries a fine of $5,000 to $15,000 or imprisonment of 5 to 15 years.

**Limit:** the statute names neither state prison nor the house of correction, and it does not use the word "felony."

## 14. Gun ownership among Black women

**In the video:** The National Shooting Sports Foundation recorded an 87 percent jump in gun ownership among Black women in 2021, and Black gun buyers were up 58 percent in 2020.

- [KRON4, "Gun ownership increasing among Black women"](https://www.kron4.com/hidden-history/black-history-month/gun-ownership-increasing-among-black-women/)
- [National African American Gun Association, "The Growth of Women of Color in Firearms"](https://naaga.co/2022/12/the-growth-of-women-of-color-in-firearms/)
- [CNN Business, "First-time buyers fuel pandemic-related surge in gun sales" (Oct. 2020)](https://www.cnn.com/2020/10/24/business/gun-sales-surge-black-americans-women/index.html)

**Limit:** these are survey and retailer figures reported by the press, and they cover firearms in general, not one type of rifle.

## 15. Bruen and Massachusetts's licensing system

**In the video:** *New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen* (June 23, 2022) struck down discretionary "may issue" licensing and named Massachusetts's "good reason" standard. Massachusetts went from discretionary to shall-issue. Before that, the local licensing authority, usually the police chief or a designee, could refuse a License to Carry. A rifle needed only a Firearm Identification (FID) card.

- [Boston Bar Association Journal, on firearms licensing after Bruen](https://bostonbar.org/journal/commonwealth-v-marquis-commonwealth-v-donnell-firearms-licensing-for-nonresidents/)
- [New York State Rifle & Pistol Ass'n v. Bruen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Rifle_%26_Pistol_Ass%27n_v._Bruen)
- [MVSLLP, on the LTC and FID distinction and Bruen's scope](https://mvsllp.com/criminal-defense/gun-charges/possession-firearm-without-fid/)
- [G.L. c. 140, § 121](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section121): defines the licensing authority as "the chief of police or the board or officer having control of the police in a city or town, or persons authorized by them."

## 16. What a License to Carry costs

**In the video:** A $100 non-refundable state application fee, plus a mandatory live-fire safety course, typically $100 to $150, required for new applicants since August 1, 2024.

- [mass.gov, License to Carry application](https://www.mass.gov/doc/non-resident-license-to-carry-firearms-application-0/download) (the fee)

**Limit:** course prices come from several Massachusetts training providers. There is no single official price list.

**In the video:** The AR-15 is banned by name. The Ruger Mini-14 is named by the Massachusetts Attorney General as an example of a gun that is not covered. They fire the same round.

- [mass.gov, Enforcing the Massachusetts Assault Weapons Ban](https://www.mass.gov/enforcing-the-massachusetts-assault-weapons-ban)
- [Guns.com, "Mass. AG releases new info on 'guns that are not assault weapons'"](https://www.guns.com/news/2016/08/19/massachusetts-ag-releases-new-info-on-guns-that-are-not-assault-weapons)

## 18. What the statute bans, and what each part does

**In the video:** The statute bans a semiautomatic, centerfire rifle with a detachable magazine and at least two of five features: a folding or telescopic stock; a thumbhole stock or pistol grip; a forward or second grip; a threaded barrel; or "a shroud that encircles either all or part of the barrel designed to shield the bearer's hand from heat."

- [G.L. c. 140, § 121](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section121), definition of "assault-style firearm." Quoted text: "a semiautomatic, centerfire rifle with the capacity to accept a detachable feeding device and includes at least 2 of the following features: (i) a folding or telescopic stock; (ii) a thumbhole stock or pistol grip; (iii) a forward grip or second handgrip or protruding grip that can be held by the non-trigger hand; (iv) a threaded barrel designed to accommodate a flash suppressor or muzzle break or similar feature; or (v) a shroud that encircles either all or part of the barrel designed to shield the bearer's hand from heat, excluding a slide that encloses the barrel."

**What the parts do.** The video says an adjustable stock changes the length of pull, so a smaller frame gets a proper fit. It also says a pistol grip is easier to control, a forward grip steadies the support hand, and a threaded barrel accepts a flash suppressor or muzzle brake. These sources support the practical claims:
- [American Hunter (NRA), "The Importance of Length of Pull"](https://www.americanhunter.org/content/the-importance-of-length-of-pull/): proper length of pull varies with the shooter's frame.
- [Pew Pew Tactical, "Best Guns & Gear for Disabled Shooters"](https://www.pewpewtactical.com/choosing-handgun-disabled-shooters/): a disabled shooter's account of foregrips.
- [Savage Arms, "How Muzzle Brakes Work and The Physics of Recoil Reduction"](https://www.savagearms.com/blog?p=how-muzzle-brakes-work-and-the-physics-of-recoil-reduction)
- [NRA Hunters' Leadership Forum, on the NRA Adaptive Shooting Program](https://www.nrahlf.org/articles/2019/9/30/nra-s-adaptive-shooting-program-aids-disabled-hunters/)

**Limits:** the sources cover fit and control in general terms. None names the Massachusetts law. The illustrations of the parts in the video are computer-generated, not photographs of a specific product. The argument that these parts mainly help women, smaller shooters, and shooters with disabilities, and that banning them therefore falls hardest on those groups, is the speaker's argument. Gun-rights plaintiffs in *FFL-IL v. Pritzker* (S.D. Ill.) make a similar argument about Illinois's ban. Those are allegations in a complaint, not findings.

## 19. Who already owns guns, and who is buying

**In the video:** Historically, the people who already owned guns were overwhelmingly white. Black buyers and Black women in particular are among the fastest-growing groups of new owners.

- [Pew Research Center, "The Demographics of Gun Ownership" (June 2017)](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2017/06/22/the-demographics-of-gun-ownership/): 48 percent of white men own a gun, against 24 percent of white women, 24 percent of nonwhite men, and 16 percent of nonwhite women.
- [Pew Research Center, "Key facts about Americans and guns" (2024; June 2023 survey)](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/07/24/key-facts-about-americans-and-guns/): 38 percent of White, 24 percent of Black, 20 percent of Hispanic, and 10 percent of Asian adults own a gun. Among non-owners, 56 percent of Black adults could see themselves owning one, the highest of any group.
- [Pew Research Center, "For Most U.S. Gun Owners, Protection Is the Main Reason They Own a Gun" (Aug. 2023)](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2023/08/16/for-most-u-s-gun-owners-protection-is-the-main-reason-they-own-a-gun/)

**Limit:** all figures are national. Pew's 2017 category is "nonwhite women," not Black women specifically.

## 22. New England, poverty, and firearm homicide

**In the video:** In 2024, firearm homicide ran from 0.8 per 100,000 in New Hampshire, which Giffords grades F, to 1.9 in Connecticut, graded A, and in Vermont, graded B-minus. Across the 50 states and D.C., poverty tracks firearm homicide at about +0.72. Gun-law strength shows a weaker link, about +0.29, and that link disappears once poverty and inequality are accounted for. Total gun deaths in those states are mostly suicide.

- [CDC NCIPC, Mapping Injury, Overdose, and Violence (state data)](https://data.cdc.gov/d/fpsi-y8tj)
- [Giffords Law Center, Annual Gun Law Scorecard](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/resources/scorecard/)
- [Everytown Research, gun law rankings](https://everytownresearch.org/rankings/)
- [Census Bureau, Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/saipe.html)
- [Method and data for every number (this repository)](https://github.com/th30d0re/TheOriginalPower/blob/main/Paper/data/gun_laws_new_england/RESULTS.md)

**Limits:** rates are crude, not age-adjusted. The comparison uses 51 places (50 states and D.C.) for poverty and 50 for law rankings. This is a cross-section and cannot establish cause. With poverty and inequality controlled, the law-rank coefficient falls to about zero (−0.004, p = 0.89).

## 23. What RAND's reviews find

**In the video:** RAND's reviews find evidence for specific laws, such as child-access prevention and background checks.

- [RAND, Gun Policy in America: analysis](https://www.rand.org/research/gun-policy/analysis.html): supportive evidence, RAND's strongest rating, that child-access prevention laws reduce firearm homicides and self-injuries among youth. Moderate evidence that background checks reduce firearm and total homicides.
- [RAND, 2023 update press release (Jan. 10, 2023)](https://www.rand.org/news/press/2023/01/10.html)

**Limit:** RAND rates each policy against each outcome separately.

## 24. Safe storage, G.L. c. 140, § 131L

**In the video:** In Massachusetts, child-access prevention is a safe-storage law that covers every gun owner, with or without children. A violation carries a fine of $1,000 to $7,500 or up to a year and a half in jail.

- [G.L. c. 140, § 131L](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section131L): it is unlawful to store or keep any firearm unless it is secured in a locked container or has a tamper-resistant mechanical lock. A firearm carried by or under the control of the owner or another authorized user is not "stored or kept." The penalties for standard firearms are as stated above. Enhanced penalties apply when a minor can access the firearm.

## 25. Background checks and private transfers

**In the video:** Dealer sales require a Form 4473 and a federal background check. In Massachusetts, a private transfer has to go to someone holding a license, through the state's electronic registration system.

- [G.L. c. 140, § 128A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section128A): private sales are limited to holders of a License to Carry or FID card, and must be conducted through the electronic firearms registration system.
- [18 U.S.C. § 922(t)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/922) (the federal background check for licensed dealers)

**Limit:** the video says a license applicant passes a background check. That describes the licensing process and is not quoted from these statutes.

## 26. Boston: the 1938 map and today's shootings

**In the video:** In Boston from 2019 to 2025, the areas graded C and D in 1938 have about seven times the shooting rate of the areas graded A and B.

- [Mapping Inequality, University of Richmond: Boston](https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/redlining/map/MA/Boston/context)
- [Analyze Boston, Boston Police shootings and crime incident data](https://data.boston.gov)
- [Method and results for every number (this repository)](https://github.com/th30d0re/TheOriginalPower/blob/main/Paper/data/spatial/boston/RESULTS.md)

**What the data show.** There were 1,197 shooting victimizations with coordinates. Per 10,000 residents per year, the rate is 3.14 in grade D areas, 3.12 in grade C areas, and 0.43 in A and B combined. The rate ratios against A and B are 7.36 (95% CI 4.07 to 14.86) for D and 7.33 (4.07 to 14.75) for C. The pattern holds in each sub-period and with the densest tract cluster removed.

**Limits:** grades A and B hold only 11 of the 1,197 shootings. With poverty and the share of Black residents controlled, the tract-level redlining coefficient is null (rate ratio 1.00, 95% CI 0.68 to 1.46). So the data show that shootings remain concentrated where the 1938 map drew its lines. They do not separate the 1938 grade from the poverty and disinvestment that followed. About 8 percent of shootings fall in ungraded territory.

## 27. Intersectionality

**In the video:** Kimberlé Crenshaw coined the term in 1989, writing about Black women. In *DeGraffenreid v. General Motors*, Black women challenged a layoff policy, and the court held that they could claim race discrimination or sex discrimination but not both at once.

- [Crenshaw (1989), "Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race and Sex," University of Chicago Legal Forum](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/uclf/vol1989/iss1/8/)
- [DeGraffenreid v. General Motors Assembly Div., 413 F. Supp. 142 (E.D. Mo. 1976)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/413/142/1660699)

**Limit:** the case description follows the court's opinion as quoted in case reporters and summaries.

## 28. Firearm-feature argument and evidence

**In the video:** The speaker argues that the features the law bans help people outside the shooter the rifle was first designed around, and that the law therefore falls hardest on women, smaller shooters, and shooters with disabilities.

The sources for the practical claims are in section 18. Further evidence on who owns and buys these rifles:
- [William English, 2021 National Firearms Survey (Georgetown), expanded report (May 2022)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4213687): 42.2 percent of gun owners are female, and Black gun owners report AR-15-style ownership at a higher rate than White gun owners.
- [NSSF, retailer surveys of first-time buyers (Jan. 25, 2022)](https://www.nssf.org/articles/nssf-retailer-surveys-indicate-5-4-million-first-time-gun-buyers-in-2021/): over a third of 2021 first-time buyers were women.

**Limit:** neither source tracks AR-style rifle ownership over time, and neither addresses the Massachusetts law.
