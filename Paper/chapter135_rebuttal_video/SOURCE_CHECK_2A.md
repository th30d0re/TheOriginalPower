# Source Check 2A — Massachusetts Licensing, Storage, and Background-Check Claims

**Scope:** Three claims from a spoken script about Massachusetts firearms law, checked against primary sources only.
**Dates fetched:** All live pages (malegislature.gov, mass.gov, atf.gov, ecfr.gov, uscode.house.gov) fetched 2026-09-25. Historical (pre–St. 2024 c. 135) statute text taken from Wayback Machine captures of malegislature.gov (G.L. c. 140 §131: 2020-era capture showing text as amended by St. 2014 c. 284; §131L: 2024 capture predating the c. 135 amendment). fbi.gov blocked direct fetch; the FBI NICS page was read via a 2024 Wayback capture of fbi.gov.

Bottom line: the script's three claims are directionally right but each is overstated or out of date in a specific way. Details below.

---

## Claim 1 — "Under Massachusetts law the licensing authority for a License to Carry or FID card is the local police chief."

**Verdict: PARTLY VERIFIED.**

**Who the licensing authority is.** The statute is broader than "the local police chief."

- Source: G.L. c. 140, §121 (current text, post–St. 2024 c. 135), https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section121 (fetched 2026-09-25).
- Quote: "'Licensing authority', the chief of police or the board or officer having control of the police in a city or town, or persons authorized by them" (22 words).
- The same definition has stood since St. 1998 c. 180, §8 ("'Licensing authority', the chief of police or the board or officer having control of the police in a city or town, or persons authorized by them"), https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/1998/Chapter180 (fetched 2026-09-25).

So: the chief of police is the usual licensing authority, but the statute also covers the "board or officer having control of the police" (this is how Boston's police commissioner fits) and expressly allows "persons authorized by them" — i.e., a designee such as a civilian or sworn licensing officer can act. If no such chief or officer exists, "the colonel of the state police or their designee shall act as the licensing authority" (§121, current text). The statute nowhere names selectmen; a select board qualifies only in the unusual case where it is the "board … having control of the police."

**Who makes the decision.** Not necessarily the chief personally. Current §131(f) speaks of "the licensing authority, or his designee" revoking or suspending, and the §121 definition builds in "persons authorized by them." A delegated licensing officer deciding applications is squarely within the statute.

**Pre-Bruen / pre-2024 standard.** The script's framing is correct: before the 2024 rewrite, LTC issuance was discretionary ("may issue") and the statute contained a "good reason" clause. What the script likely gets wrong is timing: the "good reason" language was not removed by Bruen — it remained in the statute until St. 2024 c. 135.

- Pre-2024 G.L. c. 140, §131(d) (text as amended by St. 2014 c. 284, §49, eff. Jan. 1, 2021; Wayback capture of malegislature.gov): "…which the licensing authority or the colonel may issue if it appears that the applicant is not a prohibited person … and that the applicant has good reason to fear injury to the applicant or the applicant's property or for any other reason…" (quote trimmed to the operative clause).
- Suitability discretion existed alongside it since St. 2014 c. 284: the licensing authority "may deny the application … if, in a reasonable exercise of discretion, the licensing authority determines that the applicant or licensee is unsuitable" (pre-2024 §131(d), same source).
- FID cards were different: since St. 1998 c. 180 the FID has been shall-issue — §129B(1) (1998 text): "which the licensing authority shall issue, unless the applicant:" followed by disqualifiers (https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/1998/Chapter180).

**What the statute says now.** St. 2024 c. 135 ("An Act modernizing firearm laws," signed July 25, 2024) rewrote §131(d) to shall-issue with a defined unsuitability standard:

- Source: G.L. c. 140, §131(d) (current), https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section131 (fetched 2026-09-25).
- Quote: "…which the licensing authority or the colonel shall issue if it appears that the applicant is neither a prohibited person nor determined to be unsuitable to be issued a license…"
- Unsuitability is now defined: it "shall be based on reliable, articulable and credible information that the applicant or licensee has exhibited or engaged in behavior that suggests that … the applicant or licensee may create a risk to public safety or a risk of danger to self or others," with written reasons and judicial review (§131(d), (f)).
- Enrolled act: St. 2024 c. 135, https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/2024/Chapter135.

**Correction line:** "The licensing authority is the chief of police, the board or officer having control of the police, or persons they authorize — often a designated licensing officer; and since St. 2024 c. 135, LTC issuance is 'shall issue' unless the applicant is prohibited or unsuitable, replacing the old 'may issue' and 'good reason' language."

## Claim 2 — Safe storage, G.L. c. 140, §131L

**Verdict: VERIFIED as to applicability and terms; VERIFIED with nuance as to license consequences.**

**Applies to every owner, children or not.** Yes. The duty is categorical and does not depend on minors in the home.

- Source: G.L. c. 140, §131L(a) (current), https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section131L (fetched 2026-09-25).
- Quote: "It shall be unlawful to store or keep any firearm in any place unless such firearm is secured in a locked container or equipped with a tamper-resistant mechanical lock or other safety device."
- The script's quoted terms are accurate. The under-control exemption reads: "For purposes of this section, such firearm shall not be deemed stored or kept if carried by or under the control of the owner or other lawfully authorized user" (§131L(a)).
- Minors matter only to penalty enhancement and evidentiary presumptions (§131L(c)–(e)), not to whether the duty exists.

**Penalties** (§131L(b)–(d); amounts identical in the pre-2024 and current texts):

- Non-large-capacity firearm: "a fine of not less than $1000 nor more than $7,500 or by imprisonment for not more than 1 1/2 years" (§131L(b)).
- Large capacity or semiautomatic weapon or machine gun: "a fine of not less than $2,000 nor more than $15,000 or by imprisonment for not less than 1 1/2 years nor more than 12 years" (§131L(b)).
- Where a person under 18 without an FID "may have access without committing an unforeseeable trespass": $2,500–$15,000 and 1 1/2–12 years (§131L(c)); for a large capacity weapon or machine gun accessible to a minor: $10,000–$20,000 and 4–15 years (§131L(d)).

**License consequences.** Section 131L itself contains no suspension/revocation clause — but a violation feeds directly into the licensing statutes:

- A §131L offense is a crime "regulating the use, possession, ownership, transfer, purchase, sale, lease, rental, receipt or transportation of weapons … for which a term of imprisonment may be imposed," so a **conviction** makes the holder a "prohibited person" under §131(d)(i)(D). Current §131(f): "A license issued under this section shall be revoked or suspended by the licensing authority … upon the occurrence of any event that would have disqualified the holder."
- For all license/card types, current §121F(u)(1): "A licensing authority shall revoke or suspend any license, card or permit … upon the occurrence of any event which makes the licensee, card or permit holder a prohibited person" and "may also revoke or suspend … upon satisfactory proof that the license, card or permit holder has violated or permitted any violation of this chapter" (https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section121F, fetched 2026-09-25). §131L is a section of chapter 140, so even absent a conviction the licensing authority may suspend or revoke on proof of the violation; on conviction it must.

**What St. 2024 c. 135 changed about storage.** Comparing the pre-amendment text (2024 Wayback capture of §131L) with the current official text: the penalty amounts are unchanged. The changes are: (i) terminology consolidation — the duty now reads on "any firearm," and post-2024 §121 defines "firearm" to include pistols, revolvers, rifles, shotguns, stun guns, and machine guns, replacing the old enumeration "any firearm, rifle or shotgun … or machine gun"; (ii) semiautomatic weapons were added to the higher penalty tier in §131L(b) ("a large capacity or semiautomatic weapon or machine gun"); (iii) the minor-access enhancement in §131L(c) was broadened from "a rifle or shotgun that is not a large capacity weapon" to any "firearm that is not a large capacity firearm or machine gun." The locked-container / tamper-resistant-lock rule, the owner's-control carve-out, and the stun-gun locked-container rule all predate c. 135.

**Correction line:** none needed for the duty itself; if the script implies §131L textually authorizes suspension, sharpen it to: "a storage conviction makes the owner a prohibited person, which compels revocation under §131(f) and §121F(u)."

## Claim 3 — Background checks and private transfers

### (a) Federal law

**Verdict: VERIFIED, with one caveat.**

- NICS duty applies to licensees only. 18 U.S.C. §922(t)(1): "a licensed importer, licensed manufacturer, or licensed dealer shall not transfer a firearm to any other person who is not licensed under this chapter, unless — (A) before the completion of the transfer, the licensee contacts the national instant criminal background check system…" (https://uscode.house.gov, current preliminary Title 18, fetched 2026-09-25).
- Form 4473 likewise binds only licensees. 27 CFR §478.124(a): "A licensed importer, licensed manufacturer, or licensed dealer shall not sell or otherwise dispose … of any firearm to any person, other than another licensee, unless the licensee records the transaction on a firearm transaction record, Form 4473" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-27/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-478/subpart-H/section-478.124, fetched 2026-09-25).
- ATF on unlicensed private sellers: "A person may transfer a firearm to an unlicensed resident of their state, provided the transferor does not know or have reasonable cause to believe the transferee is prohibited from receiving or possessing firearms under federal law" (ATF Q&A, "To whom may an unlicensed person transfer firearms under the GCA?", https://www.atf.gov/firearms/qa/whom-may-unlicensed-person-transfer-firearms-under-gca, fetched 2026-09-25).
- FBI confirms the buyer-facing process runs through dealers: "the seller, known as a Federal Firearms Licensee (FFL), contacts NICS" (FBI NICS page, via 2024 Wayback capture of https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/more-fbi-services-and-information/nics).

Caveat for the script: "not covered federally" is true only for genuinely occasional private sales. 18 U.S.C. §922(d) still bars **any** person from transferring to someone they know or have reason to believe is prohibited, and §922(a)(1)(A) makes it unlawful to "engage in the business" of dealing without a license — a line the 2022 BSCA and ATF's 2024 rule (89 FR 28968) moved, so repetitive for-profit private sellers can be federally required to become FFLs and run NICS.

### (b) Massachusetts private transfers

**Verdict on the quoted statement ("In Massachusetts a private transfer can only go to someone who already holds a license, and getting that license already requires a background check"): PARTLY VERIFIED — substantially right, but incomplete and slightly imprecise.**

What state law actually requires (all current text, fetched 2026-09-25):

- Seller eligibility and transferee eligibility: G.L. c. 140, §128A(b) — a licensed seller may privately transfer only to "(i) a person with a license to carry under section 131; (ii) an exempted person if permitted under section 129C; and (iii) a person with a firearm identification card under section 129B," with "no more than 4 firearm transfers … per calendar year." For anything other than a non-large-capacity, non-semiautomatic rifle or shotgun, an FID-holder transferee "shall also have a valid permit to purchase under section 131A" (§128A(b)(iii)). §131A is a real, still-current permit: "a licensing authority … may grant to such a person … a permit to purchase, rent or lease a firearm if it appears that such purchase, rental or lease is for a proper purpose."
- §129C exempts, among others, active military and police acting in their official capacity (§129C(f)) — so strictly, not every lawful private transferee "already holds a license."
- Reporting: all private transfers now run through the state's system **at or before the point of sale**, not after the fact. §128A(g): "All purchases, sales or transfers of a firearm permitted under this section shall, prior to or at the point of sale, be conducted through the electronic firearms registration system pursuant to section 121B," which "shall automatically review such information and display a message indicating whether the seller may proceed." Mass.gov, DCJIS FAQ: "Massachusetts law requires all residents to report any private sale or transfer of a firearm via the MIRCS Unified Gun Portal at or before the point of sale" (https://www.mass.gov/info-details/firearms-license-and-transaction-frequently-asked-questions, fetched 2026-09-25). The Firearms Records Bureau still exists as "the Commonwealth's repository for all firearms license and transaction data" (https://www.mass.gov/firearms-services, fetched 2026-09-25), and portal submissions generate the EFA10 record (https://www.mass.gov/how-to/record-a-private-firearms-sale-or-registration, fetched 2026-09-25). §128B separately requires registration of firearms obtained from any other source.
- The license behind the transfer does require a background check. §121F(d): the colonel of state police must search "statewide and nationwide criminal justice, warrant and protection order information systems and files including, but not limited to, the National Instant Criminal Background Check System" and certify in writing before any license, card, or permit issues; the licensing authority must also query DCJIS, probation, and mental health records (§121F(e)). §131(d) adds a personal interview for initial LTC applications. So every lawful private transferee has passed a state-run check more searching than a point-of-sale NICS call.

**Correction line:** "In Massachusetts a private transfer may go only to an LTC holder, an FID holder (who also needs a §131A permit to purchase for handguns and most other firearms), or a §129C-exempt person such as active-duty military or police; the sale is limited to four per year and must be run through the state's electronic firearms registration system at or before the point of sale — and every LTC/FID applicant has already passed a fingerprint-based state and NICS background check."

## Could not verify / caveats

- **Selectmen as licensing authority.** No primary source located naming selectmen; the statute's phrase is "the board or officer having control of the police." A select board would qualify only where it in fact controls the police. The script should not say selectmen issue licenses.
- **Enrolled text of St. 2024 c. 135 not read line-by-line.** The act's full enrolled text (https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/2024/Chapter135) is too large to fetch completely; the c. 135 changes described above were established by comparing the official pre-amendment and current General Laws texts (both malegislature.gov, the former via Wayback capture). Section-level pinpoint cites inside c. 135 were not verified.
- **FBI NICS page** was read through a 2024 Wayback capture of fbi.gov because the live site blocked automated fetches; substance matches 18 U.S.C. §922(t), which was read directly.
- **Post-Bruen administrative practice** (e.g., the 2022 AG/DCJIS guidance to chiefs on abandoning "good reason" and restrictions before the statute caught up) is not in any statute and was not verified here — it is a matter of guidance documents, not statutory text.
- The live §131L page's extraction garbled two words in subsection (b) ("in the case of a [firearm] that is not…"); the meaning is unambiguous from context and the pre-amendment text, but a clean pull of the current (b)–(d) wording from the printed Official Edition was not obtained.
