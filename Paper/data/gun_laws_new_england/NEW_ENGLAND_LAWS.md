# New England state gun laws — summary

Scope: the six New England states. Sources: Giffords Law Center state and policy pages,
state legislature records, and flagged tertiary sources where noted. Every policy statement
carries its URL; enactment years marked (tertiary) rest on Wikipedia and are recorded as
flags, per the repository's provenance rules. All pages accessed 2026-09-24/25.

## Massachusetts

Massachusetts requires a license (FID card or License to Carry) to possess any firearm, and
FID holders need an additional permit to purchase a handgun
([Giffords licensing](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/licensing/)).
It bans assault weapons by name list and by a features test
([Giffords assault weapons](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/hardware-ammunition/assault-weapons/)),
limits magazines to 10 rounds
([Giffords magazines](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/hardware-ammunition/large-capacity-magazines/)),
requires background checks on all sales including private sales
([Giffords universal background checks](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/universal-background-checks/)),
has an extreme risk protection order law (enacted 2018, tertiary:
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_law); existence confirmed on the
[Giffords ERPO page](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/risk-based-gun-removal-and-disarmament/extreme-risk-protection-orders/)),
has no statutory waiting period
([Giffords waiting periods](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/waiting-periods/)),
and requires a permit to carry
([Giffords concealed carry](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/guns-in-public/concealed-carry/)).
Recent major change: H.4885, "An Act modernizing firearm laws," signed by the Governor on
July 25, 2024 as Chapter 135 of the Acts of 2024
([Massachusetts Legislature bill record](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/193/H4885)); the law
converts the assault-weapon ban to an "assault-style firearm" definition, adds ghost-gun
serialization rules and a registration system, and a veto referendum is scheduled for the
November 2026 ballot ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Massachusetts_Firearm_Regulations_Referendum_(2026)), tertiary).

## Connecticut

Connecticut requires a permit or eligibility certificate to purchase handguns and long guns
([Giffords licensing](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/licensing/)).
It bans assault weapons by name list and by a one-feature test, in force since 1993
([Giffords assault weapons](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/hardware-ammunition/assault-weapons/)),
limits magazines to 10 rounds (pre-ban magazines allowed if registered)
([Giffords magazines](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/hardware-ammunition/large-capacity-magazines/)),
requires background checks on all sales (2013)
([Giffords universal background checks](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/universal-background-checks/)),
and operates the nation's oldest risk-warrant law (1999, tertiary:
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_law); only law enforcement and state
officials may petition, per
[Giffords ERPO](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/risk-based-gun-removal-and-disarmament/extreme-risk-protection-orders/)).
It has no statutory waiting period
([Giffords waiting periods](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/waiting-periods/))
and requires a permit to carry
([Giffords concealed carry](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/guns-in-public/concealed-carry/)).
Recent major change: Public Act 23-53 (2023) expanded the assault-weapons ban, restricted
open carry, raised the purchase age for semiautomatic rifles, and capped handgun purchases
at three per 30 days ([CT News Junkie](https://ctnewsjunkie.com/2023/06/06/lamont-signs-bill-expanding-gun-regulations/)).

## Rhode Island

Rhode Island requires a pistol/revolver safety certificate to purchase a handgun
([Giffords licensing](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/licensing/)).
It enacted an assault-weapons ban in June 2025 (S.359, Chapter 281) covering sale, transfer,
and manufacture, effective July 1, 2026
([Congressional Sportsmen's Foundation](https://congressionalsportsmen.org/news/update-rhode-island-state-so-called-assault-weapon-ban-signed-by-governor/);
[Giffords assault weapons](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/hardware-ammunition/assault-weapons/)).
It limits magazines to 10 rounds (2022), requires background checks on all sales (1990),
imposes a seven-day waiting period on all firearm purchases, and requires a permit to carry
([Giffords magazines](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/hardware-ammunition/large-capacity-magazines/);
[Giffords universal background checks](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/universal-background-checks/);
[Giffords waiting periods](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/waiting-periods/);
[Giffords concealed carry](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/guns-in-public/concealed-carry/)).
It has an extreme risk protection order law (2018, tertiary:
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_law); petitioners limited to law
enforcement and state officials, per
[Giffords ERPO](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/risk-based-gun-removal-and-disarmament/extreme-risk-protection-orders/)).

## New Hampshire

New Hampshire requires no permit to purchase, bans no assault weapons, limits no magazines,
has no universal background check requirement, no extreme risk protection order law, and no
waiting period; each of these appears under "What New Hampshire Is Missing" on the
[Giffords New Hampshire page](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/states/new-hampshire/).
It adopted permitless carry in 2017 (SB 12)
([Giffords concealed carry](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/guns-in-public/concealed-carry/)).
No major legislative change in 2022–2026 could be verified; Giffords states that New
Hampshire "has not passed any meaningful gun safety laws in years."

## Vermont

Vermont requires no permit to purchase and bans no assault weapons
([Giffords Vermont page](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/states/vermont/)).
It limits magazines to 10 rounds for long guns and 15 for handguns (2018, S.55; pre-April
2018 magazines exempt) and requires background checks on private sales (2018, S.55)
([Giffords magazines](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/hardware-ammunition/large-capacity-magazines/);
[Giffords universal background checks](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/universal-background-checks/)).
It has an extreme risk protection order law (2018, tertiary:
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_law); existence confirmed on the
[Giffords ERPO page](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/risk-based-gun-removal-and-disarmament/extreme-risk-protection-orders/)).
It has a 72-hour waiting period, enacted by H.230 in June 2023, which also added safe-home
storage and expanded risk-protection orders
([Vermont Public](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2023-06-02/vermont-gun-bill-including-72-hour-waiting-period-becomes-law-phil-scott);
[Giffords waiting periods](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/waiting-periods/)).
Vermont has never required a carry permit in the modern era; the adoption date is UNVERIFIED
([Giffords concealed carry](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/guns-in-public/concealed-carry/)).

## Maine

Maine requires no permit to purchase, bans no assault weapons, and limits no magazines
([Giffords Maine page](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/states/maine/)).
Its background-check requirement (expanded 2024) covers sales at gun shows and advertised
private sales; Giffords lists "true universal background checks" as missing
([Giffords universal background checks](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/universal-background-checks/)).
It enacted a 72-hour waiting period in 2024
([Giffords waiting periods](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/gun-sales/waiting-periods/)).
Voters approved an extreme risk protection order ballot initiative on November 4, 2025,
adding to the 2020 "yellow flag" law; the effective date is UNVERIFIED
([Giffords Maine page](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/states/maine/); years
tertiary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_law)).
Maine adopted permitless carry in 2015
([Giffords concealed carry](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/policy-areas/guns-in-public/concealed-carry/)).
Recent major changes: the 2024 post-Lewiston package (expanded background checks, 72-hour
waiting period) and 2025 measures (ERPO ballot initiative, ghost-gun prohibition effective
2027) ([Giffords Maine page](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/gun-laws/states/maine/)).
