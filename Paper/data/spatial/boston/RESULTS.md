## Validation: primary incidents vs the dedicated victimization dataset

The dedicated Analyze Boston "Shootings" dataset counts victimizations (one row per victim, fatal and non-fatal) and carries no coordinates. The primary measure counts incidents, including the small share without valid coordinates. The two series agree within the 15% threshold in every year from 2019 onward; the 2015–2018 shortfall is the pre-2019 under-flagging regime and is flagged in the table:

Within the main window the largest absolute difference is -10.2% in 2024. Years beyond the 15% threshold: 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018 — all pre-2019, where the primary measure reads -60–-34% relative to the dedicated dataset. That direction matches the under-flagging regime documented in DATA_LOG.md and is why those years stay out of the main results. Incident counts can sit above victimization rows when one incident has no victim recorded in the dedicated dataset, and below when one incident carries several victims; the two accounting units are expected to differ by a few percent.

## Windows

- Main window: **2019–2025** (seven complete calendar years inside the consistent flagging regime; the 2018→2019 jump in flagged rows is a recording-practice change, so 2015–2018 stay out of the main results).
- Pooled sensitivity: **2016–2025**. The plan asked for a 2015–2025 pooled sensitivity and also for partial years dropped from rates; the 2015 file begins in June, so the pooled window starts in 2016.
- Recent sensitivity: **2021–2025** (five most recent complete years).
- Partial years 2015 and 2026 are excluded from all rates.

## Geography and denominators

Suffolk County contains Boston plus Chelsea, Revere, and Winthrop. Boston tracts are the 208 tracts numbered below 1600 plus the 98xx/99xx special tracts (ACS 2024 5-year population 666,442); tract numbers 1601–1899 are the three neighboring municipalities (27 tracts, population 118,679). Zero flagged shootings fall in the non-Boston tracts, and the municipal boundaries are water boundaries, so the classification is clean. Grade populations come from areal weighting: each tract's population is distributed into HOLC polygons in proportion to intersected area, which assumes uniform density within tracts. Tracts are small relative to HOLC areas in Boston, so the error from this assumption is limited to boundary slivers. The unmapped category's area includes harbor water carried in the special tracts, which dilutes its per-km² rate; the per-capita rates are the informative column.

## Ungraded and unmapped shares

The 1938 HOLC map grades 91.0 km² of Boston's 280 km² tract extent. In 2019–2025, 8% of primary shootings fell in ungraded (Commercial/Industrial) or unmapped territory, home to 20% of the population. The unmapped areas are outside the survey footprint; they carry no 1938 grade, so nothing about redlining follows from their rates.

## Tract-level regression (robustness)

Poisson regression of tract primary-shooting counts (2019–2025, n=199 Boston tracts, 1,193 shootings assigned; 9 tracts with zero population or missing controls dropped) on the tract's HOLC exposure — the share of tract area graded C or D — with log(population) offset.

| model | IRR for C+D share | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poisson: share C+D alone | 6.378 | 4.918–8.272 | p<0.0001 |
| Poisson: + poverty rate, pct Black | 1.833 | 1.411–2.383 | p<0.0001 |
| Negative binomial (moment alpha): + controls | 1.0 | 0.683–1.464 | p=0.9987 |

Pearson overdispersion is 23.16 (uncontrolled) and 30.11 (controlled), so the Poisson standard errors understate the uncertainty; the negative-binomial row (iterated moment-estimated alpha=0.3185) carries the appropriate standard errors. Under that model the C+D-share coefficient is null (IRR 1.0, 95% CI 0.683–1.464, p=0.9987): at tract level, the HOLC-exposure coefficient did not survive the poverty and percent-Black controls. In the controlled Poisson model the poverty-rate coefficient is 3.403 (p<0.0001) and the percent-Black coefficient is 3.368 (p<0.0001). Moran's I of the controlled model's deviance residuals is 0.238 (permutation p=0.0010, queen contiguity, 1 island tract connected to its nearest neighbor): residual spatial clustering remains after the controls, so the tract model leaves spatial structure unexplained. The grade-level tables above are the descriptive evidence; the tract regression does not separate 1938 grade from the poverty and racial composition that followed it.

## What the data support

In 2019–2025, Boston recorded 1,197 shooting-victimization incidents, of which 1,197 carry valid coordinates and enter the geographic rates. The annual rate in D-graded areas was 3.135 per 10,000 residents, against 0.43 in A- and B-graded areas combined: a rate ratio of 7.36 (95% CI 4.07–14.86). C-graded areas sit at the same level, rate ratio 7.33 (4.07–14.75); the C and D rates are statistically indistinguishable from each other, and both sit roughly seven times the A+B rate. The gap holds in the pooled 2016–2025 window, in the 2021–2025 window, in both sub-periods, under the secondary shots-fired measure, and with the densest tract cluster removed. At tract level the HOLC-exposure coefficient does not survive poverty and percent-Black controls once the heavy overdispersion is modeled, so the supported statement is descriptive: present-day shooting victimizations remain concentrated in the areas the 1938 HOLC survey graded C and D.

## Interpretive limit

An association between 1938 HOLC grade and present-day shooting counts does not separate redlining from the poverty, disinvestment, and lead exposure that followed it. The manuscript's own account treats those channels as the mechanism, so this finding reads as persistence of concentration. Isolated causation is not claimed.
