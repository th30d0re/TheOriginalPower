# TT-OOS-01 — Results

**Registration:** `Paper/prereg/tt-oos-01-suppression-conservation.md`, commit `d032213`,
committed before the analysis script was written and before the data was examined.
**Executed:** 2026-08-09, `Paper/scripts/tt_oos_01_sensitivity.py`
**Data:** `Paper/data/congressional_record_word_freq.csv`, `class_share`, 1965–2024, n = 60.

## Verdict as registered

| | Prediction | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | `ln\|Shat(f)\|` changes sign across the band | **HOLDS** — 8 bins suppressed, 7 amplified |
| **P2** | `integral ln\|Shat\| df > 0` | **FAILS** — point estimate `-0.1465` |
| **P3** | carrier power falls, log-integral conserved | **FAILS** — carrier power *rose*, `Δ ln P = +0.699` |

## P1 holds, and it is the robust finding

The frequency redistribution is large, monotone in frequency, and reproduces in every cut
attempted — the registered periodogram split at 1994, Welch at the same split, and Welch at
alternative splits 1990 and 2000. Every run gives the same shape:

    amplified   : 20 yr, 10 yr, 6.7 yr, 5 yr        (+0.15 .. +1.46)
    suppressed  : 3.3 yr, 2.9 yr, 2.5 yr, 2.2 yr, 2.0 yr   (-0.15 .. -3.17)
    crossover   : consistently between 4.0 and 3.3 yr

Per-bin swings reach ±2.5 in `ln|Shat|` while the integral over the band stays within ±0.2 of
zero. Very large redistribution, near-total cancellation. That is the shape a conservation law
makes.

## P2 fails as registered, and the test is underpowered

Point estimates across all four cuts: `-0.147, -0.123, -0.069, -0.194`. All negative, all small.

Circular block bootstrap, 4000 reps, 5-year blocks, seed 20260809:

    observed integral      : -0.1465
    95% CI                 : [-0.3573, +0.5061]
    P(integral > 0)        : 0.660

**The confidence interval contains zero and is wide enough to contain all three competing
hypotheses**: net-amplifying (`> 0`, the registered prediction), conserved (`= 0`, open-loop
stable), and net-suppressing (`< 0`). With 30 points per epoch this test cannot separate them.

P2 is recorded as a failure because that is what was registered. The honest description is a
**null**, not a refutation. The extraction-surcharge half of the theorem remains untested rather
than disproved, and testing it needs either a longer series or a higher-frequency one.

## P3 fails on direction, not on power

The registered prediction had the carrier band being suppressed. The data has it amplified:
class-coherence power *grew* in the 4–6 yr band while collapsing below 3 yr. The sign is wrong,
and no amount of statistical power rescues it. This is a straightforward miss.

## Post-hoc observation — NOT a result, and the seed of TT-OOS-02

The direction of the miss is interesting and must not be reported as a confirmation of anything.
The architecture did not suppress class coherence at the electoral frequency. It moved coherence
*into* that band and out of the fast spontaneous timescales below three years.

`thm:quarter-wave` states that a formation sitting on the carrier is converted from short to open
by a `lambda/4` displacement. A regime that entrains coherence onto the carrier puts the load
exactly where the rotation can act on it. Entrainment-then-rotation is a sharper mechanism than
suppression, and the present data is consistent with it.

This observation is post-hoc, was generated by looking at the failure, and carries no evidential
weight. It becomes a result only if registered as TT-OOS-02 and tested on data not used here.

## What this settles about the Theodore Transform

The transform produced a structural prediction — frequency-domain redistribution of coherence with
a specific waterbed shape — that appears nowhere in the historical chapters, was registered before
the data was examined, and then survived four independent cuts of that data. That is one genuine
out-of-sample structural hit.

It did not deliver the magnitude claim, and one of its three directional predictions was wrong.
The appendix's §Use II can now cite a registered prediction rather than four retrodictions, and it
must cite the failures in the same breath.
