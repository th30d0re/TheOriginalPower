## Part A: the left-handed curve
L1. Halpern & Coren (1988 Nature; 1991 NEJM) reported that left-handers die earlier (the "nine
    years" claim) from the decline of left-handedness across age groups. What exactly did they
    report and how?
L2. The rebuttals: was the curve an artifact of cohort effects, especially forced conversion of
    older cohorts (e.g. Harris 1993; Salive et al. 1993; Hugdahl et al.; Ellis et al.; the
    Gilbert & Wysocki 1992 National Geographic smell-survey handedness-by-age data)? What is the
    current consensus on left-handed mortality?
L3. Does the cohort decline in left-handedness line up with the documented end of forced
    conversion (e.g. Guber 2019 Germany cohorts; US/UK twentieth-century practice)?

## Part B: height
Context: the manuscript's framework classifies an axis by (1) friction from a design default,
(2) whether a partition is enforced, (3) whether anyone collects. Test height.
T1. Height wage premium: size and robustness for men and for women (e.g. Persico, Postlewaite &
    Silverman 2004; Case & Paxson 2008; Judge & Cable 2004 meta-analysis; later work). Is the
    premium explained by cognitive ability, adolescent height, or discrimination?
T2. Leadership and elections: are taller candidates more likely to win US presidential elections
    or be chosen as leaders (e.g. Stulp et al. 2013 Leadership Quarterly; CEO height studies)?
    Effect sizes and criticism.
T3. Gendered asymmetry: is height an advantage for men and a neutral or penalized trait for
    women past some point? Look at wages, leadership, and mate-preference research (e.g. the
    male-taller norm; Stulp et al. 2013 on height preferences; Sohn; others). Is there evidence
    that tall women are penalized because height reads as challenging a masculine role? Report
    exactly what the evidence supports and what it does not.
T4. Design default: are built environments, products, vehicles, or safety equipment designed
    around a male reference height/body (e.g. crash-test dummies, PPE, workstation standards)?
    Who pays the cost (short people, women)?
T5. Collector: does anyone systematically collect from the height partition (employers,
    markets), or is it a selection bias without a beneficiary?
