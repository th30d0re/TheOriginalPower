### SEG 01 — Bias, prejudice, discrimination: three constructs, three citations (est. 7 min)

**Purpose.** Supply the per-term precision the manuscript leaves lumped. Chapter 2 defines
"prejudice or bias" in a single entry — "individual attitudes, stereotypes, or discriminatory
behaviors based on group membership" [book §2.2, L1865] — and discrimination never receives a
standalone definition anywhere in the chapter. YT02 gives each construct its own definition
from the established social-psychology literature.

**Key claims.**

- **Bias:** a leaning for or against that can operate outside awareness; implicit bias as
  introspectively unidentified traces of past experience that mediate favorable or unfavorable
  feeling, thought, or action toward social objects [external: Greenwald, A. G., & Banaji,
  M. R. (1995). "Implicit social cognition: Attitudes, self-esteem, and stereotypes."
  *Psychological Review*, 102(1), 4–27]. Optional measurement note: the Implicit Association
  Test [external: Greenwald, A. G., McGhee, D. E., & Schwartz, J. L. K. (1998). "Measuring
  individual differences in implicit cognition: The implicit association test." *Journal of
  Personality and Social Psychology*, 74(6), 1464–1480].
- **Prejudice:** an antipathy based on a faulty and inflexible generalization, felt or
  expressed, directed at a group as a whole or at an individual because of membership in that
  group [external: Allport, G. W. (1954). *The Nature of Prejudice*. Addison-Wesley, p. 9].
- **Discrimination:** the behavioral component — differential treatment of persons by group
  membership. Name the tripartite partition: stereotype as the cognitive component, prejudice
  as the affective component, discrimination as the behavioral component [external: Fiske,
  S. T. (1998). "Stereotyping, prejudice, and discrimination." In D. T. Gilbert, S. T. Fiske,
  & G. Lindzey (Eds.), *The Handbook of Social Psychology* (4th ed., Vol. 2, pp. 357–411).
  McGraw-Hill].
- The book assigns all three constructs to one interpersonal class, direction-free between
  any groups, and racism to a separate systemic class [book §2.2, L1865–L1897]. The external
  definitions sharpen the interpersonal class. The categorical boundary the book draws stays
  intact.
- Stated on air or in the description: none of the three anchor works appears in
  `Paper/references.bib` [references.bib audit, this session]; they enter the project's corpus
  through this video.

**Equations/figures.** None. On-screen citation cards for each anchor.

## Hypothesis and test

**The hypothesis, as stated on air (SEG 05).** "Elite economic interests produce systemic
racialization, and systemic racialization cultivates interpersonal prejudice." [book Eq 2.6]

**Predictions.**

- **P1 — Institutional production.** Racial ideology appears after or alongside the extraction
  arrangement, produced by identifiable institutions acting for the arrangement's
  beneficiaries. Tested in SEG 06 (the Zurara commission, 1453; the Casa dos Escravos, 1486;
  the papal bulls, 1452/1455).
- **P2 — Codification follows solidarity threats.** Law hardens the racial partition after
  cross-racial labor coherence spikes. Tested in SEG 07 (the 1640 → 1662 → 1676 → 1691 → 1705
  sequence) and SEG 08 (Nat Turner 1831 → the legislative tightening).
- **P3 — Maintenance by cultivation.** Once codified, the system manufactures and refreshes
  interpersonal prejudice through its update channels, and public political language tracks
  the cost of explicit appeal. Tested in SEG 08 (the interface swap; Atwater; the coded-policy
  record).
- **P4 — Suppression scales with extraction.** Enforcement expenditure tracks extraction
  output. Tested in SEG 08 (the antebellum suppression ratio, 2.70/2.88/2.75% across a 233%
  revenue expansion).

**Refutation standards, announced on air in SEG 05.**

- **R1.** A durable phenotype-keyed caste system arising without elite sponsorship, or
  preceding the extraction arrangement it serves, would damage the hypothesis. [Outline-level
  operationalization of the Eq 2.5/Eq 2.6 contest; author review requested — see Open
  questions.]
- **R2.** The book's own case-level falsifiers: a sustained period of rising cotton revenue
  with flat or falling militia and patrol expenditure [book §2.3.2]; a simultaneous post-1965
  rise in union density, fall in incarceration, and fall in top-decile wealth concentration
  [book §2.3.6 case study].
- **R3.** The vector definition's stated failure condition: a group's prejudice, backed by
  that group's own control of police, lawmaking, and capital, flowing steadily down onto white
  Americans [MANUSCRIPT_GAP §1 — flagged there as needing author confirmation or replacement].

**Where each test lands.** SEG 06 carries P1. SEG 07 carries P2. SEG 08 carries P2 (second
instance), P3, and P4, and runs the R2 checks explicitly. R1 and R3 are stated in SEG 05 and
answered in SEG 09's synthesis.
