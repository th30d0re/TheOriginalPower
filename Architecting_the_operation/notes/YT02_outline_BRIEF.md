# Brief: outline for YT02, a standalone long-form "what racism is" video

**Never run git. Never edit anything under `Paper/`. Write one file only:
`Architecting_the_operation/notes/YT02_outline.md`.**

## What the author wants

YT01 maps one-to-one onto Chapter 2 and stays that way. YT02 is a separate long-form video
that explains what racism is, drawing on the whole book and compressing it. Its order:

1. **Open where Chapter 2 opens.** Show the conventional dictionary hierarchy: individual
   prejudice or discrimination first, belief in racial superiority second, systemic racism
   third (if present at all).
2. **Define the terms the book leaves undefined.** The book lumps "prejudice or bias" into a
   single definition (`Paper/The_Original_Power.tex`, around line 1865). YT02 must give bias,
   prejudice and discrimination each a precise definition of its own, from established
   social-psychology sources: Allport 1954 for prejudice; the stereotype/prejudice/
   discrimination split (cognitive/affective/behavioral, e.g. Fiske 1998); and implicit bias
   (Greenwald & Banaji 1995). Check `Paper/references.bib` for which ones the book already
   cites. Tag each definition [external] with its full citation.
3. **Define a system by its characteristics**, as you would any system: components, boundary,
   inputs and outputs, purpose, feedback. Then show that racism has each one.
4. **Define the In-group and the Out-group**, then bring in set theory. Treat these groups as
   sets and walk through the set equations from Chapter 2.
5. **Mark the point of departure.** Up to here, no viewer should disagree. The causal order is
   where the framework departs. The common causal story (prejudice → discrimination → systemic
   outcomes) follows the order in which dictionaries define racism, and that order contradicts
   the historical record. That inconsistency produces the misdiagnosis.
6. **State the reversal as a hypothesis.** Present the reversed arrow (Elite economic
   interests → systemic racialization → interpersonal prejudice) as a testable claim. Say what
   evidence would support or refute it, tell the viewer it will be tested, and test it later
   in the video against the historical record: Portugal in the 15th century, Bacon's
   Rebellion and the 1705 codes, and the other case studies in the book.

## Output: `YT02_outline.md`

- Segment list in order. For each segment: a working title, its purpose (1–2 sentences), the
  key claims with provenance tags ([book §/eq/fig] or [external: citation]), the equations and
  figures it uses, and an estimated length.
- A "hypothesis and test" section: the hypothesis as stated on air, the predictions, which
  historical cases test it, and where in the video each test lands.
- A "compression" section: which book material is in, which is cut or deferred to other
  episodes, and why.
- A short list of open questions for the author.

## Rules

- Direct declarative sentences. No formulaic antithesis ("not X but Y", "more than just X").
- College/graduate register; name the constructs.
- Call the five positions "tiers", never "jobs".
- Ground every claim in the book or in a cited external source. Never invent axes, figures or
  equation numbers.
