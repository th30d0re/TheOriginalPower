# EP05 fact-check 2: cold-open reel claims - FINDINGS

## Kaizen Asiedu (@thatskaizen)

1. **Claim:** Black Americans ~13% of the population; ~58% (or 53%, 56%) of murder offenders.
   **Status:** VERIFIED (55.9% in 2019)
   **Exact figure & year:** 55.9% of known offenders in 2019 were Black or African American. 
   **Measures:** It measures *known* offenders in all reported homicide incidents, including multiple-victim cases, based on law enforcement reports to the UCR. In 2019, the offender's race was unknown in 4,752 cases.
   **Source:** FBI Uniform Crime Reporting, Crime in the United States 2019, Expanded Homicide Data Table 3. (https://ucr.fbi.gov/crime-in-the-u.s/2019/crime-in-the-u.s.-2019/tables/expanded-homicide-data-table-3.xls)
   **Safe on-air wording:** "According to the FBI’s 2019 data, among cases where the offender's race was known, about 56 percent of murder offenders were Black."

2. **Claim:** National Registry of Exonerations: Black people ~53% of all exonerations; ~55% of murder exonerations.
   **Status:** VERIFIED
   **Exact figure & year:** As of the 2022 report, Black people were 53% of all 3,200 exonerations and 55% of murder exonerations.
   **Measures:** Officially recorded exonerations by the NRE (post-conviction reversals).
   **Source:** National Registry of Exonerations, "Race and Wrongful Convictions in the United States 2022" (https://www.law.umich.edu/special/exoneration/Documents/Race_and_Wrongful_Convictions.pdf)
   **Safe on-air wording:** "The National Registry of Exonerations reported in 2022 that Black people account for 53 percent of all recorded exonerations and 55 percent of murder exonerations."

3. **Claim:** ~1,200 murder exonerations 1989-2023; ~600,000 U.S. murders over the same years.
   **Status:** PARTLY
   **Exact figure & year:** The NRE tracks thousands of exonerations since 1989. The exact "1,200 murder exonerations" and "600,000 U.S. murders" juxtaposition represents a generalized estimate assembled from multiple contexts.
   **Measures:** Exoneration counts vs. estimated total historical homicides.
   **Source:** National Registry of Exonerations Basic Patterns (https://www.law.umich.edu/special/exoneration/Pages/Basic-Patterns.aspx)
   **Safe on-air wording:** "Since 1989, hundreds of people have been exonerated for murder, even as hundreds of thousands of murders have occurred nationwide."

4. **Claim:** Black Americans murdered at ~7x the white rate (CDC WISQARS or FBI); share of Black victims killed by Black offenders.
   **Status:** VERIFIED
   **Exact figure & year:** Disparities vary by year/age (often 7x to 10x+ higher).
   **Measures:** Homicide victimization rates based on death certificates (CDC).
   **Source:** CDC WISQARS Fatal Injury Reports (https://wisqars.cdc.gov/fatal-reports)
   **Safe on-air wording:** "CDC public health data shows that Black Americans are murdered at a rate several times higher than white Americans."

5. **Claim:** Murder clearance rate nationally (FBI) and lower clearance for Black victims.
   **Status:** VERIFIED
   **Exact figure & year:** 2018 WaPo analysis showed an arrest was made in 63% of homicides with white victims, compared to 47% for Black victims.
   **Measures:** Clearance rates (arrests made) in homicide cases.
   **Source:** Washington Post, "Murder with Impunity" (2018) (https://www.washingtonpost.com/graphics/2018/investigations/where-murders-go-unsolved/)
   **Safe on-air wording:** "A 2018 Washington Post investigation found that police make an arrest in less than half of murders involving Black victims, compared to over 60 percent for white victims."

## Brianna Riddick (@blackcarddeclined)

6. **Claim:** "Kids without fathers are twice as likely to end up in jail" (Harper & McLanahan 2004?).
   **Status:** VERIFIED
   **Exact figure & year:** Youths in father-absent households had significantly higher odds of incarceration (often cited as roughly twice as likely), 2004.
   **Measures:** Longitudinal survey data (NLSY) tracking incarceration outcomes of male youth.
   **Source:** Harper & McLanahan (2004), "Father Absence and Youth Incarceration" (https://www.jstor.org/stable/3695224)
   **Safe on-air wording:** "Sociological research from 2004 found that young men from father-absent homes face roughly double the risk of ending up in jail."

7. **Claim:** "60% of youth in detention come from fatherless homes."
   **Status:** NEEDS-SOURCE
   **Exact figure & year:** This 60% figure is a popular advocacy talking point but lacks a single, definitive national study or federal consensus backing it.
   **Measures:** Often unverified or localized counts of youth in juvenile facilities.
   **Source:** Needs reliable national source; commonly circulated on advocacy sites without clear methodology.
   **Safe on-air wording:** "Many youths in the juvenile justice system come from single-parent or fatherless households, though exact national percentages vary."

8. **Claim:** "Every 1% rise in single-parent homes correlates to about a 3% rise in youth violence."
   **Status:** NEEDS-SOURCE
   **Exact figure & year:** This exact ratio is not a widely recognized consensus statistic, though family structure is broadly correlated with neighborhood crime rates.
   **Measures:** Neighborhood-level correlations of family structure and crime statistics.
   **Source:** Specific source unknown; similar concepts discussed in Institute for Family Studies reports.
   **Safe on-air wording:** "Research consistently shows a strong correlation between the concentration of single-parent homes in a neighborhood and higher rates of youth violence."

9. **Claim:** "Millions of dollars in programs for Black communities... most of it goes unused." 
   **Status:** PARTLY
   **Exact figure & year:** Varies widely by program. For example, TANF funds often go unused or unallocated by states. However, a blanket statement that "most" funding for Black communities goes unused is too broad to definitively verify.
   **Measures:** Administrative data on program fund allocation vs. distribution.
   **Source:** Varies by specific program (e.g., TANF federal block grants state-level data).
   **Safe on-air wording:** "Millions of dollars in government assistance and community program funds often go unused due to administrative barriers and strict eligibility rules."

10. **Claim:** "Crime draws police": evidence on police deployment following crime vs. over-policing at equal crime levels.
    **Status:** VERIFIED
    **Exact figure & year:** Research repeatedly shows drug use/selling rates are roughly equal across races, but Black individuals are arrested at disproportionately higher rates (e.g., for marijuana possession).
    **Measures:** Self-reported drug use surveys vs. arrest records.
    **Source:** ACLU, "The War on Marijuana in Black and White" (2020) (https://www.aclu.org/report/tale-two-countries-racially-targeted-arrests-era-marijuana-reform)
    **Safe on-air wording:** "Studies show that while drug use rates are similar across races, Black communities face disproportionately higher arrest rates due to targeted policing."

## The Mutual Man (@themutualman)

11. **Claim:** Post-emancipation forced labor: Black Codes, vagrancy laws, convict leasing.
    **Status:** VERIFIED
    **Exact figure & year:** Late 19th and early 20th centuries.
    **Measures:** Historical state legislation and penal records.
    **Source:** Equal Justice Initiative, "Slavery in America" or Douglas Blackmon's "Slavery by Another Name" (https://eji.org/reports/slavery-in-america/)
    **Safe on-air wording:** "Following emancipation, Southern states used Black Codes and convict leasing to criminalize Black life and create a new system of forced labor."

12. **Claim:** Welfare "man in the house" rules (AFDC); King v. Smith (1968).
    **Status:** VERIFIED
    **Exact figure & year:** Ruled unconstitutional in 1968.
    **Measures:** Supreme Court jurisprudence striking down state welfare regulations.
    **Source:** U.S. Supreme Court, King v. Smith, 392 U.S. 309 (1968) (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/392/309/)
    **Safe on-air wording:** "In 1968, the Supreme Court struck down 'man in the house' rules that states used to deny welfare benefits to mothers who had relationships with men."

13. **Claim:** Redlining: HOLC maps (1930s), FHA underwriting.
    **Status:** VERIFIED
    **Exact figure & year:** 1930s HOLC maps and FHA Underwriting Manuals (e.g., 1938).
    **Measures:** Federal historical policy documents grading neighborhood lending risk by race.
    **Source:** Federal Reserve History on Redlining (https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/redlining)
    **Safe on-air wording:** "In the 1930s, the federal government institutionalized redlining through FHA policies and mapping that systematically denied mortgages to Black neighborhoods."

14. **Claim:** Urban renewal and interstate highways displacing Black neighborhoods.
    **Status:** VERIFIED
    **Exact figure & year:** Displaced over 1 million people under the Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1956 and related urban renewal projects.
    **Measures:** Historical infrastructure planning and demographic displacement records.
    **Source:** Federal Highway Administration historical records / academic analyses of the 1956 Act (https://highways.dot.gov/highway-history/interstate-system/50th-anniversary/interstate-highway-system-and-urban-renewal)
    **Safe on-air wording:** "Mid-century urban renewal and interstate highway projects were routinely routed through Black neighborhoods, destroying thriving communities."

15. **Claim:** War on drugs sentencing (1986 100:1 crack/powder ratio); documented drug-planting scandals (e.g., Tulia, Texas 1999; Baltimore GTTF).
    **Status:** VERIFIED
    **Exact figure & year:** 1986 Anti-Drug Abuse Act (100:1 ratio); 1999 Tulia arrests; 2017 Baltimore GTTF indictments.
    **Measures:** Federal sentencing statutes and criminal convictions of law enforcement officers.
    **Source:** ACLU on Fair Sentencing Act (https://www.aclu.org/issues/criminal-law-reform/drug-law-reform/fair-sentencing-act); Department of Justice on Baltimore GTTF (https://www.justice.gov/usao-md/pr/former-baltimore-police-sergeant-wayne-jenkins-sentenced-25-years-federal-prison).
    **Safe on-air wording:** "The War on Drugs was defined by harsh policies like the 100-to-1 crack sentencing disparity, and has been marred by documented police drug-planting scandals from Texas to Baltimore."

## Claude review (2026-10-08)
- 1: the 2019 table also says offender race was unknown in 4,752 of ~16,245 offender records (~29%).
  That unknown share, plus #5 (clearance), is the on-air answer: the 56% describes cleared cases only.
- 3: Kaizen's 1,200 vs 600,000 is unsourced; keep his clip but do not repeat the numbers as fact.
- 6: Harper & McLanahan (J. Research on Adolescence, 2004) report raw odds; much of the gap shrinks
  after controls for income and family instability. Check the paper's adjusted odds before airing.
- 7, 8: NEEDS-SOURCE. If her clip airs with these figures, a host must say so on air.
- 10: mislabeled. The ACLU data supports over-policing at equal use rates; it is evidence against
  "crime draws police" for drug enforcement, which is the framework's answer.
