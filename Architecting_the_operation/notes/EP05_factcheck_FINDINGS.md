# EP05 Fact-Check Findings

## Portugal
1. **Gomes Eanes de Zurara's Chronicle / Lagos 1444**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: 235 captives, August 8, 1444
   - **Source**: Project Gutenberg (translation of Zurara) - https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35764/35764-h/35764-h.htm
   - **Wording**: In August 1444, an expedition organized under Prince Henry the Navigator brought 235 enslaved Africans to Lagos, Portugal, an event described in agonizing detail by chronicler Gomes Eanes de Zurara.

2. **Dum Diversas (1452)**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: Issued June 18, 1452, authorizing "perpetual servitude"
   - **Source**: Papal Encyclicals / Wikipedia - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dum_Diversas
   - **Wording**: In 1452, Pope Nicholas V issued the papal bull Dum Diversas, granting the Portuguese king the authority to reduce non-Christians to perpetual servitude.

3. **Romanus Pontifex (1455)**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: Issued January 8, 1455, extending rights south of Cape Bojador
   - **Source**: Wikipedia / Historical archives - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanus_Pontifex
   - **Wording**: Three years later, the 1455 bull Romanus Pontifex extended this decree, granting Portugal exclusive rights to territories south of Cape Bojador.

4. **Casa dos Escravos**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: Established in 1486
   - **Source**: Contested Legacies Portugal / BUALA - https://www.contestedlegaciesportugal.org/
   - **Wording**: By 1486, the Portuguese Crown had established the Casa dos Escravos in Lisbon to administer and tax the rapidly growing slave trade.

5. **Portugal rural labor shortage / Madeira sugar**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: Mid-15th century transition to sugar following Black Death labor shortages
   - **Source**: World History Encyclopedia - https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1775/the-sugar-trade-in-the-early-modern-atlantic-world/
   - **Wording**: A severe rural labor shortage following the Black Death, combined with the lucrative new sugar plantations in Madeira, drove Portugal's initial demand for enslaved laborers.

6. **Lisbon's enslaved population share**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: ~10% of Lisbon's population in the 16th century
   - **Source**: Cambridge University Press (A.C. Saunders) - https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/black-slaves-and-freedmen-in-portugal-14411555
   - **Wording**: By the middle of the 16th century, enslaved Africans made up roughly ten percent of Lisbon’s population.

7. **Ibn Battuta / Shift to African-only slavery**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: Shift occurred during the 15th century in Iberia
   - **Source**: Historical consensus (various academic sources on Iberian slavery)
   - **Wording**: While Mediterranean slavery in Ibn Battuta’s time was multi-ethnic, the 15th-century Iberian trade marked a pivotal shift toward exclusively African enslavement.

## Modern Echo
8. **ACLU 2020 Cannabis Report**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: Black people arrested 3.6x as often
   - **Source**: ACLU "A Tale of Two Countries" - https://www.aclu.org/report/tale-two-countries-racially-targeted-arrests-era-marijuana-reform
   - **Wording**: According to a 2020 ACLU report, Black people are 3.6 times more likely to be arrested for marijuana possession than white people, despite similar usage rates.

9. **National Registry of Exonerations 2022**
   - **Status**: VERIFIED
   - **Exact figure**: Black people 7.5x more likely to be wrongly convicted of murder
   - **Source**: National Registry of Exonerations - https://www.law.umich.edu/special/exoneration/Documents/Race_and_Wrongful_Convictions.pdf
   - **Wording**: A 2022 report by the National Registry of Exonerations found that Black people are approximately 7.5 times more likely than white people to be wrongfully convicted of murder.

10. **Pierson et al. 2020 (Nature Human Behaviour)**
    - **Status**: VERIFIED
    - **Exact figure**: ~100M stops analyzed
    - **Source**: Nature Human Behaviour - https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-020-0858-1
    - **Wording**: A Stanford study of 100 million traffic stops found that police search Black and Hispanic drivers on the basis of less evidence than white drivers.

11. **BJS Police-Public Contact Survey**
    - **Status**: VERIFIED
    - **Exact figure**: Traffic stops are the most common form of contact
    - **Source**: Bureau of Justice Statistics - https://bjs.ojp.gov/data-collection/police-public-contact-survey-ppcs
    - **Wording**: Bureau of Justice Statistics data consistently shows that traffic stops are the most common reason for police-initiated contact with the public.

12. **Lead-crime hypothesis**
    - **Status**: PARTLY
    - **Exact figure**: Early studies (Nevin, Reyes) claimed large effects; 2022 meta-analysis (Higney et al.) points to publication bias and smaller effect
    - **Source**: Regional Science and Urban Economics - https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/regsci/v92y2022icarticle103736.html
    - **Wording**: While early studies strongly linked the 1990s crime decline to the phaseout of leaded gasoline, a 2022 meta-analysis suggests the actual effect was likely much smaller due to publication bias.

13. **Tetraethyl lead history**
    - **Status**: VERIFIED
    - **Exact figure**: 1924 Bayway deaths; Jan 1, 1996 US on-road ban
    - **Source**: EPA History - https://www.epa.gov/history/epa-history-lead
    - **Wording**: Despite refinery worker deaths in 1924 and early warnings of its toxicity, tetraethyl lead remained in U.S. gasoline until its final phaseout for on-road vehicles on January 1, 1996.

14. **Highway routing & freeway revolts**
    - **Status**: VERIFIED
    - **Exact figure**: I-94 in Rondo, Claiborne Ave in New Orleans
    - **Source**: Reconnect Rondo / Smithsonian Magazine - https://reconnectrondo.com/
    - **Wording**: Mid-century highway projects like I-94 in St. Paul and the Claiborne Expressway in New Orleans were deliberately routed through vibrant Black neighborhoods, devastating communities that lacked the political power to block them.

15. **Kennedy Krieger Institute study**
    - **Status**: VERIFIED
    - **Exact figure**: 1993-1995 study; 2001 Grimes v. Kennedy Krieger ruling
    - **Source**: Grimes v. Kennedy Krieger Institute (Maryland Court of Appeals) - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1447053/
    - **Wording**: In the 1990s Kennedy Krieger lead abatement study, researchers exposed children in low-income Baltimore families to lingering lead hazards, leading to a landmark 2001 ethical ruling.

16. **Lewontin 1972 & African genetic diversity**
    - **Status**: VERIFIED
    - **Exact figure**: ~85% variation within populations (Lewontin); greatest diversity in Africa
    - **Source**: Lewontin 1972 / Understanding Race - https://understandingrace.org/
    - **Wording**: Geneticists have long established that the vast majority of human genetic variation exists within populations rather than between them, and that Africa holds the greatest genetic diversity on Earth.

17. **Meyer Lansky & Las Vegas**
    - **Status**: VERIFIED
    - **Exact figure**: Lansky and organized crime financed Las Vegas
    - **Source**: The Mob Museum - https://themobmuseum.org/
    - **Wording**: Following the multi-ethnic organized crime wave of Prohibition, figures like Meyer Lansky funneled illicit syndicate wealth into the legitimate financing of early Las Vegas casinos.

18. **Texas plantation prisons**
    - **Status**: VERIFIED
    - **Exact figure**: Nine units on former plantation land; unpaid labor
    - **Source**: Texas Observer, July 2024 - https://www.texasobserver.org/texas-plantation-prisons-forced-labor/
    - **Wording**: A 2024 Texas Observer investigation revealed that nine Texas prison units sit on former slave plantations, where incarcerated people are still forced to work the fields without pay.
