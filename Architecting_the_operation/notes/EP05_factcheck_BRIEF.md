## Portugal
1. Gomes Eanes de Zurara's Chronicle of the Discovery and Conquest of Guinea describes a public sale
   of captive Africans at Lagos, Portugal, on 8 August 1444; about 235 people; organized under
   Prince Henry's patronage (Lançarote de Freitas). Zurara both laments and justifies it.
2. Dum Diversas (Pope Nicholas V, 18 June 1452) authorizes Afonso V to reduce "Saracens and pagans"
   to perpetual servitude.
3. Romanus Pontifex (Nicholas V, 8 January 1455) confirms and extends it to Africa south of Cape
   Bojador.
4. Casa dos Escravos in Lisbon, established 1486 (some sources 1481/1486); state administration of
   the slave trade.
5. Portugal faced a rural labor shortage after the Black Death; sugar in Madeira was the driver.
6. Lisbon's enslaved population share in the 16th century (often cited ~10%).
7. Ibn Battuta / earlier Mediterranean slavery was multi-ethnic (Slavs, Muslims); the shift to
   African-only slavery in Iberia during the 15th century.

## Modern echo
8. Cannabis: Black and white Americans use at similar rates; Black people arrested ~3.6x as often
   (ACLU 2020 report "A Tale of Two Countries").
9. National Registry of Exonerations 2022 report: Black people ~7.5x more likely than white people
   to be wrongly convicted of murder; Black share of exonerees.
10. Pierson et al. 2020, Nature Human Behaviour (Stanford Open Policing): ~100M stops; veil of
    darkness result; outcome/threshold test (searches of Black and Hispanic drivers on less
    evidence, lower contraband hit rates).
11. BJS Police-Public Contact Survey: traffic stops are the most common form of police-initiated
    contact.
12. Lead-crime: Nevin 2000/2007; Reyes 2007 (NBER) share of the 1990s crime decline attributed to
    leaded gasoline phaseout; Aizer & Currie 2019; Higney, Hanley & Moro 2022 meta-analysis
    (publication bias; smaller effect).
13. Tetraethyl lead: toxicity known in the 1920s (1924 Bayway refinery deaths; 1925 Surgeon General
    conference); U.S. on-road leaded gasoline ban effective 1 January 1996; Ethyl Corp opposition.
14. Highway routing through Black neighborhoods (e.g., I-94 St. Paul Rondo; Claiborne Ave New
    Orleans); white "freeway revolts" that blocked routes.
15. Kennedy Krieger Institute lead abatement study (1993-1995), Baltimore; Grimes v. Kennedy
    Krieger (Md. 2001).
16. Africa holds the greatest human genetic diversity; more variation within populations than
    between them (Lewontin 1972; later work).
17. 1920s crime: Italian, Irish, Jewish organized crime during Prohibition; later assimilation;
    Meyer Lansky and Las Vegas casino financing. (Only claims with strong sourcing.)
18. Texas Observer 2024: nine Texas prison units on former plantation land; incarcerated field
    workers are unpaid in Texas.
