# Reply to the Question 9 ballot guide (draft 1)

Councilor Louijeune posted the official summary of Question 9. Everything on that slide is accurate. Here's what it leaves out: where gun violence happens, who this law shuts out, and how.

Start with where. In the 1930s, the federal government graded neighborhoods for mortgage risk. Neighborhoods where Black families lived were graded D, hazardous, and outlined in red. That's redlining. A 2024 study in the Annals of Internal Medicine laid those maps over gun deaths in 202 cities, from 2014 to 2022. Neighborhoods graded D had more than six times the death rate of neighborhoods graded A, and D came out higher in three out of four cities. A 21-city study found the same staircase: the worse the grade, the more firearm deaths.

Here in Boston, from 2019 to 2025, the areas graded C and D have about seven times the shooting rate of A and B. The research can't separate the map from what followed it: the poverty, the disinvestment, the lead. Ninety percent of Boston's housing was built before lead paint was banned, and the city still has about three thousand known lead service lines. Nothing in this law replaces one.

Now who it shuts out. If you legally held one of these rifles on August 1st, 2024, you can register it by October 28th and keep it. Anyone after that date can't buy the same rifle. Same gun. Only the date differs.

That's a grandfather clause, and we've seen them before. In 1898, Louisiana let you skip its new voting tests if your grandfather could vote before 1867. Black registration fell from about a hundred thirty thousand to about five thousand in three years. Oklahoma tried the same thing. Its clause was neutral on its face, and in 1915 the Supreme Court struck it down anyway, in Guinn v. United States, because the date did the work.

So who's on the wrong side of this date? The people just getting in. Gun ownership among Black women jumped eighty-seven percent in 2021, and Black gun buyers were up fifty-eight percent the year before. The date shuts the door right as they arrive.

Now how. Since August 2024, a new license costs a hundred-dollar state fee plus a mandatory live-fire course, usually another hundred to a hundred fifty. That cost lands differently depending on who you are. The Boston Fed's 2025 survey puts median net wealth in Massachusetts at about five hundred fifty thousand dollars for white families and seventy-eight hundred for Black families. Nearly a third of Black families have zero or less.

A fee you pay before you can use a right is how a poll tax works. Virginia charged a dollar fifty to vote, and in 1966 the Supreme Court struck it down in Harper v. Virginia. Even Bruen, the 2022 gun case, warned that exorbitant fees can deny people a right.

So read that summary again. It describes a law with nothing for the lead pipes in the neighborhoods the 1938 map marked, and a date and a price in front of the people there who are trying to protect themselves.
