# Why Chapter 135 Demands a "No" Vote on Massachusetts Question 9

Question 9 is a referendum on Chapter 135 of the Acts of 2024. A "Yes" vote retains Chapter 135. A "No" vote repeals Chapter 135 and restores Massachusetts firearm law to its pre-existing statutory baseline.

Voting "No" leaves Massachusetts universal background checks, extreme risk protection orders (red flag laws), and strict safe-storage mandates intact. Those provisions existed before Chapter 135. Chapter 135 introduced compounding structural burdens that enforce systemic racism, sexism, and ableism within a single statutory framework.

---

### 1. Systemic Racism: Wealth Exclusion and the Fee Barrier

Chapter 135 establishes financial thresholds for licensing that replicate the mechanics of historical poll taxes.

Under Chapter 135 and its accompanying executive mandates, obtaining a basic License to Carry (LTC) requires an application fee of $100, live-fire training courses ranging from $120 to $200, basic safety courses, and lost wages from mandatory classroom hours. Total upfront compliance costs routinely exceed $250 to $300 before acquiring a tool for self-defense.

In Massachusetts, economic resources follow deep historical divisions. The Federal Reserve Bank of Boston's 2026 Mass ECHOS report documents that median household net wealth in Massachusetts is approximately $550,000 for White families and $7,800 for Black families—a disparity exceeding seventy to one.

A flat $300 cash barrier consumes 3.8% of total median Black household net wealth in the Commonwealth. That same flat fee represents 0.05% of median White household net wealth.

When Virginia enacted its poll tax in 1902 at $1.50 per year (equivalent to roughly $15 to $50 today), the Supreme Court struck it down in *Harper v. Virginia State Board of Elections* (1966) because economic status cannot condition an enumerated constitutional protection. Chapter 135 imposes a fee barrier multiple times higher than the historical poll tax on an unalienable civil right.

Geographically, firearm violence in Massachusetts remains concentrated. CDC and state public health data establish that approximately 60% of firearm homicides in the state occur within four zip codes comprising 4% of the population. These zip codes correspond to neighborhoods redlined under the 1938 Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) maps. Chapter 135 disarms residents living in historically disinvested neighborhoods where police response times are longest, while preserving access for affluent suburban populations.

---

### 2. Systemic Sexism: Biomechanical Fit and Recoil Management

Chapter 135 expands the state's "assault weapon" definition by prohibiting common external ergonomic components: adjustable stocks, pistol grips, forward vertical grips, barrel shrouds, and threaded barrels.

These mechanical features govern physical interface and weapon control. Ballistic velocity and cycling rate remain unchanged. A semi-automatic rifle chambered in 5.56 NATO or .223 Remington discharges the identical cartridge at the identical rate of fire regardless of stock geometry.

Fixed rifle stocks are manufactured according to standard 50th-to-95th percentile male military anthropometric tables (typically a 13.5 to 14.5-inch length of pull). An adjustable telescoping stock allows a shooter to shorten the length of pull to match smaller skeletal frames. For female shooters, an adjustable stock allows proper shoulder pocket seating, correct eye relief through optics, and balanced center-of-gravity distribution.

Mandating fixed stocks forces female shooters to use oversized, male-proportioned platforms. This structural requirement increases perceived recoil, degrades muzzle control, and compromises defensive accuracy.

Similarly, pistol grips allow an operator with lower upper-body mass to maintain a neutral skeletal wrist angle that cushions recoil directly into the forearm. Chapter 135 bans the ergonomic features required for smaller-framed individuals to exercise effective self-defense.

---

### 3. Systemic Ableism: Adaptive Assistive Equipment Classified as Contraband

Chapter 135 outlaws the mechanical adaptations that permit disabled individuals to hold, aim, and safely discharge a firearm.

- **Barrel Shrouds**: Rapid fire generates exterior barrel temperatures exceeding 300°F. A barrel shroud encloses the barrel, insulating the support hand from contact burns. For individuals with peripheral neuropathy, paraplegia, multiple sclerosis, or reduced sensory feedback, a barrel shroud prevents severe third-degree thermal injuries. Chapter 135 classifies this thermal safety guard as a prohibited combat feature.
- **Vertical Forward Grips**: Individuals living with rheumatoid arthritis, carpal tunnel syndrome, joint fusions, tremors, or limb differences cannot supinate the wrist to cup a standard horizontal rifle fore-end. A vertical forward grip provides a vertical handhold that distributes weapon weight across the forearm musculature. Chapter 135 outlaws this grip.
- **Adjustable and Folding Stocks**: Wheelchair users and individuals with spinal deformities require altered lengths of pull and cheek welds to achieve stable shooting posture from seated positions. Chapter 135 criminalizes these necessary adjustments.

Federal civil rights doctrine defines reasonable accommodation as the provision of auxiliary aids that permit disabled persons equal access to civic life. Chapter 135 criminalizes those auxiliary aids under the rubric of public safety.

---

### Conclusion

Massachusetts empirical data shows that state-level firearm homicide rates track poverty ($r = +0.72$), while tracking gun regulatory density at approximately zero once socioeconomic deprivation is controlled.

Chapter 135 concentrates state coercion against the vulnerable: lower-income Black families priced out by administrative poll taxes, women denied ergonomic weapon fit, and disabled citizens denied assistive physical interfaces.

Voting **NO on Question 9** repeals Chapter 135. It leaves universal background checks, safe storage laws, and extreme risk protection orders fully in place while removing this discriminatory framework.
