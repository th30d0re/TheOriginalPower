## Grounded facts (verified, with provenance)

- Question 9 on the November 3, 2026 Massachusetts ballot: a YES vote keeps Chapter
  135 (Acts of 2024, signed July 25, 2024); a NO vote repeals it. (Card 2)
- Chapter 135 bundles an expanded "assault weapon" definition,
  registration/serialization mandates, carry restrictions, and licensing/training
  costs. It contains no funding for after-school, arts, or mental-health programs.
  (Card 4)
- Firearms lawfully possessed before August 1, 2024 are grandfathered (subject to
  registration/serialization by October 28, 2026). A firearm newly classified as an
  "assault weapon" cannot legally be acquired at all after that date. (Card 17)
- Pew Research Center, 2017: 48% of white men personally own a gun, versus 24% of
  white women, 24% of nonwhite men, and 16% of nonwhite women. Pew, June 2023
  survey: among non-owners, 56% of Black adults say they could see themselves owning
  a gun — the highest share of any racial group (White 48%, Hispanic 40%, Asian
  38%). (Card 23)
- The AR-15 is banned by name. The Ruger Mini-14 — same 5.56/.223 caliber — is named
  by the MA Attorney General as NOT covered. The operative distinction is a features
  test: adjustable stock, grip configuration, attachments. (Card 21)

## Exact on-card copy (use this text verbatim)

**Headline:** A "YES" vote keeps Chapter 135 — including the expanded
assault-weapons definition.

**Beat 1 — the cutoff:**
Owned before Aug 1, 2024? You keep your AR-15.
Buying after? Banned outright.

**Beat 2 — who sits on which side:**
Who already owns: white men 48%, nonwhite women 16%. (Pew, 2017)
Who's buying next: 56% of Black non-owners see themselves owning a gun — the
highest of any group. (Pew, 2023)

**Beat 3 — what the locked-out buyer gets:**
The legal alternative fires the same round.
The banned "features" are the adjustability — the stock and grip geometry that lets
a smaller shooter fit the rifle.

**Footer (small type):**
Sources: Pew Research Center 2017 & 2023 · Acts of 2024, Ch. 135 · mass.gov

## Why v3 (context for the generator, do not render this section)

v2 of this card listed the law's provisions procedurally (registration mandates,
serialization, licensing fees). Review rejected that framing: the card must show who
the law's cutoff affects — existing owners keep their rifles, new buyers (the
fastest-growing segment of ownership) are locked out, and the legal alternative
lacks the adjustability that lets smaller shooters fit the rifle.
