# Reel idea: if you want to curb violence, talk about repair (2026-10-14)

Owner's argument, as dictated, for a campaign reel against Chapter 135. Not yet scripted.

## The frame

- Talking about guns is already the distraction. "Gun violence" puts the emphasis on the tool; the
  problem is violence, and violence tracks conditions the state created.
- The evidence chain already in the sources page: redlining → concentrated poverty (Dholakia et al.
  2024, section 29) → poverty tracks gun homicide at about +0.72 across the states, and gun-law
  strength drops to about zero once poverty and inequality are controlled (section 22) → lead exposure
  concentrated in the same Boston neighborhoods (section 6).
- So addressing the inequality is the violence policy. A bill packaged to do that would do more to
  curb violence than Chapter 135.
- Incoherence: supporting reparations and supporting gun control that leaves the root harms in place.
  The current law is performative (safety theater, symbolic legislation).

## What repair looks like (no cash transfers needed)

- Remove Boston's lead service lines proactively. About 3,000 known, possibly thousands more; the free
  replacement program had about 400 participants in its first year (section 6).
- Test and fix drinking water in Boston Public Schools; about 90 schools had used bottled water since
  the late 1980s, and four schools' fountains tested over the limit in 2016 (section 6).
- Reimburse or adjust for families in formerly redlined areas: trackable by address and HOLC grade,
  by application. Possible forms: housing grants, a temporary state tax exemption.
- Precedent to verify before citing: Evanston, Illinois's restorative housing program (2021), housing
  grants to Black residents affected by past housing discrimination, funded by a cannabis tax.

## Lines to check before they go on camera

- "Criminals by definition don't follow laws, so you can't make laws criminals will follow." RAND rates
  background checks as moderate evidence for reducing homicide (section 23), so critics will answer
  this. Narrower version that holds: Massachusetts already has the laws with the best evidence.
- "There's no charge for murder with a knife; the law charges the harm." Massachusetts does have
  weapon-based charges, for example assault and battery by means of a dangerous weapon and armed
  robbery (G.L. c. 265; verify exact sections). The defensible point: possession of a banned rifle is
  charged with no harm done, one to ten years for a first offense (section 13), the same rifle a
  neighbor keeps legally because of a date.
- "Reparations would drop the violence" is an inference from the correlations. Say it as an inference:
  the conditions that predict violence are the ones repair would change.
