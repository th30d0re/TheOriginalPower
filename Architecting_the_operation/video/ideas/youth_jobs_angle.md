# Angle: "Keep our communities safe" while cutting youth jobs (researched 2026-10-16)

## The news hook

- NBC Boston, about Oct. 13, 2026: ["Boston police officers join Dorchester protest walk against cuts to youth
  jobs"](https://www.nbcboston.com/video/on-air/as-seen-on/boston-police-officers-join-dorchester-protest-walk-against-cuts-to-youth-jobs/4029957/).
  Teaser: advocates say a proposed change could eliminate employment for teens and "put them on the wrong
  path." **Only the headline and teaser were readable.** Who organized it, how many officers, which district,
  what they said, and which "proposed change" it is are unconfirmed. Watch the clip before scripting.
- Likely context: the Center for Teen Empowerment (Dorchester) runs youth organizing and a long-running
  teen-police program ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/boston-police-dorchester-teen-empowerment-common-ground-a-more-perfect-union/)),
  and its staff led the spring youth-jobs push. Not confirmed as the walk's organizer.

## What was cut

**City of Boston, FY27 budget (July 2026 to June 2027)**
- Mayor Wu's proposal zeroed out the year-round (school-year) youth jobs program, $5.9 million in the prior
  budget ([Dorchester Reporter, Apr. 16](https://www.dotnews.com/2026/04/16/teen-activists-press-for-wu-to-restore-jobs-funding-in-latest-budget-proposal/)).
  The six-week summer program stayed funded. Reports put the lost positions at about 1,700 to 1,800.
- Wu said private partners (the Private Industry Council) would absorb the roughly $6 million gap
  ([Dorchester Reporter, Apr. 24](https://www.dotnews.com/2026/04/24/wu-partners-will-help-fund-youth-jobs/)).
  A June 1 Boston Globe headline points to about 500 school-year jobs through a private partnership
  ([Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/01/metro/wu-boston-500-school-year-jobs-private-partnership/));
  the number is from the URL and has not been read in the article.
- The council's amendment package added $750,000 for youth jobs; Wu accepted nearly all amendments
  ([Boston.com, June 25](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2026/06/25/after-a-tense-process-city-council-accepts-wus-amended-budget-heres-what-to-know/)).
- Police budget in the same proposal: a $7 million increase to a $477 million budget (Apr. 16). Police
  overtime was budgeted around $55 million in recent years and expected to exceed $100 million this year
  (Apr. 24, reporter's figures).

**Commonwealth, summer youth jobs**
- March 2026: hundreds of teens rallied at the State House against a 40 percent cut to state summer
  youth jobs funding for the fiscal year ending June 30; organizers said jobs would drop from 6,000 to 3,600
  ([Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/03/beacon-hill-showdown-as-boston-teens-pack-state-house-over-youth-jobs-cuts/);
  [Globe, Mar. 25](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/03/25/metro/massachusetts-youth-demand-jobs/)).
  **Who made it (checked 2026-10-16):** no single cut. Fiscal 2025 had about $23 million, of which $16.24
  million was appropriated and the rest was one-time money (prior-year balances and unspent federal ARPA
  funds); that paid for about 6,500 jobs. Fiscal 2026 had $15.24 million in line item 7002-0012 and about
  3,800 jobs (State House News via [Worcester Business Journal](https://wbjournal.com/article/summer-jobs-for-young-people-riding-on-budget-decisions/)).
  The governor's fiscal 2026 budget filing had $15.7 million, so it did not replace the expiring money
  ([mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/news/governor-healey-and-lieutenant-governor-driscoll-file-596-billion-fiscal-year-2026-budget)).
  The 2026 grant round: about $14.4 million for 3,786 youth, against $22.68 million for over 6,500 in 2025
  ([CommCorp 2026](https://commcorp.org/press-releases/14.4-million-in-youthworks-grants-awarded-to-hire-over-3500-youth),
  [CommCorp 2025](https://commcorp.org/press-releases/22-million-in-youthworks-grants-awarded-to-hire-over-6500-youth)).
  For fiscal 2027, Healey's proposal and the Senate's were about $15.4 million and the House's about $17.6
  million; Healey signed the $63.4 billion budget on July 9, 2026 with no vetoes. The final YouthWorks figure
  is unconfirmed (one release snippet says $15.6 million). No report found that the $10 million the teens
  asked for was restored.

## The evidence that youth jobs reduce violence

- Chicago, One Summer Plus (Heller, *Science*; randomized, 1,634 teens from high-violence neighborhoods):
  43 percent fewer violent-crime arrests over the follow-up, mostly after the program ended
  ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/penn-researcher-finds-summer-jobs-decrease-youth-violence-and-crime);
  [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/summer-jobs-reduce-violence-among-disadvantaged-youth-united-states)).
- Boston's own summer jobs program (Modestino, *Journal of Policy Analysis and Management*, 2019):
  35 percent fewer violent-crime arraignments over 17 months, larger for young men; the author calls it
  suggestive ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/how-bostons-summer-employment-program-affects-youth-criminal-justice-outcomes)).

## The angle

In the Yes-on-9 reel, Gov. Healey called after-school programs, sports and the arts "the number one thing"
for keeping communities safe. A yes vote funds none of it. Meanwhile the state cut summer jobs and Boston
cut year-round jobs, the programs with randomized evidence of reducing violence, so far that Boston police
walked with the teens protesting. Chapter 135 adds a date, a price and a features ban.

Lines to keep accurate: Boston cut the year-round program, not summer jobs; private partners replaced part
of it. The state cut is the one that hit summer jobs. Don't attribute the state cut to Healey until confirmed.
