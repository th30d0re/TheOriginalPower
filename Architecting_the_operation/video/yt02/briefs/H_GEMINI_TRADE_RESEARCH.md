## Why

YT02v2 jumps from the Portugal test (SEG 08, ending at 1486) straight to Virginia in 1619 (SEG 09). The author wants a segue between them: how the slave trade progressed after Portugal, who the major players became, and what the Caribbean, North America and South America looked like by 1619. Figma maps will show the triangle trade and the transatlantic trade, so the research has to supply map-ready data.

Read the book's treatment first: `Paper/The_Original_Power.tex` (grep `asiento`, `Royal African`, `Barbados`, `Saint-Domingue`, `Brazil`, `sugar`, `Middle Passage`, `triangle`). Where the book already says something, cite `tex:LINE` and agree or disagree with it.

## Answer, with a source for every claim

1. **Timeline, 1486–1700.** Each entry gets a year, an event and a source:
   - Portuguese São Tomé and the Kongo/Angola trade; the Spanish asiento (1518 onward);
   - Brazilian sugar; the Dutch West India Company (1621) and the Dutch in Brazil and at Elmina (1637);
   - the English in Barbados (1627) and the sugar revolution of the 1640s; the Royal African Company (1660/1672);
   - French Saint-Domingue (1697).
   Keep the dates exact and flag any the sources disagree on.
2. **Major players by period.** Which states and chartered companies carried the most captives in each quarter-century. Use the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database (slavevoyages.org) estimates by flag, with numbers.
3. **Volumes.** Totals embarked and disembarked; the share to Brazil, the Spanish Americas, the British Caribbean, the French Caribbean and mainland North America. Cite SlaveVoyages and Eltis & Richardson's *Atlas of the Transatlantic Slave Trade* (2010).
4. **The triangle trade.** The three legs, the goods on each leg, the main ports (Liverpool, Bristol, Nantes, Lisbon, Seville/Cádiz, Elmina, Ouidah, Luanda, Bridgetown, Kingston, Cap-Français, Salvador, Charleston) and the Middle Passage mortality. Say where "triangle" oversimplifies the trade: the Brazil–Angola bilateral route.
5. **The Americas in 1619.** A short picture of the Caribbean, Brazil, Spanish America and the Chesapeake in 1619: who held what, and where enslaved Africans were concentrated.
6. **Map data.** A table of routes (origin region → destination region, period, approximate volume) and ports with lat/long, ready for Figma.
