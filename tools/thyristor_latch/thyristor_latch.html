Thyristor Latch

A thyristor stays on after its gate pulse ends, as long as the current through it stays above the holding current IH. The Elite's extraction loop latches the same way: extraction funds the cultural field B (eq. 0.1a), and the field keeps the extraction flowing. When the Out-group routes enough of its energy through its own grid, the current falls below IH and the machine powers down (Parasitic System Collapse Theorem).

The self-exciting loop

Try it: raise s slowly until the latch releases, then lower it again. The machine stays off until the Elite fire the gate, and the gate fails while s stays above the critical share.

What the page assumes

From the manuscript: the self-exciting field, ∂B/∂t = η·(extraction) − λB (eq. 0.1a, Paper/The_Original_Power.tex:474); the reinforcing extraction loop and the Parasitic System Collapse Theorem, where routing energy through local, open-source grids severs Vcc and the holding current IH drops (:14412–:14440); the enforcement nodes as the holding current of the extraction machine (:12222).

The page adds one assumption. Eq. 0.1a is linear: with a fixed extraction term, the field settles at η·E/λ and reaches zero only when extraction stops completely. A latch needs feedback in the other direction too. Here, extraction depends on the field, because compliance depends on it: I = (1 − s)·E0·B²/(B² + K²). With that, the loop has a threshold. The on state exists only while η(1 − s)E0 ≥ 2λK, so the critical share is s* = 1 − 2λK/(ηE0), and the holding current is IH = λK/η, the current at the fold. E0 = 1 and K = 1; time is in years. The values are illustrative.
