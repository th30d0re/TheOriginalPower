Firearms timeline (proposal)

Two indices drive W. B is the Buffer's access to militia-grade arms. O is the Out-group's access. Each runs from 0 (banned) to 1 (open). The class of arms is the musket in 1705 and the AR-15 class today.

ψm = km · B / Fγ
ψs = ks · (B − O) / Fγ

ψm is the material value of the Buffer's own access. ψs is the status premium. It shrinks as O gains the access that B has.

F is the Elite's enforcement capacity: the U.S. incarceration rate relative to 1980 (eq63_mass_incarceration.csv). The repository has no series before 1980, so F = 1 there. Both wages are divided by Fγ. γ = 0 turns F off.

YearEventBO

Load manuscript events: the rows from 1911 to 2024 come from eq65_68_2a_case_law.csv. Each restriction moves an index by 0.05, or 0.025 for a state law. A law aimed at O lowers O. It also lowers B by half as much if its scope later reached the Buffer. A law aimed at the Buffer lowers B and O together. A ruling that expands rights raises them. The rows before 1911 are placeholders tied to events in the manuscript.

The seed values for B and O are my placeholders, not data. The events come from the public record and need citations before the book uses them. B and O should measure effective access, not only the written law.

Arms parity (proposal)

Parity is lawful civilian access to the state's standard infantry arm. When parity drives the material wage, ψm measures the promise "here are the weapons that let you stop us."

Parity = Pfed(t) · (1 − h · ban)
ψm = km · Parity / Fγ

Blue: the Buffer's parity. Teal, dashed: O's parity, which is O's access times the same federal level and ban term (with qO for grandfathered arms). With κ above 0, O's parity raises M: M = M₀ + κ · O parity − a·ψm − b·ψs. κ is 0 by default, so earlier results do not change.

Pfed is 1 before the National Firearms Act (1934). From 1934, automatic arms need registration and a $200 tax that was never indexed (about $4,700 in 2024 dollars). From 1986, the Hughes Amendment closes new automatic arms to civilians. The ban term uses the federal assault-weapon ban (1994–2004) and, from 1979, the RAND state bans; h is the ban depth from the state panel. The two levels are ordinal proposals (Tier 3). When parity is on, B and O use carry access only (w = 1), so a ban does not count twice.

Each state's wage in the selected year

C = Σ regime score × share of the year
A = 1 − h × ban
B = w · C + (1 − w) · A
O = (w · CO + (1 − w) · A) · (1 − g)
CO multiplies the may-issue score by δ

The scores, δ, h, and g are proposals. g covers gaps in effective access that the law data do not record, such as enforcement and prohibited-person rules. It is 0 by default.

Data rules: where two carry laws overlap, the most recently effective law sets the regime. MO1072 is a permit to acquire a handgun filed under concealed carry, so it is left out. DC1063 is a repeal, so DC counts as prohibited from 2009 to 2014. Virginia has no carry law in the database before July 1995, so it drops out of the national average before then. Population weights are 2024 values derived from Paper/data/gun_laws_new_england/states.csv.

Sensitive places and transit (post-Bruen)

Where public transit is a sensitive place, a license holder who rides it must stay unarmed for the whole trip. The page lowers each group's carry access by the share of its workers who commute by transit (ACS 2024 5-year, tables B08105B and B08105H). O uses Black workers. B uses white non-Hispanic workers.

CB × (1 − transit ban × eB)
CO × (1 − transit ban × eO)

The four states above have transit bans for licensed carry with confirmed coverage. California and Hawaii passed post-Bruen lists, but transit coverage is not confirmed here, so they are off. Washington's 2024 transit ban exempts license holders. Exposure counts only commutes to work. At scale 1, other trips are left out.

Grandfather clauses

Chapter 135 exempts firearms lawfully possessed on August 1, 2024 (G.L. c. 140, § 131M). New owners cannot get the banned configurations. q is the share of each group's owners whose arms predate a ban. With parity on, B's parity uses qB, and O's carry access is scaled by O's parity relative to B's.

No source here gives q by group. Pew finds that 24% of Black adults owned a gun in both 2017 and 2023, so the reported surge of new Black buyers does not appear as a higher ownership rate. Both values stay 0 until there is data.

What changed between two years

Each change is split among B, O, F, and arms parity with Shapley values. The parts add up to the total change.

Post-Bruen test

Method fixed before the run. Treated: the states with a may-issue carry regime on June 22, 2022, the day before Bruen. Outcome: state laws that took effect after Bruen, against the same number of days before it. Federal rows are left out. Data: RAND State Firearm Law Database 6.0.

Limits: RAND has no class for sensitive-places laws, a main part of the post-Bruen response (for example, in New York's 2022 Concealed Carry Improvement Act). Uvalde (May 2022) and the federal Bipartisan Safer Communities Act (June 2022) came at the same time and drove new laws in many states. DC was already shall-issue in June 2022, so it is not in the treated group. Known post-Bruen sensitive-places laws: New York (Concealed Carry Improvement Act, 2022), New Jersey (Chapter 131, 2022), Maryland (SB 1, 2023), Hawaii (2023), California (SB 2, 2023, in force 2024), and Massachusetts (Chapter 135, 2024). All six are states that Bruen moved from may-issue to shall-issue.

Post-Bruen response, coded from sources

Outcome A: a law signed after Bruen that bars licensed carriers from at least 10 categories of public places. Outcome B: a law signed from June 23, 2022 to September 1, 2025 that enacts an assault-weapon ban or widens its definition. I set both definitions after reading the six forced states' laws, so this comparison describes the pattern; it is not a blind test. "No" for a state means no such law was found in the sources searched.

The runtime-log ranges and the shock list are the author's own estimates. They are independent of this page, but not of the author.

Saint-Domingue, 1789–1804 (coded)

Two Buffer phasors: the gens de couleur libres (orange) and the petits blancs (violet). O, the enslaved majority, has no phasor. Its mobilization and its arms parity raise M and grow the τ zone. Every point comes from a coded row below: ordinal scores with stated rubrics, evidence, and sources. The scores are judgments, and most sources are encyclopedias; check them against Dubois and Geggus before the book uses them.

  • O mobilization
  • O arms parity

M = M₀ + p · mobilization · (0.5 + 0.5 · O parity) − a·ψm − b·ψs

a, b, and τ come from the τ model panel. While the scenario is on, dragging W is off.

Gens de couleur libres, 1789–1801, coded from sources

Gens de couleur libres, 1802, coded from sources

Both gens de couleur libres tables use one rubric. The wages measure what the colonial order gives the group. M1: commission or command in French service (+1 held, 0 lost). M2: safety of person and property under French rule (+1 secure, 0 at risk, −1 under attack by the order). S1: equal rights under the 1792 law (+1 in force, −1 repealed or acted against). S2: signals from Paris on the rights of people of color (0 none, −1 hostile). ψm = 40 × mean(M1, M2). ψs = 60 × mean(S1, S2).

Petits blancs, coded from sources

P1: posts granted by the order (+1 held, 0.5 modest, 0 none). P2: safety of person and property (+1 secure, 0 at risk, −1 attacked or destroyed). S1: privilege over free people of color (+1 exclusive, 0 equal). S2: status over the enslaved (+1 slavery in force, 0 abolished, −1 under a Black-led government). Same scales: ψm = 40 × mean(P1, P2), ψs = 60 × mean(S1, S2).

O, coded from sources

Mobilization: 0.1 marronage only, 0.3 after submission, 0.5 organized revolt in one province, 0.6 army of the colonial state, 0.8 war across provinces, 1.0 colony-wide war under a unified command. Arms parity: 0 disarmed by law, 0.25 irregular arms, 0.5 armed by an outside power or under disarmament, 0.75 standing army with state arms, 1.0 standing army that defeats the colonial force.

The gens de couleur libres cross τ twice. In 1791 the April 1792 decree buys them back. In 1802, with O armed and the Polish defections under way, nothing buys them back, and the plane ends in 1804. James, The Black Jacobins; Laurent Dubois, Avengers of the New World; works by David Geggus cited in the manuscript.
