Snubber Circuit

A kernel breach cuts the current that holds an era's racialized field in the coil L. The collapsing field drives a back-EMF surge, v = −L di/dt: the backlash (eq. 1.7d). Its energy, ½LI₀², has to go somewhere. Three paths compete for it: the Elite's recovery path into the next coil, the Out-group's snubber grid, and the armed TVS clamp.

The switching moment

Select a path in the drawing to switch it on or off.

L, Cnext, Cs, and Rs move on log scales. Vbr keeps its electrical meaning: a lower Vbr clamps the surge sooner. The manuscript says arms and training raise Vbr (:11611). In a real TVS diode, a higher Vbr lets a larger surge through before the clamp acts, so the two readings conflict (see the README).

    What the page assumes

    From the manuscript: the back-EMF surge as backlash, v = −L di/dt (eq. 1.7d), and the stored energy U = ½LI² (eq. 1.7e), with the Elite harvesting it into the next era's capacitors (Paper/The_Original_Power.tex:2416–:2440); the snubber as the Out-group routing its energy through local, open-source grids (:14435, :14348); the TVS diode as armed deterrence that clamps strikes above Vbr (:11598–:11630).

    The circuit: when the switch opens at t = 0, the coil current I flows into the node and splits among a small stray capacitance (always present, 0.002), the Elite's recovery path (an ideal diode and Rh = 0.5 into Cnext, which starts empty), the snubber (Rs in series with Cs), and the TVS clamp (an ideal clamp at ±Vbr). The coil has a small resistance (0.05). The page integrates the circuit numerically and adds up the energy in each path. Energy that charges Cnext or heats Rh counts as recovered by the Elite; energy in Rs or left in Cs counts as absorbed by the grid.

    The manuscript gives no values for these parts. Every number here is illustrative, so the page shows how the paths compete, not how large any historical surge was.
