## Transcript

Remember this clip. "If you kick every Latino out of this country, then who is going to be cleaning your toilet, Donald Trump?" Oh, that's not — in the sense that no. What Kelly Osbourne worded poorly in a 2015 episode of *The View* is actually referring to a complex sociological concept called **offshoring reproduction**, popularized by feminist scholar Laura Briggs.

Offshoring reproduction refers to how in the 1980s American families began outsourcing domestic labor — cooking, cleaning, child care — to immigrant women they could pay less than American-born women competing for the same jobs. Briggs argues that globalization moves manufacturing outside the United States to cut costs and relies on offshoring reproduction to cut reproductive costs.

In the 1980s, due to Reagan's hyper-privatization and deregulation, real wages dropped and the minimum wage was frozen, which forced more women into the workforce as their husbands' salaries no longer covered family costs. With more women working, demand for childcare rose. In 1987 only 6% of middle-class families could afford nannies — the preferred childcare arrangement — so families inserted underpaid immigrant labor.

A woman who ran a home services agency told *The New York Times* in 1987: "The reason that people hire immigrants without papers is that they're looking to save. If they want legal, they can get it, but it costs." Another woman from Queens said she was explicitly looking for an undocumented immigrant because: "I want someone who cannot leave the country, who doesn't know anyone in New York, who basically does not have a life."

Paying women of color subpar wages has consistently shifted the burden of wealth inequality off white working-class households and onto immigrants and people of color. Women make 83 cents to a man's dollar on average; Hispanic women make 57 cents. The speaker runs a five-step causal chain:

1. Reagan-era real wage decline forced more women into the workforce.
2. Greater workforce participation increased childcare demand.
3. Hiring and underpaying immigrants let white working-class families afford domestic help they could not get from U.S.-born nannies at market rates.
4. That arrangement shifted economic burden onto immigrants and people of color — a distraction that protects millionaires and billionaires.
5. These trends persist: in 2024, 97% of childcare providers were women; Hispanic women are disproportionately represented among domestic workers, especially maids and housekeepers — the structural point Kelly Osbourne was attempting, however poorly worded.

Closing line: "This is all Reagan's fault."
