# Admiral Killick and the *Crête-à-Pierrot*: Léger, FRUS, and the Haitian Record

Research completed 2 September 2026. No manuscript or bibliography file was edited.

## Finding

Jacques Nicolas Léger does not support the manuscript's full sentence. His French-language 1907 edition says that Killick sent the crew ashore, ignited a fuse leading to the powder magazine, lit a cigar, sat down, and waited for the explosion. It says nothing about a Haitian flag, the bow, a cabin, or the number of men who remained. The parallel English-language 1907 edition adds one location: Killick “seated himself on deck.” It still says nothing about a flag or companions. [Léger, French ed., p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [Léger, English ed., p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

The United States legation compilation gives a more specific account and also contains internal disagreement. William F. Powell's narrative places Killick in his cabin with two men, dressed in uniform, seated in a chair, after lighting a cigar and firing a prepared train. The same narrative later refers to “the other five” as missing. Peter Sarthou, Killick's private secretary, says that Killick entered his cabin and blew up the afterpart of the ship; Sarthou identifies the additional dead as Dr. Coles and two stewards. [FRUS 1902, doc. 653, pp. 657–58, 660–61](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

The earliest source opened in this search that contains the flag detail is J.-C. Dorsainvil's 1934 *Manuel d'histoire d'Haïti*. It says that Killick wrapped himself in the national flag and fired into the powder room. It names only Dr. Coles as remaining with him. This establishes the flag detail in a Haitian printed account by 1934. The opened sources do not establish when or from whom Dorsainvil obtained it. [Dorsainvil, p. 332](https://beta-omk.manioc.org/files/original/2821/PAP11077.pdf).

## Editions and verification method

I read both editions published by the Neale Publishing Company in 1907:

- the French-language *Haïti: son histoire et ses détracteurs*, Manioc identifier `PAP11116`;
- the English-language *Haiti: Her History and Her Detractors*, Internet Archive identifier `haitiherhistoryh00lguoft`.

The relevant printed pages were checked as page images. OCR was used to locate the passages and was not treated as the quotation source. The two editions have different pagination and a small substantive difference in the Killick passage: the English edition specifies “on deck”; the French edition gives no location for the seat. [French ed., title page and p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [English ed., title page and p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

## 1. Léger's account of Killick's death

### French edition: verbatim text

The French text appears on printed page 252. The passage begins after Léger explains that Firmin had gone to Gonaïves aboard the *Crête-à-Pierrot*, that Killick remained there with him, that Killick took the arms and ammunition from the German steamer *Markomania*, and that Berlin regarded the seizure as piracy. Léger then writes:

> Le navire de guerre allemand “Panther” arriva le 6 Septembre 1902 aux Gonaïves où se trouvait la “Crête-à-Pierrot.” Son commandant demanda que le bateau haïtien lui fut livré dans cinq minutes. Surpris, Killick ne put opposer aucune résistance; il réclama un délai d'un quart d'heure. Il fit débarquer son équipage, mit le feu à une mèche qui aboutissait à la poudrière, alluma un cigare et s'assit pour attendre l'explosion qui ne tarda pas à se produire. Il préféra disparaître avec son navire que de le livrer aux Allemands.
>
> La tragique mort de Killick et la perte de la “Crête-à-Pierrot” enlevaient toute chance de succès à la protestation de Firmin. Ce dernier quitta les Gonaïves le 15 Octobre et se rendit à Inague.

—Jacques Nicolas Léger, *Haïti: son histoire et ses détracteurs* (1907), printed p. 252. [Scanned edition](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf).

### Faithful rendering

> The German warship *Panther* arrived at Gonaïves on 6 September 1902, where the *Crête-à-Pierrot* was located. Her commander demanded that the Haitian ship be delivered to him within five minutes. Taken by surprise, Killick could offer no resistance; he requested a quarter-hour's delay. He had his crew disembark, set fire to a fuse that led to the powder magazine, lit a cigar, and sat down to wait for the explosion, which was not long in occurring. He preferred to perish with his ship rather than deliver it to the Germans.
>
> Killick's tragic death and the loss of the *Crête-à-Pierrot* removed every chance of success from Firmin's protest. Firmin left Gonaïves on 15 October and went to Inagua.

The rendering translates *disparaître avec son navire* as “perish with his ship” in this context. Léger's sentence literally uses “disappear with his ship.” [French ed., p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf).

### Parallel English edition: verbatim text

The English edition supplies the deck detail:

> Her captain demanded that within five minutes the Haitian ship be delivered to him. Killick, thoroughly taken by surprise, was incapable of offering any resistance; he requested to be allowed fifteen minutes. Sending his crew ashore he lighted a fuse connecting with the powder magazine; having done this, he seated himself on deck, lit a cigar, and quietly awaited the explosion, which was not long in taking place. Rather than give her up to the Germans, he preferred to sacrifice his life in the destruction of his ship. The tragic death of Killick and the loss of the *Crête-à-Pierrot* left no chance of success to Firmin's cause. In consequence the latter sailed from Gonaives on the 15th of October and went to Inagua.

—Jacques Nicolas Léger, *Haiti: Her History and Her Detractors* (1907), printed p. 253. [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

The English phrase “on deck” has no equivalent in the French sentence “s'assit pour attendre l'explosion.” Neither edition places Killick on the bow. [French ed., p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [English ed., p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

## 2. Direct comparison with the United States legation account

Powell's narrative reads:

> Killick, on reaching the deck of the Crête, saw that it was useless to fight, so ordered the officers and crew ashore, and at the same time directed three kegs of powder, some large cartridges and a can of kerosene oil to be placed in his room. After most of the men had left the ship Killick retired to his cabin with two men. The last thing he was seen to do was to light a cigar, fire this train, and take a seat in a chair, dressed in his uniform. Before the boats could reach the shore an explosion was heard; the rear portion or officers' quarters of the ship was destroyed.

—W. F. Powell to John Hay, 13 September 1902, in *Papers Relating to the Foreign Relations of the United States, 1902*, doc. 653, printed p. 657. [Official transcription](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

The four requested points compare as follows:

The same dispatch's later parenthesis—“the other five have not yet been found”—prevents treatment of FRUS document 653 as a consistent two-man casualty account. Peter Sarthou's enclosed letter gives three other deaths: “We are all saved except Dr. Coles and two stewards.” [FRUS, pp. 657–58, 660](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

## 3. Does Léger support the manuscript, FRUS, or neither?

Léger supports these elements of the manuscript: Killick dismissed the crew; he lit a fuse leading to the powder magazine; he lit a cigar; he sat and awaited the explosion; he died in the destruction of the ship. The English edition locates his seat somewhere on deck. [Léger, French ed., p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [Léger, English ed., p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

Léger supplies no support for the Haitian flag, the bow, or four crew members. He supplies no casualty count. [Léger, French ed., p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [Léger, English ed., p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

Léger supports several elements of Powell's version: the cigar, the prepared fuse or train, the seated wait, and Killick's deliberate destruction of the ship. Léger does not support Powell's cabin, chair, uniform, or two-man detail. The English edition's “on deck” conflicts with Powell's cabin. [Léger, English ed., p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft); [FRUS, p. 657](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

The manuscript's complete wording is therefore supported by neither Léger nor FRUS. It combines Léger's fuse/cigar/seated sequence with a flag detail found in a later Haitian source and a four-person count absent from Léger and disputed by the opened 1902 accounts. The word “bow” remains unsupported in every primary and Haitian history passage opened for this report. [Léger FR, p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [Léger EN, p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft); [FRUS, pp. 657–61](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653); [Dorsainvil, p. 332](https://beta-omk.manioc.org/files/original/2821/PAP11077.pdf); [*Savannah Morning News*, p. 1](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn89053684/1902-09-11/ed-1/seq-1/).

## 4. Léger on Môle Saint-Nicolas and Firmin

Léger gives the affair substantial treatment. In the French edition, he says that President Harrison, acting through Secretary of State Blaine, sent Rear Admiral Bancroft Gherardi to negotiate the acquisition of Môle Saint-Nicolas as a naval station. An American squadron with more than one hundred guns and two thousand men supported the negotiations. Léger says that the display provoked national protest and hardened President Hyppolite's position. [Léger, French ed., pp. 243–44](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf). The English edition gives the same account on printed pp. 245–46. [Léger, English ed., pp. 245–46](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

Léger's direct account of Firmin's role in the English edition reads:

> Rear-Admiral Gherardi was in so great a hurry to win that which he imagined would be an easy success, that he did not think it necessary to secure the cooperation of Mr. Frederick Douglass, who was at that time United States Minister at Port-au-Prince; he alone signed the letter. Mr. A. Firmin, then Haitian Secretary of State for Exterior Relations, availed himself at once of this blunder to request the credentials of the Rear-Admiral, who, not being provided with any, was obliged to write to Washington for them. When President Harrison's letter appointing Bancroft Gherardi his special Commissioner reached Port-au-Prince, public opinion was in such a state of excitement by the protracted sojourn of the powerful white squadron in Haitian waters, that it would have been impossible for President Hyppolite even so much as to attempt to grant the slightest advantage to the United States. The Secretary for Exterior Relations clung tenaciously to the Constitution, which forbids the alienation of any portion of the territory. This ended the matter.

—Léger, English ed., printed p. 246. [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

The French text is equally explicit:

> Le Secrétaire d'Etat des Relations Extérieures d'Haïti, Mr. A. Firmin, profita de cette faute pour réclamer les lettres de créance du contre-amiral; ce dernier n'en avait point. Il lui fallut bien les demander à Washington. [...] Le Secrétaire d'Etat des Relations Extérieures se retrancha derrière la Constitution qui défend toute cession de territoire et les pourparlers furent rompus.

—Léger, French ed., printed p. 244. [Manioc scan](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf).

Faithful rendering: “Haiti's Secretary of State for Foreign Relations, Mr. A. Firmin, took advantage of this error to request the rear admiral's credentials; the latter had none. He therefore had to request them from Washington. [...] The Secretary of State for Foreign Relations took his stand on the Constitution, which forbids any cession of territory, and the negotiations were broken off.” [Léger, French ed., p. 244](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf).

Léger therefore supports the central Firmin claim: Firmin identified Gherardi's lack of credentials, demanded them, forced a request to Washington, and then relied on the constitutional prohibition against alienating territory. Léger attributes the collapse of the negotiation to the credentials delay, the public reaction to the American squadron, and Firmin's constitutional position. Léger's passage gives no February 20 or April 24 date and does not quote a formal refusal based on an inability to negotiate freely under coercion. Those more specific manuscript claims require another source. [Léger, French ed., pp. 243–44](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [Léger, English ed., pp. 245–46](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft).

## 5. Other opened sources on Killick's death

### Peter Sarthou, Killick's private secretary, 9 September 1902

Sarthou's letter was printed as an enclosure to Powell's dispatch:

> Seeing himself defenseless, unable to make any resistance, he decided to put the crew on shore and blow up the ship. Seeing a German boat approaching with armed men to take possession of the ship, he bid adieu to the officers, entered his cabin, and blew up the afterpart the ship. [...] We are all saved except Dr. Coles and two stewards.

—Peter Sarthou to Mr. Terres, Gonaïves, 9 September 1902, in FRUS 1902, doc. 653, printed p. 660. [Official transcription](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

Sarthou supports the cabin and afterpart, names three other dead, and gives no flag, bow, cigar, chair, uniform, or precise ignition mechanism. [FRUS, p. 660](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

### Anténor Firmin's proclamation, 6 September 1902

Firmin's same-day proclamation states:

> Our vessel, taken by surprise, was not able to defend itself; Admiral Killick has immortalized himself in blowing it up. He has met the death of the brave.

—Anténor Firmin, proclamation at Gonaïves, 6 September 1902, translated in FRUS 1902, doc. 653, printed p. 658. [Official transcription](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

Firmin confirms Killick's deliberate destruction of the ship and supplies none of the disputed descriptive details. [FRUS, pp. 658–59](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

### Associated Press investigation from Gonaïves, reported 10 September 1902

The *Savannah Morning News* printed an Associated Press dispatch on page 1 of its 11 September issue:

> An investigation made by a correspondent of the Associated Press into the sinking of the Firminist gunboat Crete-a-Pierrot at the entrance of this harbor Saturday, shows that Admiral Killick, her commander, personally fired the after magazine of that vessel when the German gunboat Panther attempted to capture her, and that he apparently went down with his ship. [...] The surgeon of the Crete-a-Pierrot, who was asleep in his cabin, and two sailors of that vessel also perished.

—“Went Down With Vessel,” *Savannah Morning News*, 11 September 1902, p. 1. [Page image and OCR](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn89053684/1902-09-11/ed-1/seq-1/).

This period report gives three deaths in addition to Killick: the surgeon and two sailors. It places the surgeon in his cabin and says Killick “personally fired the after magazine.” It supplies no flag, bow, fuse, cigar, uniform, or chair. [*Savannah Morning News*, 11 September 1902, p. 1](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn89053684/1902-09-11/ed-1/seq-1/).

### J.-C. Dorsainvil, 1934

Dorsainvil supplies the flag detail in a Haitian history published thirty-two years after the event:

> (6 septembre 1902), l'amiral Killick, souffrant, était à terre. Il se transporta immédiatement à son bord, renvoya l'équipage entier, sauf le docteur Coles, qui voulut partager son sort; puis, s'enveloppant du drapeau national, il tira dans la poudrière: la Crête-à-Pierrot sauta.

—J.-C. Dorsainvil, *Manuel d'histoire d'Haïti* (1934), printed p. 332. [Manioc scan](https://beta-omk.manioc.org/files/original/2821/PAP11077.pdf).

Faithful rendering: “On 6 September 1902, Admiral Killick, who was ill, was ashore. He immediately went aboard, dismissed the entire crew except Dr. Coles, who wished to share his fate; then, wrapping himself in the national flag, he fired into the powder room: the *Crête-à-Pierrot* exploded.” [Dorsainvil, p. 332](https://beta-omk.manioc.org/files/original/2821/PAP11077.pdf).

Dorsainvil supports the flag. He does not support the bow, the fuse, or four companions. His method is a gunshot into the powder room, and his account names one companion, Dr. Coles. The book's title page says that the work was approved by Haiti's Council of Public Instruction on 29 March 1924; the opened 1934 edition does not establish that this exact sentence was present in a 1924 or 1925 printing. [Dorsainvil, title page and p. 332](https://beta-omk.manioc.org/files/original/2821/PAP11077.pdf).

### Status of the flag detail

The flag detail appears in at least one source that was opened: Dorsainvil's 1934 Haitian school history. It is absent from the opened 1902 accounts by Powell, Sarthou, Firmin, and the Associated Press, and from both Léger editions published in 1907. The evidence recovered here places the detail in print by 1934 and leaves its origin unresolved. Relative to the opened 1902 and 1907 record, it is a later accretion. [FRUS, pp. 657–61](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653); [*Savannah Morning News*, p. 1](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn89053684/1902-09-11/ed-1/seq-1/); [Léger FR, p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [Léger EN, p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft); [Dorsainvil, p. 332](https://beta-omk.manioc.org/files/original/2821/PAP11077.pdf).

## Source-level conclusion

The most defensible reconstruction from the opened evidence is limited to points shared by Léger and the contemporary record: Killick ordered the crew off the ship, deliberately initiated an explosion, and died aboard rather than surrender the vessel. Léger adds a fuse to the powder magazine, a cigar, and a seated wait. Powell adds a cabin, chair, uniform, and a prepared train of powder, cartridges, and kerosene. Sarthou adds the cabin and identifies Dr. Coles and two stewards. The Associated Press also gives three additional deaths. Dorsainvil later adds the national flag, describes the ignition as a shot into the powder room, and names only Dr. Coles. [Léger FR, p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [Léger EN, p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft); [FRUS, pp. 657–61](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653); [*Savannah Morning News*, p. 1](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn89053684/1902-09-11/ed-1/seq-1/); [Dorsainvil, p. 332](https://beta-omk.manioc.org/files/original/2821/PAP11077.pdf).

The manuscript's “flag + bow + fuse + four crew members” formulation is a composite that no opened source contains. Léger supports the fuse and seated wait. Dorsainvil supports the flag. The four-person count and bow remain unsupported by the opened primary and Haitian sources. [Léger FR, p. 252](https://manioc.univ-antilles.fr/omeka-s/files/original/7627/PAP11116.pdf); [Léger EN, p. 253](https://archive.org/details/haitiherhistoryh00lguoft); [FRUS, pp. 657–61](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653); [*Savannah Morning News*, p. 1](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn89053684/1902-09-11/ed-1/seq-1/); [Dorsainvil, p. 332](https://beta-omk.manioc.org/files/original/2821/PAP11077.pdf).
