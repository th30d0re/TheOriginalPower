## 1. Direct answer to Question 1

The evidence supports: **Morton's measurements were largely sound; the void sat in the
inference, not in the data.** Lewis et al. physically remeasured 308 of the 670 skulls
whose capacities Morton had published (46%) and found only 7 (2%) diverging significantly,
with errors distributed randomly across populations; the single non-random pattern (3 of 13
Egyptian crania *over*estimated) ran against the direction Gould's bias hypothesis predicts,
since Morton classified those skulls as "Negro" [web:
https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1001071]. Lewis et al.
also showed that Gould's best-known subsidiary charges collapse on inspection: Morton did
report "Indian" subsample means (at least 12 times in *Crania Americana*, including the
Iroquois mean Gould claimed was hidden); the 1839→1849 "Indian" mean "plummet" was 0.3
in³ (79.6→79.3); the grouped mean of the Native American sample (79.9) is nearly identical
to the straight mean (80.2); and Gould's own reanalysis contains material errors (his
Caucasian mean of 85 should be 87; he omitted 34 of 144 crania) [web: same]. Gould's
specific data-fudging charge is therefore **not supported** — confidence: high.
Two qualifications keep this from being a clean exoneration. First, Gould's core
bias argument rested on the *differential seed-to-shot shift* (African mean +5.4 in³
vs. Caucasian +1.8 in³ between the 1839 and later measurements), and Weisberg & Paul
(2016) point out that remeasuring the shot-era skulls — measurements Gould himself called
"objective, accurate, and repeatable" — cannot logically adjudicate a charge about the
1839 seed measurements [web: https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002444];
Lewis et al. countered that the seed-to-shot reconstruction is itself unreliable (assistant
error admitted by Morton, ~50% sample turnover, individual shifts from −10 to +12 in³
unpatterned by group) [web: Lewis et al. 2011]. That residual dispute is genuinely open —
confidence: moderate. Second, Kaplan, Pigliucci & Batta (2015) argue that *both* analyses
are inappropriate for the questions asked: given how the collection was assembled, no
interesting population question can be answered from it [web:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1369848615000035 — abstract,
introduction, and section snippets read; body claims beyond that **unverified**].
What no party defends is the inference itself: Lewis et al. explicitly concede that human
variation is continuous, mostly within-population, and that cranial capacity variation tracks
climate rather than any hierarchy of worth [web: Lewis et al. 2011]. Morton's racist
commitments and the polygenist purpose of the enterprise are undisputed — Lewis et al.
themselves affirm that Morton "believed in the concept of race and assigned a plethora of
different attributes to various groups, often in highly racist fashion" [web: same].
**Bottom line for the book: the data were not fabricated; the measurement project was
legitimate as metrology and void as an index of intelligence. The "fraud" belongs to the
inference and to the institution that commissioned it.**

## 2. What Firmin actually claimed

Method first: **Firmin made no physical skull measurements.** He audited the published
tables of others — Topinard, Broca, Morton, Barnard Davis, Mantegazza — working through
their own printed numbers [source: Firmin 1885, *De l'égalité des races humaines*, ch. VI
("Classement artificiel des races humaines"), printed pp. ~135–160, BnF scan
`Antnor1885Bnf30437548r` on archive.org; corroborated by Fluehr-Lobban, web:
https://www.berose.fr/article1424.html]. (The task brief said "Firmin did his own
measurements"; the primary text and the secondary literature both say otherwise. The
manuscript at :2846 — "a comprehensive positivist audit of the SAP's own data" — has it
right.)

His critique has three distinct layers, and only one of them touches Morton directly:

1. **Instrument unreliability.** Firmin quotes Broca's own admission that having the same
   skull "cubed" twice — even by the same measurer — yields differences of more than 50
   cubic centimeters [source: Firmin 1885, printed p. ~135, quoting Broca, *Mémoires
   d'anthropologie* t. IV, p. 5]. The measurement operation, on the practitioners' own
   account, did not reproduce.
2. **Absence of signal in the published numbers.** Reading table after table, Firmin finds
   the groups interleaved: the Auvergnats sit above the Parisians; Broca's own table puts
   the Australian group above the Nubian — "en contradiction flagrante" with every
   anthropologist's ranking of those two [source: Firmin 1885, printed p. ~138]. His
   summary verdict: "les mesures anthropologiques... sont non-seulement trompeuses,
   irrégulières, mais le plus souvent contradictoires" ("the anthropological measurements
   are not only deceptive, irregular, but most often contradictory"), and craniometry
   offered "impossible de trouver une base sérieuse de classification" [source: Firmin
   1885, printed pp. ~156–158]. Of Barnard Davis's list, "faite sans esprit de système,"
   he notes that Chinese, Dahomeans, and Marquesans surpass the Anglo-Saxons and are
   surpassed by Kanaks — "aucun caractère fixe, pouvant conduire à une classification
   exacte" [source: Firmin 1885, printed p. ~139].
3. **A motive critique aimed at Morton's table specifically.** "Quant au tableau tiré des
   moyennes de Morton, on sait ce qu'il faut en penser. L'esprit suivant lequel tous les
   savants de l'école américaine, sauf de rares exceptions, considéraient l'anthropologie,
   rend toutes leurs affirmations suspectes. Ils n'y voyaient qu'un moyen de légitimer le
   système de l'esclavage" ("As for the table drawn from Morton's averages, we know what
   to think of it. The spirit in which all the scholars of the American school, with rare
   exceptions, regarded anthropology renders all their assertions suspect. They saw in it
   only a means of legitimating the system of slavery") [source: Firmin 1885, printed
   pp. ~138–139]. This is suspicion grounded in institutional motive — **not a demonstrated
   falsification of individual measurements.**

So: Firmin alleged **absence of signal plus unreliable instrumentation**, and he flagged
Morton's school as motive-compromised. He did not allege — and did not attempt to prove —
that the numbers were fabricated. **His claim matches [B], not [A].** The manuscript's
word "anarchic" (in quotation marks at :2848, cited to `firmin_legacy`) does **not** appear
in the 1885 original — a full-text search of the BnF scan finds no instance of "anarchie"
or any cognate [kimi-inferred from exhaustive grep of the OCR — verify against the 2000
English translation, which may render Firmin's "confusion" as "anarchy" somewhere; the
nearest verifiable Firmin wordings are "trompeuses, irrégulières... contradictoires,"
"océan de doute et de confusion," and "la plus grande confusion règne dans les chiffres"
(the last about limb-proportion data)]. The quote marks around "anarchic" should be
treated as unverified against the primary source.

## 3. Is [A] defensible, and its replacement

**No.** "These studies, though methodologically fraudulent" asserts corrupted data. The
strongest available evidence — a physical remeasurement by anatomists with the skulls in
hand — says the data were generally reliable and the errors random [web: Lewis et al.
2011]. The claim survives nowhere in the current literature, including among Lewis et
al.'s critics, who attack the remeasurement's *relevance*, not its result [web: Weisberg &
Paul 2016; Kaplan et al. 2015]. What remains true, and what the sentence needs to carry:
Morton's project was conscripted to a racist inference, and the numbers never yielded the
hierarchy they were published to prove — which is exactly what [B] says Firmin found, and
what Lewis et al. concede.

Note that the companion sentence at :2844 ("the data the Elite's institutions were
producing was analytically fraudulent") is defensible as written *if* "analytically" is read
as locating the fraud in the analysis rather than the measurement — that reading is the one
this report supports, and it should be kept consistent with the :2763 replacement.

Recommended replacement for `Paper/The_Original_Power.tex:2763`, exact LaTeX, in the
book's voice (direct affirmative declaratives; no corrective-contrast constructions):

```latex
    \item \textbf{Craniology and phrenology}: Researchers like Samuel Morton measured skulls to ``prove'' African intellectual inferiority. The measurements themselves were largely sound---a physical remeasurement of 308 of the 670 skulls whose capacities Morton published found his data generally reliable and his errors random with respect to population \cite{lewis_morton}. The void lay in the inference: cranial capacity provided no index of the intellectual hierarchy it was conscripted to prove, and the cranial tables overlapped so completely across populations that no hierarchy could be extracted from them \cite{gould}. Presented as quantitative ``evidence'' that racialization reflected natural biological differences, these studies naturalized a hierarchy their own numbers did not contain.
```

This keeps the sentence load-bearing for the chapter's argument: the Elite's scientific
apparatus generated real numbers whose claimed meaning was empty — which is *stronger*
for the book's framework than a falsified-data claim, because it matches the Firmin section
verbatim and converts Morton from a cautionary tale about bad measurement into evidence
that the legitimation apparatus manufactures signal out of noise by fiat.

## 5. Consequence for the Firmin counter-signal extension (Lewontin 1972, Rosenberg 2002, Human Genome Project)

**Proceed.** This resolution makes the extension cleaner, and removes a landmine that
would have detonated under it. The argument the author plans — Firmin's "no signal in the
cranial tables" extended to Lewontin's apportionment of human genetic diversity
(1972), Rosenberg's cluster analysis under clinal sampling (2002), and the HGP's
single-species finding — is the *same* argument at three later magnifications: each
generation of the legitimation apparatus's own instruments, read without prior, returns
overlap instead of hierarchy. That continuity holds only if the book's claim is
absence-of-signal ([B]), which is what Firmin actually wrote and what the Morton
literature now supports. Had [A] stood ("the data were fraudulent"), the extension would
have inherited a falsified premise: an informed reader citing Lewis et al. 2011 could have
dismissed the whole counter-signal section as built on Gould's discredited charge.

One caution for the extension, in the book's own vocabulary: keep the signal/noise framing
uniform. Firmin found no classificatory signal in *craniometric* data; Lewontin found the
between-group component small relative to the within-group component; Rosenberg found
clusters that dissolve under continuous sampling. These are three distinct quantitative
statements. Do not flatten them into one number, and do not let "anarchic" stand in quote
marks unless it is verified against the 2000 translation — otherwise the counter-signal
section quotes a word its source never printed.
