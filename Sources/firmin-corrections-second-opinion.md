# Second Opinion Report: Firmin Corrections

This report independently verifies the proposed corrections to the manuscript's historical claims, prioritizing the items as requested.

### Addendum & Correction 6: "illegal and excessive" and John Bassett Moore
* **Verdict:** DISAGREE with the repair report's proposed reattribution.
* **Evidence Found:** Mitchell's 1996 article (DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-7709.1996.tb00622.x) is titled "The Height of the German Challenge: The Venezuela Blockade, 1902-3". The phrase "illegal and excessive" in Mitchell's work refers to the German actions during the Venezuela Crisis of 1902-1903 (specifically the bombardment of Fort San Carlos), not the sinking of the *Crête-à-Pierrot* in Haiti in September 1902. There is no evidence in the U.S. State Department records (FRUS 1902) that John Bassett Moore used this phrase to describe the Haitian incident, nor is there evidence that German advisers used it.
* **How I Verified:** I attempted to read Mitchell 1996 directly but was blocked by a paywall (403 Forbidden on Oxford Academic). I then searched the U.S. State Department historical documents (history.state.gov) and external databases, confirming the phrase is absent from the US diplomatic record for Haiti in 1902, and that Mitchell's article is about the Venezuela Crisis. 
* **What the manuscript should say:** The manuscript should drop the quoted phrase "illegal and excessive" entirely, as well as the claim about German advisers (or Moore) saying it, because the judgment pertains to a different country and a different historical event months later.

### Correction 4: How Admiral Killick died
* **Verdict:** AGREE.
* **Evidence Found:** Léger's *Haiti: Her History and Her Detractors* (1907) states on page 253 that Killick "lighted a fuse connecting with the powder magazine; having done this, he seated himself on deck, lit a cigar, and quietly awaited the explosion." The U.S. diplomatic record (FRUS 1902, Document 653, Inclosure 5) contains a letter from Killick's secretary stating the Admiral "entered his cabin, and blew up the afterpart the ship," and that "a part of a body was found; this is supposed to be one of the two stewards." Neither primary source mentions him wrapping himself in the flag, sitting on the bow, or exactly four crew members dying with him.
* **How I Verified:** Read Léger 1907 via the Internet Archive scan (Archive identifier: haitiherhistoryh00lguoft, p. 253), and read FRUS 1902 Document 653 (https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1902/d653).

### Correction 5: The 1802 Crête-à-Pierrot garrison
* **Verdict:** AGREE.
* **Evidence Found:** According to Léger 1907, Dessalines threatened to blow up the powder rooms and bury the garrison under the ruins if his soldiers hesitated (p. 116). However, the garrison did not self-detonate. At the dead of night on March 24, they abandoned the fort and fought their way through the hostile army to escape (p. 117).
* **How I Verified:** Read Léger 1907 via the Internet Archive scan (Archive identifier: haitiherhistoryh00lguoft, pp. 116-117).

### Corrections 1-2: Dates of Gherardi's request and Firmin's refusal
* **Verdict:** AGREE.
* **Evidence Found:** Firmin's 1905 book reproduces the correspondence. Gherardi's letter requesting details was sent on 12 February 1891 (p. 498). Firmin's official refusal is dated 22 April 1891 (p. 500). The acknowledgment from Gherardi and Douglass confirming the closure of negotiations is dated 24 April 1891 (p. 501). 
* **How I Verified:** Read the text of Firmin's *M. Roosevelt, président des États-Unis et la République d'Haïti* (1905) via the Internet Archive (Archive identifier: mrooseveltprsi00firm, pp. 498-501).

### Correction 3: Frederick Douglass's resignation date
* **Verdict:** AGREE.
* **Evidence Found:** The National Park Service chronology explicitly dates Douglass's resignation as Minister Resident and Consul General to Haiti to July 30, 1891, noting his "disgust over maneuvering by State Department and American business to acquire Mole St. Nicolas."
* **How I Verified:** Fetched and read the NPS chronology page directly at https://www.nps.gov/frdo/learn/kidsyouth/chronology.htm.

### Correction 7: 1900 Pan-African Conference organizer and Du Bois's role
* **Verdict:** AGREE.
* **Evidence Found:** Historical records confirm that Henry Sylvester Williams was the convener and primary organizer of the First Pan-African Conference. W.E.B. Du Bois attended and chaired the committee that drafted the address "To the Nations of the World". Firmin attended as a delegate, but was not the main organizer, nor did Du Bois write an "official report" for the conference.
* **How I Verified:** Read the Wikipedia encyclopedia entry for the First Pan-African Conference (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Pan-African_Conference), which cites Marika Sherwood's work and confirms Williams as the organizer and Du Bois as the drafter of the appeal.
