# Is Capitalism the Problem

**Resolution as it will be phrased:** "Is capitalism the problem?" / "Capitalism vs socialism"

---

## Never argue

Against **capitalism as a whole**.

The moment you do, they attach you to the USSR, Venezuela, and a hundred million deaths,
and you spend the rest of the debate on defense. This is the single easiest trap in the
entire deck to fall into, and it is unwinnable in 20-second turns.

**Fix:** split the category. **Market competition** and **rent extraction** are different
machines wearing the same word. Defend the first one loudly. Prosecute the second. Force
them to either defend rent-seeking — which loses them the room — or concede the
distinction, which gives you the entire argument.

---

## Hook (10s, no stats)

> "I'm not arguing for socialism. I'm arguing that 'capitalism' names about five different
> systems, and we keep defending one of them while living under a different one."

---

## Core (20s)

> "Markets are great. Competition is great. A landlord raising rent because the city built
> a train station didn't produce anything — that's a toll, not a business. Four companies
> control eighty percent of domestic airline seats. Three control ninety percent of baby
> formula. That's not a market, that's a condition. So let's separate the producers from
> the toll collectors. I'll defend the producers all day. You defend the toll collectors."

That last move is the whole card. Handing them the toll collectors to defend is a position
nobody wants and nobody can hold.

## Counters

**1. "Socialism killed a hundred million people."**
> "Not defending it. Never mentioned it. I'm asking why your groceries cost more when four
> companies own the supply chain."

**2. "Competition solves this on its own."**
> "Competition requires competitors. When four firms hold eighty percent, the thing you're
> counting on to fix it is the thing that's already gone."

**3. "Capitalism lifted a billion people out of poverty."**
> "Most of that is China, which did it through state-directed industrial policy and capital
> controls. If that's your headline example, you've just made an argument for the state."

**4. "Regulation is what creates monopolies."**
> "Antitrust enforcement collapsed after 1980 and concentration rose in almost every
> sector. That runs the opposite direction from your theory."

**5. "Prices are up because of inflation / supply chains."**
> "Then margins would compress. Margins hit records. When costs rise and margins rise
> together, you're looking at pricing power, not costs."

**6. "If you don't like it, start a company."**
> "In which of those four-firm sectors? That's the point — the entry is what got bought."

---

## Close

> "I want more capitalism, not less. I want actual competition instead of four companies
> and a toll booth. What we have now is a system that collects rent on things it didn't
> build and calls the collection a market."

---

## If you only have 10 seconds

> "Markets and monopoly rent are opposite things sharing one word. I'll defend markets.
> Go ahead and defend the rent."
