# Should Billionaires Exist

**Resolution as it will be phrased:** "Should billionaires exist?" / "Is it moral to be a billionaire?"

---

## Never argue

"The more they own, the less is left for us."

True, and it loses. It reads as a fixed-pie claim, and *"the economy isn't zero-sum"* is
the most rehearsed counter in this entire debate. Your opponent has it loaded before you
finish the sentence. You then spend your next two turns defending a technicality instead
of prosecuting your case.

**Fix:** argue the **rate**, not the pile. Concede that the pie grows, then compare growth
to absorption. The moment you do, their best weapon becomes your opening premise.

---

## Hook (10s, no stats)

> "A million seconds is eleven days. A billion seconds is thirty-one years. People hear
> 'millionaire' and 'billionaire' as neighbors. They are not in the same universe. If you
> can't feel that gap, you can't reason about it."

Open with this. It costs ten seconds, uses no data, and recalibrates the room before you
make a single economic claim.

---

## Core (20s)

> "I don't care how big the pie gets. I care about the rates. Wealth compounds for owners
> at five to seven percent a year. The actual economy grows at about two. When the claim
> grows three times faster than the thing being claimed, the remainder shrinks — every
> year, automatically, whether anyone intends it or not. A billionaire isn't someone who
> won. A billionaire is proof the absorption rate beat the expansion rate."

**Falsifiable challenge to hand them:** "Show me the decade where the bottom half's share
of wealth went up. There isn't one."

That challenge is the strongest sentence on this card. It converts you from someone with
an opinion into someone with a testable claim, and it puts the burden on them.

---

## Numbers

Say these as approximations. ⚠ = verify before going live.

| Claim | Figure |
|---|---|
| Return on capital vs. growth (Piketty, `r > g`) | ~5% vs ~2% |
| US top 1% wealth share, 1989 → now ⚠ | ~23% → ~31% |
| US bottom 50% wealth share | ~2–3%, flat for 35 years |
| Effective federal rate, 25 wealthiest, on wealth growth ⚠ | ~3.4% (ProPublica, 2021) |
| US median household wealth ⚠ | ~$190k (SCF 2022) |

The 3.4% is your kill shot on "they earned it." A $100B fortune is roughly 500,000x the
median household — that ratio is worth having ready.

---

## Counters

**1. "The pie grows — it's not zero-sum."**
> "Agreed. It grows at two percent. Their claim grows at six. Which number is bigger?"

**2. "They earned it. They created value."**
> "Nobody earns a billion. Wages don't do that — no salary, no hours, no talent gets you
> there. Billions come from asset appreciation: holding a claim while other people's labor
> makes it worth more. Bezos never had a billion-dollar paycheck. He had a claim."

**3. "Taxing them kills innovation."**
> "We ran that experiment. Top rate was 90% in the fifties and 70% through the seventies.
> That's the interstate system, the moon landing, the transistor, the internet. Innovation
> did fine. What we've had since is forty years of concentration and slower productivity growth."

**4. "It's not their fault poor people are poor."**
> "It's not about fault. It's about flow. They don't have to intend anything. The rate does
> the work."

**5. "They give it away — philanthropy."**
> "Philanthropy is the owner choosing where the money goes instead of the public choosing.
> That's not a return of the wealth. That's the last stage of controlling it. And it's tax
> deductible."

**6. "Buy-borrow-die" — deploy proactively if they claim billionaires pay taxes:**
> "They don't sell, so there's no taxable event. They borrow against the asset at two
> percent and live on the loan. Then it passes at a stepped-up basis and the gain is never
> taxed at all. That's not avoidance at the margins. That's the whole design."

---

## Close

> "I'm not asking whether billionaires are good people. I'm asking whether a system where
> claims compound faster than the economy grows can produce anything other than this. It
> can't. Billionaires aren't the villain of the story. They're the readout — the number on
> the dial telling you the extraction rate exceeded the growth rate."

This lands the framework without ever naming it.

---

## If you only have 10 seconds

> "The pie grows at two percent. The claim on it grows at six. That's the whole argument,
> and it's arithmetic, not politics."
