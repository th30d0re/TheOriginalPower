# Is Systemic Racism Real

**Resolution as it will be phrased:** "Does systemic racism exist?" / "Is America a racist country?"

---

## Never argue

From **intent** ("people are racist") or from **disparity alone** ("look at the gap").

Intent is unprovable and invites *"you can't read minds."* Disparity alone invites
*"correlation isn't causation"* and *"maybe it's culture."* Both hand them a defensible
position and turn the debate into your character versus theirs.

**Fix:** argue from **design history**. Racism has a birthdate, a documented trigger, and
a legislative paper trail. Never make a claim about a feeling when you can make one about
a statute.

---

## Hook (10s, no stats)

> "Systemic racism isn't a feeling. It has a birthdate. 1676."

Say it flat and stop talking. The pause does the work — nobody expects a date.

---

## Core (20s)

> "Bacon's Rebellion, Virginia, 1676. Black slaves and white indentured servants rebelled
> together and burned Jamestown. The colonial elite nearly lost everything. Their response
> wasn't more force. Within thirty years Virginia wrote the 1705 slave codes, which handed
> poor whites legal privileges they'd never had — property rights, the right to testify,
> immunity from enslavement — specifically to make that coalition impossible again.
> Whiteness was invented as a wage. That's a documented legislative response to a
> documented coalition threat."

**The reframe that wins the whole exchange:**
> "Race isn't the reason for the extraction. Race is the tool that prevents the coalition
> that would stop it."

That sentence answers *"why do you make everything about race"* better than any defense:
**"I don't. I'm making it about class. Race is the mechanism that keeps you from noticing."**

---

## Numbers

Say these as approximations. ⚠ = verify before going live.

| Claim | Figure |
|---|---|
| Virginia Slave Codes | 1705 |
| Naturalization Act — citizenship for "free white persons" | 1790 |
| Social Security Act excluded agricultural + domestic labor ⚠ | 1935; ~65% of Black workers |
| FHA loans to white families, 1934–1962 ⚠ | ~98% |
| White vs Black median household wealth ⚠ | ~$285k vs ~$45k (SCF 2022) |

**The point that closes it:** Black and white households *at the same income* hold wildly
different wealth. Income is what you earn. Wealth is what was legislated to your
grandparents and compounded.

---

## Counters

**1. "We passed the Civil Rights Act. That's over."**
> "Redlining ran from 1934 to 1968. A thirty-year head start compounding at five percent
> is roughly a four-times multiple. The law stopped. The compounding didn't."

**2. "Asian Americans do fine, so racism can't be the cause."**
> "That's a selection effect. The 1965 Immigration Act selected for advanced degrees.
> You're comparing a population selected for professional credentials to one selected for
> chattel labor and calling it a control group."

**3. "It's culture, not systems."**
> "Culture didn't write the FHA underwriting manual. It's a document with a date on it.
> You can read it."

**4. "You can't prove anyone intended this."**
> "I don't need intent. I have the statute."

**5. "Slavery existed everywhere. Every culture had it."**
> "Slavery did. *Race* didn't. Slavery in the ancient world tracked conquest and debt, and
> the children could be free. Hereditary racial slavery is a fifteenth-century Portuguese
> invention with paperwork. That's the thing that's new, and that's the thing I'm arguing about."

**6. "Bringing this up divides people."**
> "It was designed to divide people. In 1705. I'm describing the division, not performing it."

## The parallel economy (use on "just work hard" / "property rights protect everyone")

Cross-partisan, and it defuses the "you're making it about race" dismissal before it forms.

> "My opponent says this system protects property and rewards hard work. Watch what happens
> when Black Americans actually did it. Greenwood, Tulsa, 1921 — thirty-five blocks of
> Black-owned business burned, hundreds killed, thousands displaced, and the insurance
> claims were denied under riot clauses. Rosewood, Florida, 1923 — destroyed. These weren't
> failures in the market. They were successes that got erased."

**Then broaden it so they can't file it under race:**
> "And it isn't only race. Ruby Ridge. Waco. When any group builds a life outside the
> system's reach, the response looks the same. The pattern isn't who you are. It's whether
> you can be extracted from."

That second move is the important one. Pairing Greenwood with Waco reaches both halves of
a room at once, and it removes the opponent's easiest exit — dismissing the argument as a
racial grievance rather than a structural claim.

**The cotton gin, for "innovation lifts everyone":**
> "The cotton gin was one of the great inventions in American history. Enslaved population
> in 1790: about seven hundred thousand. By 1860: nearly four million. The technology
> didn't liberate anyone — it made the extraction more efficient and scaled it. Innovation
> doesn't distribute itself. It flows to whoever owns the machine."

Those population figures are undisputed and devastating. Use them rather than any
characterization of what the invention "meant."

---

## Close

> "I'm not accusing you of anything. I'm pointing at a machine. It runs whether or not
> anyone at the controls means harm — that's exactly what makes it systemic instead of
> personal. If it needed racists to operate, we'd have solved it by now."

The last sentence is the most disarming thing on this card. Use it when the room is hostile.

---

## If you only have 10 seconds

> "Poor whites and poor Blacks rebelled together in 1676. Whiteness was written into law
> in 1705 to make sure it never happened again. That's not a theory, it's a statute."
