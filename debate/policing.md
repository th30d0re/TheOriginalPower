# Policing, Prisons, and Criminal Justice

**Resolution as it will be phrased:** "Should we defund the police?" / "Is the justice system racist?"

---

## Never argue

For **abolition**, or the slogan "defund."

The slogan is a branding catastrophe and you will spend every turn explaining what it
doesn't mean instead of what you mean. They want you attached to it. Refuse.

**Fix:** argue that the system **isn't doing the job it claims**. Ask for *more* solved
murders and *fewer* cages for people who haven't been convicted. That position is nearly
impossible to attack, and it still delivers the whole structural argument.

---

## Hook (10s, no stats)

> "The US has four percent of the world's people and about twenty percent of its prisoners.
> Either Americans are uniquely criminal, or the system is doing something other than
> reducing crime."

---

## Core (20s)

> "Most people sitting in jail tonight haven't been convicted of anything — roughly
> two-thirds are pre-trial. They're there because they couldn't post bail. That's a wealth
> filter wearing a justice costume. Meanwhile about half of murders go unsolved and burglary
> clearance is around thirteen percent. So the system cages people who are legally innocent
> and poor, and doesn't solve the crimes that actually hurt people. Those are both failures
> of the same institution."

**The document, if you need the deeper cut:**
> "The Thirteenth Amendment doesn't ban slavery. Read it. 'Neither slavery nor involuntary
> servitude, *except as a punishment for crime.*' That exception is still in the text. The
> Black Codes were written in 1865 to feed it, and convict leasing ran for seventy years.
> The system wasn't reformed, it was renamed."

## Counters

**1. "We need police. Crime is real."**
> "Completely agreed. Half of murders go unsolved. I want more of that work and less of
> the pretrial cages and the thirteen percent burglary clearance. I'm asking for a police
> department that's better at policing."

**2. "But crime rates in those communities are higher."**
> "Those are *arrest* rates. Marijuana use is basically equal across race and the arrest
> rate is roughly three-and-a-half times higher. Same behavior, different enforcement. The
> statistic you're citing is downstream of the thing I'm describing."

That is the most important counter on the card. The measurement is produced by the
enforcement, so it cannot be used as evidence about the underlying behavior.

**3. "Defunding is insane."**
> "I didn't say defund. I'm asking why departments buy armored vehicles and don't solve
> murders. That's a budget priority question, not an abolition question."

**4. "Tough-on-crime worked — crime fell in the nineties."**
> "Crime fell in Canada on the same curve over the same years, without the incarceration
> boom. When the medicine and the placebo produce the same result, it isn't the medicine."

**5. "If you don't commit crimes you have nothing to worry about."**
> "Two-thirds of the jail population hasn't been convicted. Innocence isn't the protection
> you think it is — money is."

**6. "Private prisons are a small share, that's a myth."**
> "True, they're under ten percent. The extraction isn't the prison — it's the fees. Phone
> calls, commissary, e-messaging, supervision fees, court costs. That's billed to the
> poorest families in the country, and it's a real market with real shareholders."

---

## Close

> "Prison is where the extraction goes when there's nothing left to extract. It's the last
> stage of the same system, not a separate institution with separate problems."
