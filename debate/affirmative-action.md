# Affirmative Action / DEI

**Resolution as it will be phrased:** "Should affirmative action exist?" / "Is DEI discrimination?"

---

## Never argue

That it's about **diversity**, **representation**, or **role models**.

Those are soft goods, they're unmeasurable in a 20-second turn, and they hand your opponent
the fairness frame — at which point you're defending a preference against a principle and
you lose.

**Fix:** point out that **every institution already runs preferences**, and always has. The
debate was never about whether to have them. It's about which ones we're permitted to
notice. Take the fairness frame away from them in your first sentence.

---

## Hook (10s, no stats)

> "The largest affirmative action program in American history was the GI Bill, and it went
> overwhelmingly to white men. We just didn't call it that."

---

## Core (20s)

> "Every selective institution in this country has preferences. Legacy. Athletics. Donors.
> Geography. Children of faculty. At Harvard those categories cover roughly forty-three
> percent of white admits, and legacy applicants get in at five or six times the base rate.
> Nobody sued over that. Nobody called it discrimination. So the question was never whether
> we allow preferences. It's which preference we're allowed to see."

---

## Numbers

Say these as approximations. ⚠ = verify before going live.

| Claim | Figure |
|---|---|
| Harvard white admits who are ALDC ⚠ | ~43% |
| Legacy admit rate vs base rate ⚠ | ~5–6x |
| GI Bill administered by states → Black veterans largely excluded | 1944 onward |
| Black/white wealth gap at equal income ⚠ | ~6x |

---

## Counters

**1. "It's discrimination — you can't judge people by race."**
> "Legacy judges people by ancestry. That's the same operation with a better lawyer. Why
> did we litigate one and not the other?"

**2. "Make it class-based instead of race-based."**
> "I'm fine with class-based. It won't close a gap that was created by race-based statutes.
> A Black and a white family at the same income hold about six times different wealth —
> class-based programs can't see that, because income isn't the variable that was targeted."

**3. "It stigmatizes the people it helps."**
> "Nobody has ever worried that a legacy admit feels stigmatized. That concern appears
> exactly once, for exactly one group."

**4. "Mismatch — it sets students up to fail."**
> "That's contested and largely hasn't replicated. Graduation rates went up over the same
> period."

**5. "We should be colorblind."**
> "I'd love that. The statutes weren't. You can't run a colorblind remedy on a color-coded
> injury and expect the arithmetic to work."

**6. "DEI is just corporate box-ticking."**
> "Often, yes. Corporate DEI is mostly a liability shield — training modules that change
> nothing and cost nothing. That's an argument that it's too weak, not too strong."

Conceding #6 is a strength move. It shows you're arguing from analysis rather than team
loyalty, and it buys enormous credibility for everything else on the card.

---

## Close

> "I'll accept ending racial preferences the day we end all of them. Legacy goes first —
> it's bigger, it's older, and it's the one that actually moves the numbers."

---

## If you only have 10 seconds

> "Legacy admissions are five times the advantage and nobody sued. This was never about
> preferences. It was about which preference we're allowed to notice."
