# Reparations

**Resolution as it will be phrased:** "Should Black Americans receive reparations?"

---

## Never argue

From **moral debt**, **guilt**, or **slavery**.

Guilt framing triggers every defense the listener has and makes the debate about whether
they personally did something wrong. And anchoring on slavery hands them the easiest exit
in the room: *"my family arrived in 1920."*

**Fix:** argue it as **accounting**, not atonement. Anchor on **twentieth-century federal
policy**, which is in living memory, is fully documented, and has addresses attached.
Reparations is a receipt, not an apology.

---

## Hook (10s, no stats)

> "The federal government has already paid reparations. Multiple times. Reagan signed one."

They will not see this coming, and it moves the debate from *whether* to *which class*.

---

## Core (20s)

> "1988, Civil Liberties Act — twenty thousand dollars per surviving Japanese American
> internee, signed by Reagan. Same statutory logic. The only question that has ever
> mattered is whether the harm was documented and government-caused. Redlining was federal
> policy with maps. The FHA underwriting manual is a document — it explicitly instructed
> against lending in Black neighborhoods. That's not a moral claim. It's a paper trail."

**Falsifiable challenge to hand them:**
> "Name the part of the wealth gap that doesn't trace to a statute. I'll wait."

---

## Numbers

Say these as approximations. ⚠ = verify before going live.

| Claim | Figure |
|---|---|
| Civil Liberties Act — internment reparations | 1988, $20k/survivor |
| Federal redlining period (HOLC/FHA) | 1934–1968 |
| FHA-insured loans going to white families ⚠ | ~98% |
| Black/white median household wealth gap ⚠ | ~6x (SCF 2022) |
| 2008 bank rescue / 2020 CARES ⚠ | ~$700B / ~$2T |

## Counters

**1. "My family came in 1920. We never owned slaves."**
> "The claim isn't against slavery. It's against federal housing policy from 1934 to 1968.
> If your family got a GI Bill loan or an FHA mortgage in that window, they were a direct
> beneficiary of the same program that excluded Black veterans. That's living memory, not
> ancient history."

**2. "How would you even decide who qualifies?"**
> "The same way we decided for internment: a documented harm class. The redlining maps
> exist. They're organized by address. The federal government already did the paperwork —
> that's the point."

**3. "It's too expensive."**
> "We found seven hundred billion over a single weekend in 2008 and two trillion in 2020.
> Cost is a priority statement, not a constraint."

**4. "It would just cause resentment."**
> "Resentment is the product the system sells. Getting poor people to resent each other
> instead of looking up is the entire function. You're describing the mechanism, not an
> objection to it."

**5. "Money won't fix cultural problems."**
> "The gap isn't cultural, it's compounding. A house bought in 1950 in a green-lined
> neighborhood is a million dollars of inherited equity now. The same house denied in a
> red-lined one is zero. That's arithmetic on an asset, not a value system."

**6. "We already spent trillions on welfare."**
> "Welfare is income support. This is an asset claim. Handing someone rent money for forty
> years does not replace the house they were legally barred from buying."

That distinction — **income transfer versus asset restitution** — is the most important
one on this card. Most opponents cannot tell them apart, and the entire "we already tried
that" objection collapses once you separate them.

---

## Close

> "Every dollar of that gap traces to a statute. Statutes were written by people, on a
> date, with a signature. They can be unwritten the same way."

---

## If you only have 10 seconds

> "Reagan signed reparations for internment in 1988. Redlining was federal policy with maps
> until 1968. Same logic, same paperwork — tell me which part is different."
