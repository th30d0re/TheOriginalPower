# ▶ OPENERS — pick ONE

🚨 **If the format lets you choose, GO SECOND.** Scores across four rounds: 84 going
second, 60/70/65 going first. Rebuttal scored 18/20 reactive and 8–13 leading. Your
dismantling instinct is the strongest thing you have and leading doesn't let you use it.

🚨 **Target 25 seconds, not 30.** You have run past the clock in all four closings and lost
the final line every time.

### A. THE RISK TRAP ⭐ *(default — forces overextension, contains the bridge)*
> Let me concede something up front. Risk deserves reward. If you put your own money on the
> line and it pays off, you earned it — I'm not disputing that for a second.
>
> So the only question that matters is who actually carried the risk. The public funded
> DARPA. The public paid for the bailouts. Whoever carries the risk owns the upside —
> that's how equity works everywhere else.
>
> My opponent is about to tell you the billionaires carried it. Ask them to name one who
> lost everything.

**Why this is the strongest opening available to you:**
- **The concession disarms.** Agreeing that risk deserves reward makes you the reasonable
  party and costs you nothing, because you're about to show they don't meet it.
- **It forces the factual vector.** Property and flight are *value* claims you can only
  dispute. Risk is a *factual* claim you can disprove. Bait them onto the one with receipts.
- **It contains the bridge.** "Whoever carries the risk owns the upside" is your positive
  case, so you can't be dinged for diagnosis-without-remedy.
- **The prediction springs it visibly.** Naming their next move means the judge watches
  them walk in.
- **The demand is unmeetable.** No billionaire lost everything. If they reach for
  Musk-in-2008, Tesla took a $465M federal loan — the example proves your point.

### B. THE INVOICE *(clean positive case, no trap)*
> Nobody builds a billion dollars alone. The public funded the research — DARPA built the
> internet, GPS is military. Then when it collapsed in 2008, the public paid for the
> bailout. Public money at the front, public money at the back, and no equity either time.
>
> A cap is the equity payment. The public carried the risk, so the public owns the upside
> past a threshold. That isn't confiscation. It's an invoice.

Judges have credited the invoice with winning the clash twice. Use it when you want the
case stated plainly rather than baited.

### B. THE BAIT *(when you want them on defense early)*
> Nobody builds a billion dollars alone. The internet came out of DARPA. Public roads move
> the trucks. Public schools trained the workforce. A cap isn't punishment, it's an
> accounting correction.
>
> And my opponent is about to tell you they earned it by taking enormous personal risk.
> Hold that thought.

Then when they take it → **tollbooth / bailouts / our risk their reward.** Naming their move
first means they either walk in or abandon their best argument. Both are yours.

### C. THE RATE *(when you want it on economics)*
> The pie grows at about two percent a year. The claim on it grows at six. When the claim
> grows three times faster than the thing being claimed, the remainder shrinks — every year,
> automatically. A billionaire isn't someone who won. It's proof the absorption rate beat
> the growth rate.

### Cold-open attention grab (10s, bolt onto any of the above)

## 🌉 THE BRIDGE — say this once per turn

Judges have flagged the same gap twice: you prove the ledger is unfair, then never
connect it to *why a cap specifically.* Diagnosis without remedy loses persuasiveness
and logical-consistency points even in clashes you win.

**One line closes it. Pick one and land it every turn:**

- **"A cap is the equity payment."** The public carried the risk, so the public owns the
  upside past a threshold. Not confiscation — the invoice.
- **"Everything else was tried and the share kept climbing."** Antitrust, disclosure,
  higher brackets. The number went up anyway. A cap is the only instrument that binds.
- **"You don't fix a leak by measuring it."** We've measured this for forty years.

Without the bridge, your whole case is a complaint. With it, the cap is the conclusion.

## THEY SAY → YOU SAY

### "Ceiling on ambition / human potential / stifles dreams" 🚨 **NEVER LEAVE THIS UNANSWERED**
Judges have flagged this as an *unaddressed counter-impact* — it is a values claim and you
keep answering it with facts. Facts don't beat feeling. Answer it on its own ground:

- **Ambition isn't accumulation** — you can still build the rocket. You just can't own the
  launchpad, the airspace, and the senator
- **Ambition is the thing we're short of capital for** — millions of people in this country
  have more ambition than money. A cap doesn't lower a ceiling, it raises a floor
- **One person's unlimited accumulation is ten thousand people's ceiling**

### "Destroys innovation / incentive"
- **Tollbooth** — public money funds the research, public money pays the bailout, they take the middle
- **DARPA** — internet, GPS, touchscreen, voice recognition. All public research, privatized at the register
- **$999 million** — name the founder who stopped because the next one wasn't worth it
- **91%** — top *income* bracket in the fifties. Interstate, moon landing, transistor
- ⚠ Never say "there was a cap in the 1950s." There wasn't. Say *income bracket*

### "They take the risk"
- **Our risk, their reward** — privatized profit, socialized loss
- **Bailouts** — public rescued the banks in 2008
- **"But it was repaid"** → *the guarantee is the subsidy, not the loan.* Being too big to fail lowers your borrowing cost every day, on insurance nobody voted for
- **Too big to exist** — if taxpayers are *forced* to rescue you, the free market already died
- **Occupancy quotas** — private prison contracts made the state pay for empty beds

### "Capital flight / they'll leave"
- **Empty suitcase** — take what with them? Not the consumer base, roads, courts, workforce
- **Citizenship, not residence** — leaving means renouncing. Exit tax already exists
- **Hostage** — their best argument is a threat. If one person leaving crashes a country, that's the proof

### "Property rights / theft"
- **Proportional burden** *(you improvised this — it works)* — someone at $100k paying 30%
  feels it. A billionaire paying the same rate feels nothing. Same percentage, different
  burden. ⚠ Say "heavier burden," not "unconstitutional" — that word is wrong and gets marked
- **Slavery** — people were property here. We removed it without compensating owners. Property is statutory
- **All taxation is taking** — 91, 70, 37. By that definition they're against the tax code
- **Estate tax** — 40% at death, nobody calls it tyranny

### "Arbitrary limit"
- **What's your number?** — trillion? ten? If one person held 90% of everything, would you object?
- **Every threshold is arbitrary** — bracket lines, estate exemption, drinking age
- **Caps already exist** — controlling stake in a bank needs Fed approval. FCC caps media ownership. Airline foreign ownership capped by statute

### "Jobs / economic dynamism"
- **Subsidized payroll** — largest employers, workforce on public assistance. We pay the wages and call it job creation
- **Monopoly** — four airlines, ~80% of seats. That's a condition, not a market

### "Everyone's equal under the law"
- **Parking** — if the fine doesn't hinder you, you bought a permit
- **Finland** — day-fines since 1921
- **Meta** — record $5B fine, stock went *up*

### "Democracy / just vote"
- **Where they diverge, the affluent win** (hedged Gilens & Page — do not overstate)
- **Anarchy at the top, law at the bottom**
