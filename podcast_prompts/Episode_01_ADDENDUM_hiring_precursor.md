### Cold Open — The Detector at the Mid-Level Filter

Open with a concrete audit before introducing Chapter 1's formal vocabulary. Describe the
author's unpublished 2024 exploratory hiring study as the applied detector promised by the
earlier *From Bias to Bytes* / *The Calculus of Discrimination* proposal. The study generated
synthetic résumés across four levels of seniority and encoded racial identity through explicit
markers, inferred markers such as names and educational institutions, and mixed markers. A
language model then ranked candidates under a selection prompt containing no racial
instruction.

Direct the hosts to make the distribution audible. Entry-level and executive selections
approached parity. At the mid-career rung, White-marked candidates received about two of every
three selections: 68.33 percent, compared with 31.67 percent for Black-marked candidates. The
aggregate marker-category test did not reach statistical significance. The career-level split
made the mid-level skew legible. Explain why the split matters methodologically: an aggregate
can report little while a partitioned view identifies the location where selection changes.

State the confidence boundary in the same breath as the result. This was one unpublished
course-project study using synthetic résumés. Qualifications were not held constant across all
marker conditions, and the result requires replication with controlled and real-world data.
Present it as an exploratory signal and an example of the detector operating. Use that signal
to enter the Preface's research sequence, then load Chapter 1's account of racism as a system
whose outputs can persist without an explicit racial command in the immediate interface.

### Supported By — Peer-Reviewed Context

- **Weisshaar et al.** locate an anti-Black hiring penalty at entry and describe the pattern as
  **“diversity commodification.”** Use this as peer-reviewed evidence that hiring penalties can
  vary by career stage.
- **Law & Tan** find rising board diversity alongside declining Black workforce representation
  and describe the pattern as **“diversity tokenism.”** Use this as peer-reviewed evidence that
  an improved representation count at one organizational level can coexist with deterioration
  elsewhere.
- Name the framework's **reform-absorption dynamic** as a forward reference. The **Concession

### Bridge — Early Draft → Framework (Updated)

Frame the precursor as a four-beat research sequence. *From Bias to Bytes* / *The Calculus of
Discrimination* identified a recurring structure and proposed set theory, discrete mathematics,
and machine learning as the tools for an automatic bias detector. *The Calculus of Injustice*
registered an empirical trace in fatal-police-shooting data while specifying the limits of
incident-only features. *Exploring Bias and Fairness in Language Models Applied to Hiring*
built the proposed detector and applied it to synthetic hiring decisions, where disaggregation
located an exploratory mid-career selection skew that the aggregate test did not register.
*The Original Power* derives the mechanism: the early In-group / Out-group partition develops
into a formal architecture with defined tiers, variables, extraction dynamics, and falsifiable
claims. Give the listener the method in four verbs: **identify → detect → build → derive**.
Then move directly into Chapter 1's causal reversal and its distinction between prejudice and
the system that organizes prejudice into durable output.
