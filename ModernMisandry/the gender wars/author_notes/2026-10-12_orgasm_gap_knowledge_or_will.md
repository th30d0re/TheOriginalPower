# Open question: do men lack the knowledge or the will? (the orgasm gap)

Provenance tags: `[operator: 2026-10-12]` Emmanuel, in chat; `[claude-inferred, verify]` derived here, not yet checked against sources.
Origin: aside during the reciprocity reel (`Architecting_the_operation/video/reel_reciprocity/`).

## The question `[operator: 2026-10-12]`

> Do men not know how to make women orgasm, or do they not care to know, or are they unwilling? My whole argument is from the male perspective: if you are self-aware enough to see what's going on, why would you do that instead of just getting gratified, when you are already giving non-sexual compensation? Unless you're wired that way and do it regardless, like me.

This splits the orgasm gap into three candidate mechanisms that the reel's argument treats as one.

| Mechanism | What it predicts | How to tell it apart |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge**: men don't know what works | Gap shrinks with partner-specific experience and communication; men overestimate partner orgasm | Look for men's estimates of partner orgasm vs. women's reports; effects of practices (oral, manual, clitoral stimulation) |
| **Will / entitlement**: men know but don't prioritize her pleasure | Gap tracks context (hookup vs. relationship) more than skill; men report caring less about a casual partner's pleasure | Look at stated entitlement to pleasure by context |
| **Transaction**: the reel's thesis; non-sexual payment makes self-centering rational | Gap widens as non-sexual compensation before sex grows, holding commitment constant | No direct test found yet. This is the novel claim and needs a design |

## Field observations `[operator: 2026-10-12]`

- **Feedback is the calibration signal.** He tells partners outright to tell him what works: "If you don't tell me, I cannot calibrate myself." He can often detect faking, and when he can't, communication falls on her. This bears on the knowledge channel: faking corrupts the man's training data.
- **Oral is easier than penetration for him.** Penetration depends on the person and the angle. Harder, not impossible. Compare with Frederick et al. (2018).
- **The porn script runs one way.** Mainstream porn routinely shows fellatio as foreplay and rarely shows cunnilingus. The default sequence is fellatio, then intercourse. If porn is a primary sex education, this is a knowledge channel and a norm channel at once. Worth a content-analysis source.
- **He reverses the script on purpose.** He goes down first to signal that he's serious about reciprocity.
- **Reciprocating first doesn't guarantee reciprocity back.** Sexually selfish partners exist among men and women alike. The practical lesson is selection: find a partner who wants to reciprocate and is willing to.

## Leads to check `[claude-inferred, verify]`

- **Armstrong, England & Fogarty (2012)**, already cited in the reel. Their interviews report that both men and women questioned women's entitlement to pleasure in hookups but endorsed it in relationships. That points to **will/entitlement varying by context**, not only knowledge.
- **Faked orgasm literature** (e.g. Muehlenhard & Shippee 2010, *Journal of Sex Research*). If women fake orgasm often, men get false feedback, a **knowledge** channel that women's behavior feeds.
- **Frederick et al. (2018), *Archives of Sexual Behavior*** (large US sample). Practices that correlate with women's orgasm (oral sex, communication, variety) point to a **knowledge/practice** channel.
- **Men's estimates vs. women's reports** of partner orgasm. A consistent overestimate would mean men often don't know there's a gap at all.
- **Muise et al., sexual communal strength.** "Wired that way" has a measured construct: men high in sexual communal strength are motivated by partner pleasure. That's the self-description at the end of the question.

## Why it matters for the book

The reel argues that a self-aware man in a transactional setup has no incentive to center her pleasure. If the gap is mostly **knowledge**, the fix is information and the transaction argument is secondary. If it's **will under transaction**, the reel's loop is the mechanism. If it's **communal disposition**, the gap is partly a trait difference between men, and reciprocity sorts partners. These are probably all present. The research task is to find their relative weights and whether any study separates them.

## The thesis is about the incentive, not about every man `[operator: 2026-10-12]`

His answer to the counterpoints (Klein 2024's null context effect for men; women in sex work reporting pleasure with paying clients):

- The claim describes what the transactional structure **rewards**. It makes no estimate of how many people follow the incentive. Communally wired people give pleasure regardless of payment, because giving is itself their pleasure. "Even in transactional sex I still do it." Klein's null and the sex-work counterexamples are consistent with a communal minority, so they qualify the size of the effect and leave the direction standing.
- **The communal giver still loses under transaction.** In casual or transactional sex he still gives, but "I feel like I'm catered to less than when it's reciprocal." The structure extracts from the giver: he supplies the pleasure and receives less of it. This predicts the loss falls on whoever is communal, man or woman. Testable: communal strength × transactional framing → own satisfaction.
- **Commitment and reciprocity come apart.** In one past relationship: a committed relationship where sex was transactional. She set conditions, he worked for it, he centered her pleasure, she rarely reciprocated, and it became a chore. "My reward is to see your lack of reciprocation." This matches PHAM §1.13's point that reciprocity, not commitment, is the variable (`Pseudo_Hybrid_Asymmetric_Monogamy.tex:1393`). It's also why friends-with-benefits matters as a comparison: reciprocity without the commitment.

## Reciprocity first, as a selection strategy `[operator: 2026-10-12]`

His reading of the OSF result (wanting romance doesn't lower sexual satisfaction): wanting more from a reciprocal FWB is the rational response, not a sign it's failing. Sexual reciprocity that holds up on its own is the most solid foundation to build a relationship on. It removes the "waiting period" of committing first and hoping reciprocity arrives later. In his own FWB he wanted more; she wasn't in a place for a relationship; he moved on and found a partner who wanted a relationship and reciprocated sexually from day one.

Data check (osf.io/pazmy, exploratory): men show the same pattern. Men who want the FWB to become romance rate satisfaction 5.33 (n = 9), men who want it unchanged 5.27 (n = 35). Of the people who wanted romance at Wave 1, 7 of 47 were in a romantic relationship with that partner at follow-up (5 women, 2 men). Most ended (18) or reverted to friends (13). Wanting more is common, and converting is uncommon.

Implication: "sex first as a test of reciprocity" is a defensible selection strategy that the PHAM framing (commit first, sex as reward) inverts. Candidate Part 3 or a Gender Wars section.

## Simultaneous commitment: both vulnerabilities at once `[operator: 2026-10-12]`

Refinement of "reciprocity first". Either order leaves one partner exposed:

- **Sex first exposes her.** A woman who wants romance and gives sex first risks ending up in a situationship or FWB she didn't want. After that happens, many women are reluctant to be that vulnerable again, especially with someone they really like. (His hypothesis, unverified.) The OSF data fits the risk: most people who wanted romance from an FWB didn't get it (7 of 47).
- **Commitment first exposes him.** A man who invests while sex is withheld is gambling: he doesn't know whether they're sexually compatible, sex may never come, and he may be valued for money and attention. That's the PHAM entry condition.

**The resolution is to commit both at the same time.** Each partner takes the vulnerable step together, so neither carries the risk alone, the way an escrow settles both sides of a trade at once. His example: a partner said she wanted reciprocity too, but not outside a relationship, so they started the relationship and the sex together, and it was reciprocal from day one.

**His own history as data (n = 1, retrospective, partners anonymized here):** across about eight relationships he reports alternating between partners who reciprocated from the start and partners who made him wait or withheld (the pseudo-hybrid pattern). Open question for him: in his own history, does "made me wait" predict "didn't reciprocate later", or are the two independent? If they track each other, waiting may itself be a signal; if not, the issue is reciprocity, not timing.

Ties to Part 2's line "Reciprocity runs at the same time, or it runs out."
