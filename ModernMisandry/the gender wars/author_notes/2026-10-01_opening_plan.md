## The decision `[operator: 2026-10-01]`

The Gender Wars opens with *The Original Power*: how that book was written and rewritten, how the
etymology of *power* closed the loop, and why a book on the gendered axis follows from it. Emmanuel's
account, in his words (dictation, lightly trimmed):

> The book started as one thing, got rewritten many times, I realized I needed a better perspective, the
> Theodore Transform, and I went back to Redefining Racism and rewrote it into the mathematics of
> oppression and then into The Original Power. When I go over the etymology of the original power, why I
> arrived on that title, it came full circle, because the original system of extraction that I can see from
> writing The Original Power is on the gendered axis. This book is dedicated to that axis. So we trace it
> from its origins, do the research, and see what we uncover. This is what The Original Power points to as
> the source code. It is still running, and we can analyze how it is running right now. But how did it
> start? If we really want to deconstruct patriarchy, we have to understand what patriarchy actually is.

The title note carries its own limit. It says the etymology "is offered as an observation and carries no
argumentative weight," that the claims are "established from the historical and quantitative record," and
that the coincidence was "noticed after the argument was complete" `[book: TOP:175]`. The Gender Wars

carries no part of the argument.

## What the opening has to add

1. **The Transform as the book's method.** The appendix defines an invariance test that detects selective
   application of a claim across axes `[book: apx_theodore_transform.tex §Invariance Condition]`. The
   Gender Wars runs that test in the direction the formal program has not yet taken: from the younger axis
   (race, where the instruments read) to the older one (gender, which the book names the template).
   `[claude-inferred, verify]` This also connects the opening to the draft's central diagnosis, that each
   camp invokes whichever rule-set benefits it. That diagnosis is a failure of invariance. The appendix
   gives it a formal name.
2. **The research question the book cannot yet answer.** The manuscript establishes the European
   installation (coverture, the breeding apparatus, the legal timeline) and the counter-model. It stops
   at "the European subjugation of women is the original template." Emmanuel's question is wider: what
   patriarchy is, and how it began. The text does not answer how it began before Europe, so this needs
   research from outside the manuscript. `[operator]` Candidate lines of evidence to investigate, each
   to be tagged and audited before use: property and paternity-certainty accounts of origin, the archaeological
   and genetic record on sex-biased migration and mortality, early law codes, the comparative record of
   matrilineal and complementary societies. None of these has been read for this project yet.
3. **Reconcile the opening with the book's own scope clause.** The manuscript limits the template claim:
   "original" means "upstream in that compiled lineage relative to American juridical history", "not the
   first patriarchy on Earth or the sole locus of gendered domination", and it states that "patriarchy,
   status hierarchy, and sexual division of labor appear across human societies", West African baselines
   included `[book: TOP:4354, §Ideological Genealogy]`. The opening as dictated asks the wider question,
   how patriarchy started and what it is. That question sits outside the clause. Two consequences:
   - The Gender Wars can claim "upstream in the lineage the Atlantic kernel inherited" on the book's
     evidence. It cannot claim "first patriarchy" or "source code for everything else" without new evidence
     and an amendment to that clause. No passage I found supports origin-of-class or origin-of-other-axes.
   - The book holds two positions that pull against each other. §Proto-Partition calls the claim that
     domination of women is universal and natural "a foundational lie" `[book: TOP:4026]`, and the scope
     clause concedes that patriarchy-like hierarchy appears across societies `[book: TOP:4354]`. The
     reconciling reading is that the European *format* (coverture, legal erasure) is a regional
     innovation while some hierarchy is widespread. `[claude-inferred, verify]` The Gender Wars is where
     that distinction has to be made precise, because Part I depends on it.

## Proposed structure `[claude-inferred, verify]`

- **Part I, Origin.** What patriarchy is as a mechanism (defined in the book's terms: partition, wage,
  buffer), and what the evidence says about how it started. Ends where the manuscript's Proto-Partition
  begins.
- **Part II, Installation.** Coverture to the present as the compile history of one kernel
  (legal erasure, autonomy asymmetry, reproductive extraction, the amended-but-unreplaced contract),
  including the DC-to-AC derivation `[derivations/dc_to_ac_gendered_axis.md]`.
- **Part III, Running now.** The corpus as a live instrument: the reels, PHAM, the calibration argument,
  and the real-time intake from Muse. The corpus is the book's measurement apparatus. It supplies the
  `Q_gender` proxy the derivation calls for.

## Deferred to the next book `[operator: 2026-10-01]`

Emmanuel's hypothesis: religion may have preceded patriarchy as the first system of oppression, with
patriarchy built later on top of it. He places this in the next book, deeper than the Gender Wars.

What the manuscript says today that bears on it: religion appears as the theological layer of the
gendered lineage (Roman penetration hierarchy, then Augustine's compilation of shame doctrine)
`[book: §Ideological Genealogy TOP:4348-4395]`, which is downstream of patriarchy-like hierarchy in that
account. The hypothesis reverses the order of dependence, so it would amend the scope clause at TOP:4354
and the genealogy above it. `[claude-inferred, verify]` Keep it out of the Gender Wars' claims. If Part I
meets evidence on the order of religion and hierarchy, record it here with its source and leave the
inference for the next book.
