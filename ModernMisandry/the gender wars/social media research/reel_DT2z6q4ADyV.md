## Transcript

Let's debunk the patriarchy, at least the way it's described today. It's often framed as men building a system to benefit themselves with little or no consideration for women, when in reality, for most of human civilization, the average man couldn't vote, didn't own land, and had no real say in the laws that he lived under. What he did always have were obligations. Being a man meant being expected to fight and die, to do dangerous work, and to absorb the risk when things went wrong. Extremely limited agency paired with heavy responsibility, and we forget that formal authority was usually about duty and not control. The duty was to protect the women and children of society. And women weren't powerless spectators, they held real influence, running households, shaping values and religion, culture and tradition, raising the next generation. In other words, women may not have commanded the armies, but they shaped the people, values, and social structures that armies existed to protect, a role-based system built around survival. When we flatten that into an oppressor-victim story, we erase how constrained most men were and how much influence that most women actually had.
