So let's slow this down for a second and actually look at the logic here. The claim is this. Out of 10 men, one makes an inappropriate joke. Some laugh, some awkwardly chuckle, some stay silent, and nobody speaks up. And the conclusion? Most men are the same. Most men are protecting the system. Here's the problem. That's a moral expansion trick. You take the worst actor in the room, the guy who made the joke, and then you stretch responsibility to everyone present. But silence is not the same as endorsement. There are many reasons why people don't intervene in the moment. Social pressure, fear of escalation, not wanting public conflict, being caught off guard, not knowing what to say. You can't criticize passivity, sure. But equating passivity with offensive behavior is a logical leap. Now let's test this logic properly. Imagine, out of 10 men, one actually speaks up and shuts it down. What happens then? Do all men suddenly become good? Of course not. So the framework collapses the moment the numbers change. Let's go deeper. Men also created the very systems that restrain bad men. Police, law enforcement, courts. Why do this exist? Because without them, society turns into chaos, rule of the jungle. Those are not identical categories. The moment you collapse them into one, you remove new ones. And when you say most men are the same, you are not encouraging accountability.
