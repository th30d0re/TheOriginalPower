## Transcript

If I'm being bullied and I say I hate my bully, is that the same thing as my bully saying he hates me? Think for a second, lock in your answer. Obviously, the answer is no. Whereas I hate my bully for what he's doing to me, my bully hates me for what I am. Now it's not a nuance, Max, and recognize the difference between a black person saying they hate white people and a white person saying they hate black people. Same thing as a woman saying they hate men as opposed to a man saying they hate women. So if you are trying to use this bully analogy to explain why a black person hating white people is different from a white person hating black people or why a woman hating men is different from a man hating women, there is a problem. your analogy isn't actually equivalent. To make it equivalent, imagine you are a woman and the man bullies you specifically because you are a woman. Now, hating that particular man because of what he did to you is one thing. But then imagine you take that experience and say, because this man treated me this way, I now hate men. That is the actual equivalent. Or a black person is mistreated by a white person specifically because they are black. And then concludes, I hate white people. Now you are no longer just hating your bully for what he did. You are taking the actions of one person or even a number of people and applying that hatred to millions of completely unrelated people because they share the same sex or skin color. And you know what we normally call that? Depending on what characteristic you are generalizing by, racism, sexism, or some other form of prejudice. Now, does context matter? Of course it does. There can be differences in history, power, discrimination, and in why someone developed that hatred in the first place. That might help explain why somebody feels that way. But explaining prejudice is not the same thing as making prejudice reasonable. Because once your principle becomes, people from this group have treated people like me badly, therefore it is understandable for me to hate that group, that principle can be used by basically anyone. A man could point to bad experiences with women. A Latino person could point to bad experiences with Asian people. A local person could point to bad experiences with immigrant people. And suddenly, everyone has their own personal justification for hating an entire group. That is exactly why we normally judge individuals for what they actually do, rather than treating people as guilty because they share somebody else's race or sex. So yes, hating your bully because they bullied you is very different from your bully hating you because of what you are. But hating millions of strangers because they happen to look like your bully, that is not the same argument anymore. And ironically, now you are the one judging people for what they are rather than for what they did.
