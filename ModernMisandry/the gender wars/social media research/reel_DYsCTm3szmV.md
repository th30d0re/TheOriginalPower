Now Professor Neil's claim number 4, 8 in 10 of us lied to convince a woman to sleep with us, most likely comes from this strategy in the survey. Told her whatever she wanted to hear. 78.1% of men reported using this at least once. So roughly 8 in 10. Before I go into that strategy though, let me address a few elephants in the room. Professor Neil takes this 78.1% and applies it to us, to the general population of all men. But there are several problems with that that I didn't talk enough about in the previous video. First, despite the sample here being larger than in the previous two studies, it is still quite restricted. 18 to 34 years old, mostly white Caucasian or black African origin, this is still far from being representative of all men. Second, the sample was pre-selected. Only men who had sex with a woman in the past two years were included. If you're a virgin or simply had a dry spell for two years, you're not in this study. Think about it this way. A study of gym goers cannot tell you about the exercise habits of all people. A study of car owners cannot tell you about the transportation habits of all people. And a study of sexually active men aged 18 to 34 cannot tell you about all men. By definition, a pre-selected sample cannot represent the group it excluded. Third, the study itself shows that not every attempt ended in sex. For the strategy, told her whatever she wanted to hear, 23.9% of cases resulted in no intercourse at all. So even on its own terms, you cannot use the 8 and 10 figure for a phrase like convince a woman to sleep with us. Because almost a quarter of the time, no sleeping happened. Now let's go to the strategy itself. Told her whatever she wanted to hear. The phrase from the survey and Lie to convince a woman to sleep with us, the claim made by Professor Neal, are not the same thing. Not even close. And a professor of English should really know that. Let me give you an example. A woman asks you if she looks pretty tonight. You say yes. That is telling her what she wants to hear. And if you find her attractive, which is the entire reason you are flirting with her, it is also the truth. Is that a lie? No. Is that you manipulating her into bed? Not necessarily. That is just a normal human interaction. Now, can told her whatever she wanted to hear also include actual lying and manipulation? Like promising things you have no intention of fulfilling, or making up a whole personality just to get her home? Yes, of course it can. And that kind of behavior could be genuinely problematic.
