Is gender pay gap real? Or is it a propaganda pushed by media and feminists? In my last video, I tried to explain the gender pay gap with data, but seems like it didn't work out for some people. Imagine one day you hear that tech activists start demonstrations. They claim that Apple phones are 50% more expensive than Samsung phones, and that this is discrimination, sexism, and shouldn't be allowed. Media picks up the phone price gap and suddenly everywhere you hear that greedy Apple is abusing the system. You get curious, so you look into it. You check the data and realize how this 50% gap was calculated. They simply took the median price of all iPhones and compare it to the median price of all Samsung phones. And yes, based on that, iPhones do look 50% more expensive. Case closed, right? But then you think, wait a minute. Samsung sells a wide range of devices, including many cheaper models, while most iPhones are positioned as premium products. So comparing all phones to all phones is, at the very least, misleading. If you want to compare, sorry for the pun, Apples to Apples, you should compare iPhones to Samsung's premium Galaxy line. And once you do that, the gap drops to about 20%. Then you refine further. Compare phones by release year. Now the gap is 15%. Match the storage capacity, 128GB to 128GB, 256 to 256. Now the gap is 10%. At this point, you exclaimed 40 out of 50% for the phone price gap. So you dig deeper. You look at distribution, discounts, bundles, production costs, marketing, brand positioning. For example, Samsung devices are often sold through distributors with aggressive discounts, while iPhones are frequently bundled with career plans. Once you start accounting for these factors, the gap shrinks even further, to about 5%. And now the question becomes, is that remaining 5% really taxism and discrimination, or is it simply due to factors you haven't fully captured yet? So what can we say overall? It is not factually wrong to say that, in this simplified example, iPhones are 50% more expensive than Samsung phones. But it is clearly misleading, because it compares broad averages across very different categories. Once you start comparing, like for like, the gap drops significantly. And even the remaining difference may have nothing to do with discrimination, but rather with factors like cost, strategy, or market conditions. So claiming that this phone price gap exists mainly because of greed or taxism simply doesn't hold up. And the same logic applies when we talk about the gender pay gap.
