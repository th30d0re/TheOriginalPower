# Research brief: friends-with-benefits relationships as a test of sexual reciprocity

You are doing a literature review. Use web search and fetch. Read-only on the web. Write only the files named under Output.

## Context

A companion review (`../orgasm_gap_mechanisms/findings.md`) found that no study directly tests this hypothesis: when sex is gated behind non-sexual "payment" (dates, gifts, chores), men center their own pleasure and women's orgasm rate drops.

The author proposes friends-with-benefits (FWB) relationships as a natural comparison. FWB pairs have ongoing sex with a known partner, usually without the dating, gifts and provisioning of romance. Sometimes they're exclusive and sometimes not, and some last for years. He holds that FWB:
- is distinct from hookup culture (one partner, repeated, with a friendship base);
- requires sexual reciprocity to form and to last, because sex is close to the whole exchange;
- would therefore show women's orgasm and satisfaction closer to committed relationships than to hookups, even though the non-sexual "payment" is largely absent.

Test these claims. Report what supports them and what cuts against them with equal care.

## Questions

- **Q1. Definitions and prevalence.** How does the literature define FWB, and how does it distinguish FWB from hookups, casual dating and "situationships"? Prevalence by age and gender. Recognized FWB subtypes (e.g. "true friends", "network opportunism", "transition in"/"transition out").
- **Q2. Orgasm and sexual satisfaction.** Women's and men's orgasm rates or sexual satisfaction in FWB compared with (a) one-time hookups and (b) committed or romantic relationships. Exact figures, samples and DOIs. This is the central question.
- **Q3. Reciprocity and communication.** Evidence on sexual communication, negotiation of rules, mutual attention to pleasure, and equity or reciprocity within FWB. Does sexual reciprocity predict whether an FWB starts, how satisfied the partners are, or how long it lasts?
- **Q4. Non-sexual exchange.** How much dating, gift-giving, financial provision or domestic labor happens in FWB compared with romantic relationships? Any measure of "investment" or Rusbult-style investment model applied to FWB.
- **Q5. Duration, stability and transitions.** How long FWB relationships last. Rates of transition to romance, back to friendship only, or ending the friendship. What predicts each outcome.
- **Q6. Exclusivity.** Prevalence of exclusive (monogamous) FWB arrangements and how they compare with non-exclusive FWB on satisfaction, sexual health and duration.
- **Q7. Gender differences.** Differences in motives, emotional outcomes, desire for romance, and satisfaction between men and women in FWB. Start from Lehmiller, VanderDrift & Kelly (2011) and related work.
- **Q8. Stigma and framing.** How FWB is stigmatized or framed in research and public discourse, and any evidence that stigma affects outcomes.

## Rules

- Primary sources first: the paper itself, its abstract, or its publisher page. Every claim needs a citation with a DOI or stable URL.
- Quote numbers exactly and give the sample (N, country, population, year). Mark any number from secondary coverage as `[secondary]`.
- Say "not found" plainly when you can't find something. Never present an inference as a finding.
- Report findings against the author's claims as fully as findings for them.
- Keep quotations under 15 words.

## Output

Write `findings.md` in this folder with:
1. One section per question (Q1–Q8): key findings as bullets with citations, then a one-line verdict on whether it supports, qualifies or contradicts the author's claims.
2. A summary table: question, best source, finding, verdict, strength (strong / moderate / weak / none).
3. **Gaps**: what no study has tested, especially a direct three-way comparison (hookup vs. FWB vs. romantic relationship) of women's orgasm that also measures non-sexual exchange. Sketch a design small enough for an independent researcher, and say whether it could be combined with the 2×2 vignette or diary designs in `../orgasm_gap_mechanisms/findings.md`.

Also write `sources.bib` with a BibTeX entry for every source cited.
