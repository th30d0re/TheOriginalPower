**New lead to read in full:** a 2026 meta-analysis of 38 studies, *A Meta-Analysis of Friends with Benefits Relationships*, *Archives of Sexual Behavior* (doi:10.1007/s10508-026-03426-0). Its abstract reports that FWB is associated with greater "sexual features", more sexual-health risk and less violence or aggression than other relationship types, with men reporting greater sexual features. Check whether its included studies report orgasm or satisfaction by gender.

## Olmstead, LeFebvre & Allen (2026) meta-analysis: what is checkable

**Full text is paywalled** (Springer, USD 39.95; not open access per Unpaywall). The abstract plus PsyPost coverage give:
- k = 38 studies. "Sexual features" pools sexual satisfaction, sexual acts, fantasies and motives; orgasm isn't named. No effect sizes are available without the full text.
- FWB scored higher on sexual features than all other relationship types. Versus committed partners: committed partners reported more deep kissing and affectionate touching, FWB more oral and anal sex.
- **Men reported greater sexual features in FWB than women.** This is the result that most needs the full text, because it could mean FWB serves men more.
- FWB has more sexual-health risk (similar to hookups), less violence and aggression than committed relationships, and weakly worse mental health.
- Limitations: mostly cross-sectional; mostly young, white, college samples; many single-item author-made measures; no dyadic data; no subtype analysis reported in the coverage.

## Birnie-Porter & Hunt (2015), included in the meta-analysis

*Canadian Journal of Human Sexuality* 24(2):174–183, doi:10.3138/cjhs.242-A5, via Vrangalova's summary in Psychology Today. N = 475, mean age about 25, five types: FWB, casual dating, exclusive dating, engaged, married. Five-item 1–5 semantic-differential sexual satisfaction scale.
- **FWB was rated about as sexually satisfying as exclusive dating and marriage.** Engaged was highest. All means were near the scale maximum (a ceiling effect limits discrimination).
- Results were nearly identical for men and women in the blogger's additional analysis (not in the paper).
- This conflicts with Lehmiller et al. (2014), where FWB was lower than romantic partners, though both groups were above the midpoint.
