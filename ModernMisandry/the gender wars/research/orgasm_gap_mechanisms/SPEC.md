# Research brief: what drives the heterosexual orgasm gap?

You are doing a literature review. Use web search and fetch. **Use swarm mode (AgentSwarm), with one worker per question Q1–Q8 below**, so no single worker runs long. Read-only on the web. Write only the files named under Output.

## Context

A short video argues that when a woman gates sex behind non-sexual "payment" (chores, dates, effort), the man treats sex as something already paid for, centers his own pleasure, and her orgasm rate drops. The author wants to know which of three mechanisms the evidence actually supports before publishing:

- **Knowledge**: men don't know what works (skill, information, false feedback).
- **Will / entitlement**: men know but don't prioritize her pleasure, and this varies by context.
- **Transaction**: non-sexual compensation before sex makes male self-centering more likely. This is the novel claim.

He also holds that a third group of men is motivated by their partner's pleasure as a trait ("wired that way").

## Questions (one swarm worker each)

- **Q1. Men's knowledge.** Do men overestimate how often their female partners orgasm? Studies comparing men's estimates with women's reports.
- **Q2. Faked orgasm.** Prevalence of women faking orgasm with male partners, the reasons given, and whether faking is linked to a lower real orgasm rate (the false-feedback channel).
- **Q3. Practices.** Which sexual practices predict women's orgasm with men: oral sex, manual stimulation, communication, duration, variety? Start from Frederick, John, Garcia & Lloyd (2018), *Archives of Sexual Behavior*, and any meta-analyses since.
- **Q4. Entitlement by context.** Evidence that men (and women) treat women's pleasure as less of an entitlement in hookups than in relationships. Start from Armstrong, England & Fogarty (2012), *American Sociological Review*, and later replications.
- **Q5. Transaction.** Any study that measures non-sexual investment or exchange before sex (dates paid for, gifts, "sexual economics" / sexual exchange theory, transactional framing) **and** links it to either partner's orgasm or to sexual self-centeredness. If none exists, say so explicitly, and list the closest designs and datasets that could test it.
- **Q6. Communal motivation.** Sexual communal strength (Muise, Impett and colleagues): is men's motivation to meet a partner's sexual needs associated with her orgasm or satisfaction? Effect sizes.
- **Q7. Porn scripts.** Content analyses of mainstream heterosexual pornography: how often fellatio vs. cunnilingus appears and in what order, and depictions of female orgasm. Any evidence that porn use predicts men's sexual scripts or partner orgasm.
- **Q8. Race and the gap.** Wetzel, Sanchez, Gesselman, Adams, Campbell & Garcia (2024), "Exploring the orgasm gap across racial/ethnic groups," *Journal of Sex Research*. Report the actual orgasm percentages by gender and race exactly as the paper states them. A social-media summary claimed Black men 75%, White men 83%, Hispanic men 82%, and women around 53–54%. Confirm or correct each number.

## Rules

- Primary sources first: the paper itself, its abstract, or its publisher page. Every claim needs a citation with a DOI or stable URL.
- Quote numbers exactly and give the sample (N, country, population, year). Mark any number taken from secondary coverage as `[secondary]`.
- Say "not found" plainly when you can't find something. Never fill a gap with an inference presented as a finding.
- Report findings that cut against the video's argument as fully as findings that support it.
- Keep quotations under 15 words.

## Output

Write `findings.md` in this folder with:

1. One section per question (Q1–Q8): the key findings as bullets, each with citation and DOI, then a one-line verdict on which mechanism (knowledge / will / transaction / communal) it supports, and how strongly.
2. A summary table: question, best source, finding, mechanism, strength (strong / moderate / weak / none).
3. **Gaps**: what no study has tested, especially Q5, and a sketch of a study design small enough for an independent researcher (survey items, sample, analysis).

Also write `sources.bib` with a BibTeX entry for every source cited.
